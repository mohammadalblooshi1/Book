# Global Marketing Plan: *The End Is the Beginning* (Desert Jinn Edition)

*Based on `../research/book-study.md`, Part 1 and Part 3, idea 12. Check every prize rule, date and fair deadline on the official website before acting. They change every year.*

---

## 1. The one-line pitch
> **Arabian folk horror.** In the deserts of 1920s Arabia, an outcast Bedouin boy carries a light that can drive back an ancient Ifrit, if he burns for it. Told by the jinn-touched saluki who has watched eleven children burn before him.

**Short tagline options:**
- *Never say your name to the wind.*
- *They say it truly happened.*
- *The end is the beginning.*

## 2. Genre and shelf
- **Primary:** horror / folk horror (adult)
- **Secondary:** literary fantasy, Middle Eastern fiction, mythic fiction
- **Keywords for stores and online listings:** Arabian folk horror, jinn, djinn, Ifrit, Bedouin, UAE, Oman, Empty Quarter, Rub' al Khali, lost city of Ubar, saluki, 1920s Arabia, Gulf folklore, Umm al-Duwais, desert horror

## 3. Comparable titles ("comps")
Agents and publishers expect 2–3 recent, successful books that yours sits beside:
| Comp | Why it fits |
|---|---|
| ***Frankenstein in Baghdad***, Ahmed Saadawi | Arab horror rooted in real place and folklore; IPAF winner; Man Booker International shortlist |
| ***The City of Brass***, S.A. Chakraborty | Global bestselling djinn fantasy; shows the size of the market for jinn stories |
| ***The Alchemist***, Paulo Coelho | Evergreen desert fable; circular "fate" structure |
| *(optional)* ***The Bleeding of the Stone***, Ibrahim al-Koni | A desert man-and-animal bond, written by an Arab literary master |

Avoid using Neil Gaiman as a comp because of the 2025 allegations (see the research file).

**Pitch line using comps:** *"Frankenstein in Baghdad meets The City of Brass, told like a Bedouin fireside tale."*

## 4. Hooks for press and readers
1. **Ubar is real.** The lost city was found in 1992 with NASA satellite images; part of it had fallen into a sinkhole. It is now part of UNESCO's *Land of Frankincense*. This works for articles, podcasts and social media.
2. **Real Gulf folklore.** Umm al-Duwais, Bu Darya, Hmarat al-Qayla, Abu Fanous and Umm al-Sibyan, the creatures Gulf grandmothers told of, are new to most world readers and nostalgic for Gulf readers.
3. **A dog narrator who already knows the ending.** It is easy to explain and makes people curious.
4. **"They say it truly happened."** The elder frame invites "is it true?" conversations.
5. **The hidden message.** Readers who find it share it (*"every kindness comes back"*).

## 5. Routes to publication
**A. Traditional publishing (English, global)**
1. Finish revisions; get a professional edit, or at least beta readers, including Gulf readers for cultural accuracy.
2. Write a query letter (pitch plus comps plus a short bio) and a 1–2 page synopsis.
3. Query literary agents who represent **horror, speculative fiction, or Middle Eastern / international fiction** (UK and US). Look for agents who list "own-voices" or "MENA" stories.
4. An agent then sells the book to publishers, including world and translation rights.

**B. Regional publishers (Gulf / Arab world)**
- Approach UAE and Gulf publishers who publish English and Arabic fiction. Ask at the book fairs below. Some run open submission calls.

**C. Self-publishing** (fastest, and you keep control)
- Amazon KDP (paperback, ebook), plus regional online bookshops.
- You must pay for or arrange: professional editing, a **professional cover** (the realistic anime-style art you wanted), formatting, and ISBNs.

## 6. Arabic edition and prizes
- Arab success often comes **through Arabic first, then translation** (Saadawi, al-Koni).
- **International Prize for Arabic Fiction (IPAF)** and the **Sheikh Zayed Book Award** focus on Arabic-language works; the Zayed award also has other categories. Read each prize's current rules for eligibility, publisher submission and dates.
- Plan: commission a **literary Arabic translation** (or write the Arabic edition with a translator), published by an Arab publisher who can submit it to prizes.
- The English edition can go to UK/US horror and fantasy awards. Check the rules and whether a publisher must submit it.

## 7. Events and places to launch
- **Sharjah International Book Fair** (usually November)
- **Abu Dhabi International Book Fair** (usually spring)
- **Emirates Airline Festival of Literature**, Dubai (usually early in the year)
- Other Gulf book fairs and literary festivals (Riyadh, Muscat, Kuwait, Doha)
- Horror and fantasy conventions in the UK and US (online panels count)

## 8. Building readers before launch
- **Social media:** short videos about each real folklore creature ("The jinn Gulf grandmothers warned you about"), the real Ubar discovery, and saluki history. Instagram and TikTok (#BookTok, #HorrorBooks, #ArabBooks).
- **A newsletter or simple website** with a free short story, perhaps Ghadban's tale (the Ifrit's origin) as a teaser.
- **Book clubs and schools:** the original teen edition could go to schools and young readers, and the horror editions to adults.
- **Podcasts** on folklore, horror and Middle Eastern history.

## 9. What the book still needs before submission
1. A full read-through by you, plus editing (AI-written drafts need human revision and a consistent voice).
2. Decide on one **lead edition** to pitch (the Desert Jinn Edition is the most original).
3. A professional cover.
4. Back matter: glossary, "Notes on the Folklore", the real history of Ubar, and acknowledgements.
5. Cultural accuracy check by Gulf readers or a folklorist.
6. Legal: since AI helped write the book, check the publisher's or platform's AI-disclosure rules (Amazon KDP, for example, asks about AI-generated content) and the copyright rules where you publish.
