# Jinn Additions: Spec, Round 2 (approved by the author)

Apply this to the Desert Jinn Edition **after** the folklore additions in `folklore-additions.md`. Same rules apply: weave it in with new scenes and passages, keep Ghareeb's voice, keep all names and fixed beats, keep the hidden message hidden. Each affected chapter grows by roughly **500–1,500 words**. The author asked for **more energy**: more action, faster scenes, real fights, real danger.

---

## 1. Four kinds of jinn, each with real powers
In the old belief, jinn are made of smokeless fire, and they live in every part of the world. In our book they come in four kinds. Each kind has its own powers and its own weaknesses. The Ifrit under Ubar commands them all through the Nameless.

| Kind | Who / what | Powers | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fire jinn** (*jinn al-nar*) | **The Ifrit** under Ubar, and lesser fire jinn: sparks with eyes, flames that walk | Smokeless fire that burns without wood; heat that kills; **possession**; taking the shape of smoke, a tall man, or a black dog; whispering into anger. They are the strongest, and they rule the other kinds | Recitation; iron; water read over by a holy man; the Light |
| **Wind jinn** (*jinn al-rih*) | **The zawba'a**, the sand-devils; voices carried on the wind | Storms and whirlwinds; carrying a spoken name to their masters (this is why you never say your name to the wind); hurling sand like knives; lifting men and camels; mimicking voices from far away | Calm air at dawn; enclosed stone places; iron charms; recitation |
| **Sea jinn** (*jinn al-bahr*) | **Bu Darya**; the drowned divers' voices; a **marid** of the deep (the strongest, oldest water jinn), which now sleeps under the dead sabkha | Calling in human voices; dragging people under water, or under salt; storms at sea; mirages over water; pearls that become bad luck | Fresh water; salt thrown at them (folk belief); light; noise and blades |
| **Land jinn** (*jinn al-ard*) | **The ghilan**, **Hmarat al-Qayla**, **Umm al-Duwais**, **Abu Fanous**, the jinn-jackals, and jinn of wells, ruins, graves and lone trees | Shape-shifting (always with donkey hooves); luring travellers off the path; moving sand and opening sinkholes; living in ruins and wells; stealing children at noon and dusk | Iron; recitation; staying on the path; daylight (for most); naming the Creator |

The four kinds are introduced naturally across the book (Mutawwa' Hilal can explain them in Ch 3 or Ch 4). The climax uses all four together: fire under Ubar, wind on the Night of Lamps, earth opening beneath the city, and sea-voices calling from the far sabkha.

## 2. The holy man: **Mutawwa' Hilal** ("the crescent moon": a small light in the dark)
- A wandering **mutawwa'**, a Bedouin religious teacher who teaches children to read, reads over the sick, and **casts out jinn by recitation**. He is about 60, thin and sun-blackened, with a white beard and a goat-hair cloak, and carries a stick and a leather satchel of amulets and ink. He is gentle, tired, brave, and dry-humoured.
- **What he can do:** read over the **malboos** (the possessed) and force lesser jinn out; read over water that burns jinn; write protective **hirz** amulets; recognise jinn by their signs. **What he cannot do:** defeat the Ifrit. "That one is not mine to send away. That one is waiting for *you*." Only the Light, freely given, can.
- **Showing recitation:** describe him reciting (his voice, his breath, the rhythm, the effect on the possessed person and on the jinn), but **never quote religious text**. Keep it respectful: he is the most decent man in the book.
- **Appearances:**
  - **Ch 3:** he arrives at Hilat al-Sarab after the grey, reads over a malboos villager (see section 4), sees Theyab's light, and is the one adult who does not call him monster. He gives Theyab his first **hirz**.
  - **Ch 4:** he explains the four kinds of jinn, tells Theyab what a **zahwi** is (section 5), and warns him that men will hunt him for it.
  - **Ch 16:** he reappears at the Al Bu Ghaf clan's camp below Jebel Hafeet, reading over the freed children.
  - **Ch 18:** in the battle of Ubar he reads over the malboos guards, saving many. He is wounded, and **dies** in Ch 18 or 19, still reciting. This is a return of kindness: the children he read over carry him out, or sit with him at the end.

## 3. Protection: what people wear and do
Use these realistically and often, especially at turning points:
- **Hirz:** a protective amulet. A small silver or leather case worn on a cord, holding a folded paper written by a mutawwa'. Theyab's hirz from Hilal should matter in Ch 13–15. It burns hot when jinn are near, and **the Nameless tear it from his neck** at the buried dhow.
- **Iron:** a khanjar or nail under the pillow, an iron ring, iron filings in the doorway. Jinn cannot cross cold iron easily.
- **The blue bead** against the evil eye.
- **Water read over** by the mutawwa', sprinkled on the possessed or drunk.
- **Naming the Creator** before pouring hot water, entering ruins, or lighting fire. Never quote, only describe it.
- **Salt** in folk practice: across thresholds, or thrown at sea-voices.
- **Staying on the path, coming in before dusk and at noon, never saying your name to the wind.**

## 4. **Malboos**: the possessed who attack
A **malboos** is a person a jinn has entered. They look normal until it rises: the eyes roll white or go black, the voice changes (deep, doubled, or someone else's), they show inhuman strength, speak in languages they never learned, and **attack the people they love**. It is frightening because the victim is still in there.
Required malboos scenes:
- **Ch 2 or 3:** a villager becomes malboos (suggestion: **Saqi**, the well-keeper and Nadim's father) and attacks his own family. Theyab and Ghareeb fight him off. In Ch 3, Mutawwa' Hilal reads the jinn out of him. This is a high-energy action scene.
- **Ch 7 or 11:** a member of the caravan, or a rider, turns malboos mid-journey.
- **Ch 14–15:** the Nameless deliberately make malboos fighters. Some of the **stolen children** are half-possessed: they fight Theyab, then become children again when he reaches them with the Light.
- **Ch 18:** on the Night of Lamps, when the lamps go out, **Sheikh Mutlaq's guards turn malboos** and attack the city. This is a big battle scene. Hilal reads over them, Theyab fights with the light and Hazza''s broken sword, and Ghareeb fights in his jinn form.
- **Saqr (the Seventh)** is partly malboos: a fire jinn rides him. When his mask cracks in Ch 15, the jinn's voice fights his own.

## 5. **Zahwi** (زهوي, also called *zuhri*): the marked child wanted for sacrifice
- In Gulf and Arabian folk belief, a **zahwi** is a person born with special marks: an **unbroken line straight across the palm**, a split in the eyebrow, a split in the tongue, or other signs. Sorcerers and jinn crave a zahwi's **blood**. It is said to open sealed treasures and buried places guarded by jinn, so treasure-hunters and sorcerers kidnap zahwi children to **sacrifice** them. Parents hid such children's marks.
- **Theyab is a zahwi.** Besides the feather mark, he has the **unbroken line across his palm** (seed it in Ch 1). The Nameless want him not only for the Light but as the **zahwi whose blood can open the Ifrit's last seal** under Ubar, freeing it completely.
- **Sharood hid baby Theyab partly because of this.** Sorcerers were hunting the zahwi child (Ch 16 reveal).
- **Saqr**, too, was taken because the Nameless thought he might be the zahwi. They were wrong, and they kept him anyway.
- In Ch 15 the Nameless try to cut Theyab's palm over the brass vessel. Ch 19 is the final attempt: Wazir Muhaisen wants the zahwi's blood **and** the Light. His theft destroys him.
- **The darker ending:** the newborn Nahidh in Ch 20 also has the unbroken line across his palm. Hessa sees it and is afraid, because she knows what *zahwi* means.

## 6. Half jinn, half human: **Jasir**
- **Jasir** (the caravan survivor and later guide) is **half jinn**. His mother was a pearl-diver's wife on the coast. His father was not a man but a **jinn of the sea**, and that is why the mirage called to him as a boy (folklore-additions, Ch 7).
- **Signs:** his eyes reflect light like an animal's at night; he can hear the dead and the jinn; he can walk in jinn places unharmed, and **Ghareeb accepts him at once** (one jinn-touched creature knowing another). Hilal's recitation makes him dizzy and ill, but not hurt.
- **His powers (limited):** he sees the hidden ways, which is why he can guide Theyab into Ubar through the dead city's secret channel (Ch 17). He can briefly speak to jinn and bargain with them. He is torn between both worlds, and the Ifrit offers to make him whole jinn.
- **Revealed gradually:** hinted in Ch 7, partly revealed in Ch 17, confirmed in Ch 18 when he uses it to save Zaid and the children, at a cost.
- Keep his loyalty a "return": he is loyal because Theyab gave him his last water in Ch 7.
