## Creatures
| Creature | Where | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Hmarat al-Qayla** ("Donkey of the Midday": half woman, half donkey, takes children who are out in the noon heat) | Ch 1–3 | The mothers of Hilat al-Sarab call their children in at noon with her name. No one calls Theyab, so he is the only child out in the qayla heat, scavenging. In Ch 2 or 3 something with hooves walks the empty lanes at noon, and only Theyab and Ghareeb see the tracks. Can echo once later (e.g. Ubar's noon streets, Ch 17). |
| **Abu Fanous** ("Father of the Lantern": a light at night that leads travellers away to die) | Ch 5 and/or Ch 7–8 | Lanterns that aren't lanterns bob over the dunes at night. Theyab nearly follows one. Ghareeb blocks him and herds him back. The ghilan use these lights. |
| **The ghilan, by the classic rules** | Ch 4–8 | They take the shapes of travellers or beautiful women. Their tell is **donkey-hoof tracks**, which Ghareeb smells and sees. They lure people off the path. |
| **Khattaf Rafai-style mirage** | Ch 7 | Jasir's backstory: as a boy on the coast he followed a shimmer of gold across the water, or across the sabkha, and lost his father's boat. It's why he never trusts what shines. It ties to Hilat al-Sarab, "the mirage". |
| **Bu Darya** ("Father of the Sea": a giant half-fish jinn who calls sailors and pearl divers in a human voice and drags them under) | Ch 14–15 | The buried dhow by the dead sabkha was a pearling boat that Bu Darya took, generations ago. The salt flat was once sea. At night, voices call from the salt in the voices of drowned divers. The Nameless chose the wreck because of it. In Ch 15, during the escape, something vast moves under the salt crust. |
| **Umm al-Duwais** (a beautiful jinn woman with an intoxicating perfume, who lures men at night and turns hideous, with blades for hands) | Ch 12–14 | **Nadim's betrayal begins with her.** On the night road, ragged and desperate, Nadim is met by a perfumed woman who knows his brother Shibl's name. She is a Nameless agent wearing the legend, or the legend itself; never confirm which. Her scent, of oud, musk and something rotten beneath, lingers on him when he meets Theyab in Ch 14, and Ghareeb smells it. Show her briefly from Ghareeb's point of view (Ch 13 or 14), and as Nadim's confession later. |
| **Umm al-Sibyan** ("Mother of Children": a demon who attacks newborns and mothers) | Ch 20 and epilogue | Hessa guards the newborn as real Gulf mothers did: an amulet, a blue bead, and an **iron blade under the pillow** (iron keeps jinn away). The grey's last attempt on the baby wears Umm al-Sibyan's shape at the tent door. Ghareeb is the one creature who will not be frightened away (this is the planned "return"). Keep it ambiguous and chilling. |

## The Ifrit's backstory: "The Fisherman and the Jinni" pattern
- Long before Ghadban, the Ifrit was **sealed beneath Ubar** in a brass vessel stamped with an ancient seal, set in the limestone cavern under the city's well. (The real Ubar at Shisr was a ring of towers around a well over a limestone sinkhole, and part of it collapsed into the cavern.)
- In its first century of prison it swore to **reward** whoever freed it with riches and long life. In the next it swore to grant three wishes. Then, as the centuries rotted its gratitude, it swore to **destroy** its rescuer and take everything they loved.
- **Ghadban**, the shepherd boy and first bearer, terrified of the sacrifice, broke the seal believing the old promise of reward would save him. The Ifrit had long since turned its vow, and it took him as its vessel. That is how the grey began.
- **Chapter 10** (Ghidd al-Zujaj): the rock carvings show it, and Ghareeb remembers it, told as a short tale within the tale in the style of the old *Nights* ("It was, or it was not…", in our own words).
- **Chapters 17–19**: pay it off. The broken brass vessel and the seal are in the cavern beneath Ubar's well. The Ifrit's voice repeats its rotted vows. On Tal Moreeb it offers Theyab the same first-century promise (riches, long life), and Ghareeb growls at the lie.
- This makes the hidden message part of the structure: even the monster is a vow that turned.
