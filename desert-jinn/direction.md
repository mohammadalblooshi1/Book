# Desert Jinn Edition: Direction

This is a rewrite of the **adult horror edition** (`../horror/chapters/`), moved into the **real desert of the UAE and Oman around the 1920s**, with real places, real Bedouin life and **jinn** in place of the invented monsters. It is still fiction, but it is told as a tale the old people swear really happened. The other editions stay unchanged.

Keep the same 20 chapters, the same plot beats, the same order and the same horror tone (creeping dread, monster horror, psychological horror, cult horror, and the darker ending). Only the world, names, creatures and details change, as listed below, plus the **hidden message**.

---

## 1. The hidden message (never said out loud)
**The good you do comes back to you as a reward.** Never state it as a moral. Weave it into the events so that every act of kindness returns later, often when it's needed most, and readers find the pattern themselves. Cruelty and cheating also come back. Required "returns":

| Kindness (early) | Returns (later) |
|---|---|
| Umm Hanan secretly leaves bread and dates for Theyab (Ch 1) | Ch 3: she hides bread in his hands as he's driven out, and it keeps him alive in the desert (Ch 4–5). Ch 20: when the curse lifts, her house is the first where colour returns, and her lamp is the one that doesn't go out. |
| Theyab saves Nadim from the emptied ones and heals Shibl (Ch 2–3) | Nadim betrays him (Ch 14), but his guilt drives him to raise the new bearer and protect him (Ch 20). The kindness returns slowly, and twisted, but it returns. |
| Theyab shares his last water with Jasir, the caravan survivor (Ch 7) | Jasir, now a guide, appears at the right moment in Ch 17 and leads Theyab through the secret way into Ubar. |
| Muhannad hid Suhail, the emptied stable boy (backstory) | Suhail, still half-emptied, wanders Ubar's catacombs and shows Theyab the hidden channel (Ch 17). |
| Theyab frees the eleven stolen children (Ch 15) | In Ch 18 the children (with the Al-Ghaf clan) send riders and warnings, and one child's whistle saves Zaid. |
| Theyab refuses to kill the Eldest of the Nameless (Ch 18) | Ch 19: the Eldest, unmasked and remembering, lets Zaid run instead of cutting him down. |
| Umm Shamma fed the princes as boys | She hides Theyab and feeds him (Ch 17). |
| Ghanim the trader cheats Theyab (Ch 1) | His cheating returns: he loses everything to the Nameless (glimpsed in Ch 14 or 18). |
| Wafi, the dog, stays loyal across every bearer | In Ch 20 he is the one creature the Ifrit's voice cannot frighten away from the cradle. |

The **elder's last line** (see section 5) may brush against the message without stating it.

## 2. Setting (real world)
- **Place:** the Rub' al Khali's eastern edge, **Liwa**, the Al Ain and Buraimi oases, **Jebel Hafeet** and the **Hajar mountains**, the Omani interior, and coastal *sabkha* salt flats. Use real geography, real plants (ghaf trees, date palms, calotropis, desert gourd), real animals (Arabian oryx, sand cats, Arabian wolves, red foxes, fennec-like foxes, scorpions, vipers, falcons, camels, salukis) and real weather (shamal winds, sandstorms, summer heat that kills, cold desert nights, mirages).
- **Time:** around **the 1920s**: camel caravans, falconry, pearl-diving on the coast, mud-brick forts and watchtowers, *falaj* water channels, Bedouin tents of goat hair, coffee with cardamom, dates, camel milk, *bisht* and *ghutra*, old rifles (matchlocks and Martinis) alongside khanjar daggers and swords.
- **Money:** **Maria Theresa silver thalers** and Indian rupees.
- **Sky:** real names: the sun, the moon (*badr* for the full moon), the stars. Bedouins navigate by **Suhail (Canopus)** and **the Pleiades (al-Thurayya)**. "The full moon" replaces "the full Ash".
- **Faith:** the **Creator** is respected but never shown, named or quoted. Characters may speak of "the One who made the sands". Jinn are real: some good, many wicked. Do not quote religious texts.
- **Sayings:** keep *"Never say your name to the wind,"* *"May the sand remember you"* and *"May the dunes forget you,"* recast as old Bedouin sayings.
- **The Night of Embers** becomes **the Night of Lamps** in Ubar.

**Wafi (the narrator).** He is a **real saluki**, the ancient Arabian hunting dog: lean and sand-coloured, with feathered ears and tail and old eyes. He **cannot speak to humans**. The narration is his inner voice, sarcastic and sad as before, addressed to the listener. Instead of a glowing tail, he **senses lies**: his hackles rise and he growls low. He is impossibly old. The Bedouins say a jinn lives in him, or that he is jinn-touched; it is never fully confirmed. That jinn half is his secret "monster" side (Ch 11: in the storm his shadow swells into something huge and wrong, and he moves with a strength no dog has). Realistic dog behaviour always comes first: he scents, tracks, guards, hunts and digs. In Ch 15 he doesn't lift the ship. He **digs under the rotten hull and brings down a buried section**, with that same frightening strength, opening a way out. One eye is amber and one is clouded silver (the jinn eye). Humans see only a strange old saluki. Only the bearers feel they understand him.

## 5. The supernatural (jinn instead of invented monsters)
| Old | Desert Jinn Edition |
|---|---|
| The Hollow / the grey | **the Ifrit's curse**, called **"the grey"** or **al-Ghasaq** (the darkness of night). It drains colour, sound, memory and names |
| Malek's Hollow | Ghadban made a pact with an ancient **Ifrit** sealed beneath Ubar and became its vessel. The Ifrit speaks through him |
| Husks | **the emptied**: people whose names a jinn has eaten. They repeat their last words |
| Sandwraiths | **ghouls (ghilan)** that lie under the sand and take the shape of travellers to lure them |
| Hollow Hounds | **jinn-jackals**, packs with burning eyes that track the Light |
| The Veilbeast | a colossal **zawba'a**, a jinn sand-devil, a whirlwind the height of a mountain with a mouth in it |
| The Unnamed | **the Nameless**, a cult that burns their names to serve the Ifrit |
| Grey blades | blades quenched in the Ifrit's ash that cut memory |
| The Light | the bearer's gift from the Creator: golden light that heals and drives back jinn, shows past and future, and must be released through a freely chosen sacrifice |

Riding beasts are **camels** only (no giant lizards). The sandstorm chase in Ch 11 is on racing camels. Scorpions are real scorpions.

## 6. The elder frame ("They say this truly happened")
- Ch 1 opens with a short italic **elder's prologue**, decades later, at a fire under the stars in Liwa. An old man (it is **old Nadim**, though he isn't named until the end) tells children: *"You'll say it's a tale. I tell you it happened. I was there."* An ancient saluki lies at his feet. He tells it, he says, "the way the dog would tell it, for the dog saw all of it." Then Wafi's narration begins.
- Ch 20 ends with Wafi's last line (*"Wake up, little one."*) and then a brief italic **elder's epilogue**: the old man by the fire, the children silent, the old saluki rising and going to the tent where a newborn sleeps. The darker ending stands.

## 7. Rules
- Same file format, chapter numbers and titles (adjust a title only if it names an old place, e.g. Ch 17 becomes **"The Walls of Ubar"**). Same length (3,000–4,000 words). Adult horror and the darker ending as in `../horror/horror-direction.md`.
- The fixed beats from `../continuity.md` all still apply under the new names (e.g. last words **"I'm coming, Muhannad."**, Ghadban: *"I remember my name."*, Saqr's first word **"Nahidh"**, final line **"Wake up, little one."**).
- Realism: real customs (hospitality, coffee, the three-day guest right, blood debts, tribal honour, the majlis), real survival (water, heat, navigation by stars and dune shapes), and realistic dog behaviour for Wafi.
- Respectful toward faith: no quotations, no named God, no mocking.
