# Chapter Twelve
## The Night of the Full Ash

> *Under the full Ash, every promise is weighed. And what is found wanting is taken.*
> — from the oath-sayings of the free towns, spoken over water at weddings and at graves

---

I didn't speak again before the full Ash.

I'll tell you about those days anyway. That's the strange thing about being the one who tells a story: you can be silent all the way through it and still have to describe every minute, the way a witness has to describe a room he only stood in.

I walked. That's mostly what I did. I limped along behind the two of them with my fur scorched brown down one side and my silver eye swollen shut. Something in the burn kept weeping a thin black fluid that smoked when it touched the sand. I didn't catch scorpions. I didn't eat much at all. At night I slept a little way off from the fire instead of against Zayn's side, and every night he looked over at me before he lay down, and every night I turned my face away.

He tried to say sorry. Sometimes out loud, sometimes just by bringing me water in his cupped hands and setting it beside me without a word. Once he left me the best piece of a roasted sand-hen and sat with his back to me so I could eat without being watched. I ate it after he'd fallen asleep. I want that on the record.

I wasn't angry with him. You need to understand that, because he didn't. I wasn't angry at all.

I was ashamed. He had seen what I was. He had heard the voices that live in me. And there is no word for how that feels, except that it's the opposite of being remembered.

---

Soren was different too, after the storm.

He still joked. He still complained about his feet, his bruised legs, and the general unfairness of sand. He still drew letters every evening, and Zayn learned fast now. He could read whole short lines. One night Soren wrote ZAYN AND SOREN, MONSTERS in big letters beside the fire and made him read it aloud, and Zayn did, and they both laughed, and for a moment the dark beyond the firelight felt a long way off.

Remember those letters. I'm sorry. I have to ask you to remember them.

But Soren had started touching the hilt of his sword. Not the grip, the way a fighter does. The pommel. He'd rest his thumb on the bronze cap without seeming to know it, the way Zayn touched his feather mark. And when he thought no one was looking, he'd stand on top of a dune and stare west, toward Veimore, the way you stare at a house where you know someone is waiting for you in the dark.

"We're changing direction," he announced on the fourth morning. "West. I need to go home."

Zayn stared at him. "You were exiled."

"I know. I was there."

"They'll kill you."

"They'll *try*." Soren's grin came and went too fast, like a lantern swinging past a window. "I need to see Idan. I need to make sure he's all right."

My tail glowed. That was true. Every word.

But Zayn had learned to watch the spaces between words, too. He looked at Soren a long moment. He knew there was something more. And because Zayn had never in his life asked anyone for something they didn't want to give, he didn't ask.

I knew what it was. I'd watched it go into the hilt. I didn't say anything either.

Three of us walking west together, and every one of us carrying something we didn't say. You could have heard the silence from a dune away.

---

Every night, the Ash got fuller.

Zayn stopped looking at it. Then he started looking at it all the time. He'd lie on his back by the fire with his hand on his chest, staring up at that white face as it swelled a little more each evening, and I knew what he was seeing. The dune. The fire. The sound of somebody shouting a name at the bottom of the world.

He'd started waking in the night and touching his arms, his hair, his face, quickly, the way you pat out sparks. Checking. Once I saw him hold his hand an inch above the fire and keep it there, his face blank, as if he were trying to learn the feeling in advance. Soren knocked his wrist away and said nothing, and Zayn said nothing, and they didn't speak of it.

He knew the Crown Dune was nowhere near. But every full Ash would bring it one turn closer, and this was the first one since he'd read the word *ash* at the end of eight lines on a glass wall.

On the twenty-ninth night, it rose full.

It came up out of the eastern dunes huge and white and close, so close it seemed to fill half the sky, exactly as it did in his vision. The whole desert went silver under it. Every grain of sand cast its own small shadow. You could see for miles, and there was nothing moving in any of them, not a beetle, not a breath of wind. The desert was holding still the way an animal holds still when something larger is passing overhead.

It was the brightest night Zayn had ever seen, and he sat by the fire and couldn't stop shaking.

One turn of the Ash. Thin to full, and the dark between. That's how long they'd had.

I knew it. I'd counted. Neither of them knew.

---

It started, as the worst things do, with someone trying to help.

"Come to Veimore with me," Soren said.

He'd been quiet a long time, sitting across the fire turning his ring round and round on his finger. Now he leaned forward, and his face in the firelight was serious in a way Zayn had hardly ever seen it.

"I mean it. Under the city there are the catacombs, and in the catacombs there's the Archive. Every book anyone in Veimore ever wrote. Every story. Every name. Thousands of years of them, Zayn, rooms and rooms, all the way down into the dark. If there's a third way written anywhere in this desert, it's there. You can read now. Not fast. But we could look together."

"Soren—"

"I can get you in. I know a way through the kitchens. The cook would hide an army if I asked her nicely and brought back her honey jars."

"I can't go to Veimore."

"Why not?"

"Because it's not *there*." Zayn's voice cracked. "It's not in a book. You read them. In the glass. You read them out loud to me. Eight of them burned, and three of them ran, and the ones who ran—" He pointed east, at nothing, at the empty silver desert. "Look what happened. One of them *is* the Hollow. That's the third way, Soren. That's the only other way there is. You become the thing in the storm."

"You don't *know* that."

"I've *felt* it! Every time I use the Light I'm standing on that dune and I can feel the fire coming up my legs, I can *smell* it, I wake up and I can still smell it—" He stopped. He pressed his hand flat over his chest. "It isn't a story anymore. It's going to happen. It's *written*."

"Then we unwrite it."

"You can't unwrite the *sand*!"

The wells, the glass, the storm: none of it had been as loud as that. The words went out across the silver dunes and the desert swallowed them whole and gave nothing back, not even an echo.

They sat there breathing hard on either side of the fire.

Soren looked away first. He looked down at his hands. When he spoke again, his voice had gone quiet.

"Promise me something," he said.

Zayn didn't answer.

"Promise me you'll never give up." Soren looked up. His eyes were very bright. "Whatever happens. Whatever you see in your visions. Whatever the walls say. You keep going. You don't just *lie down* and let it take you. Promise me."

Zayn looked at him across the fire, under the full Ash.

He could not lie. You know that. So when he said it, he said it knowing that the feather on his chest would have burned like a coal if he hadn't meant it with everything he had.

"I promise," said Zayn.

It didn't burn.

My tail lit up the sand like a second fire.

And somewhere out in the silver dark, a long way off, something answered. Not a Hound. Not a voice. A single soft sound, like a heavy book being closed.

Neither of them heard it. I did. I've never known what it was. I've been afraid for a very long time that I do.

---

If they'd stopped there, this would be a different story.

They didn't stop there.

"Good," said Soren. He let out a long breath, almost a laugh. "Good. Then come to Veimore."

"That's not—"

"You just promised."

"I promised not to give up. Going to the dune isn't giving up." Zayn's voice was shaking. "Don't you see? It's the *opposite*. The ones who gave up are the ones who ran. If I keep going, Soren, *that's* where I'm going. That's what not giving up *is*, for me. It's walking into the fire with my eyes open."

Soren stood up so fast he nearly kicked the fire.

"No," he said. "No. I'm not doing this again. I'm not standing by while someone I—" He bit it off. "I'm not letting them take you. I'll drag you to Veimore by your ankles. I'll lock you in the catacombs. I'll find the third way myself and read it to you through the door. I am *not* losing anybody else."

"You can't save me."

"Watch me."

And then Zayn said it.

He didn't plan it. I know that, because I know him. He was frightened, so frightened, and tired past the point where tiredness has a bottom, and the Ash hung full and white above him like an eye that wouldn't close. Soren was standing over him promising things nobody could promise. And it hurt so much to be loved like that, when he knew exactly how it ended, that something in him reached for the sharpest true thing it could find, and threw it.

"You can't save me," he said. "You couldn't even save Nim."

---

The fire cracked.

That was the only sound in the whole desert.

Soren didn't move. He didn't shout. His face didn't even change, at first. He stood there with the full Ash behind him, looking down at Zayn, and something went out of his eyes. It was like watching a lantern go out on a high red wall, one of the ones that must never all go out, and then the next one along, and the next.

My tail glowed. I wish it hadn't. But it was true, every word, and nothing in the world cuts like the truth said at the wrong moment for the wrong reason.

Zayn saw his face. He saw what he'd done.

He got up. He didn't say sorry. He couldn't make his mouth do it. He turned round and walked away from the fire, out into the silver dark.

"Zayn," said Soren behind him.

Just his name. Once. Not loud.

Zayn didn't turn round.

I've thought about that moment more than any other in this story. More than the dune, more than the fire. A boy walking away across moonlit sand, and another boy standing alone by a small fire, saying a name once into the wind.

You know what the rule is. *Never say your name to the wind.*

Nobody ever told Soren what happens when you say someone else's.

He meant to come back. You should know that. He meant to walk until he'd stopped shaking and then come back and say sorry and make it right. There'd be time in the morning, he thought. There is always time in the morning. That's the oldest lie there is, and the feather never burns for it, because nobody knows it's a lie until afterwards.

He walked away and left Soren alone.

It was the worst mistake he ever made. He'd tell you so himself.

---

And where was I?

I'll tell you the first part.

I'd smelled Hounds that evening, before the argument started. A long way off, but coming, drawn by the Light still glowing in the cracks on Zayn's arms. I'd been lying a little way from the fire with my scorched side to the cold sand, listening. When Zayn walked off into the dark, I got up and went the other way.

I went hunting.

I let the Hollow half out. It hurt, worse than it ever had. The burned part of me screamed every time it moved, and the voices inside were louder that night, as if the full Ash had pulled them up near the surface the way it pulls the sea. *Is someone there. Where did everyone go.* But there were Hounds in the dunes and the boy I loved was alone out there, and that is the only use I've ever found for the thing that I am.

I hunted them across the silver sand under the full Ash, for most of the night.

That's all I'm going to tell you about where I was. Don't ask me any more. Not yet.

I said, *don't ask*.

---

Zayn didn't go far. He climbed a high dune a long way off and sat at the top, knees pulled up to his chest, the Ash pouring white light down on him until he cast no shadow at all. He could still see the fire, very small, a single orange point down in the hollow where he'd left it.

He said sorry to it. Out loud, over and over, practising.

*I'm sorry. I didn't mean it. You were right to try. You were right to try to save him. You were right to try to save me.*

He went through it again and again, getting the words right, the way he'd copied his name in the sand until the letters were perfect.

Some time after the middle of the night, he looked down, and the little orange point had gone out.

He told me later that he'd thought nothing of it. Fires burn down. Soren would have let it go and rolled up in his cloak. He'd thought nothing of it, he said, and then he said it again, and the second time his mark burned, very faintly, and he had to stop and start over. *I thought it was nothing. I told myself it was nothing.* That one was true.

Near dawn, with the Ash sinking into the western dunes and the sky going the grey of old bone, he fell asleep sitting up.

He didn't dream. No visions, no warnings. He felt nothing at all. Not a whisper in the sand. Not a single bell.

The Weaver was very quiet that night. I've never forgiven it for that either. It's a long list, the things I haven't forgiven it for. Most of them are quiet nights.

---

He woke when the Fire touched his face. He scrambled up and ran down the dune, sliding and stumbling, the words ready in his mouth. *I'm sorry. You were right to try.*

He came over the last rise and stopped.

There were camels in the hollow. Five of them, kneeling, with blocks of salt strapped to their sides. And there were men, five desert travellers in dusty white, standing in a ring around the cold black circle of the fire.

They weren't talking. That was the first thing. Five grown men and not one of them talking. One had his hand over his mouth.

They were looking down at something on the sand.

Zayn walked toward them. He didn't feel his legs moving. The morning was very bright and very still, and the only sound was a falcon on one man's wrist, screaming and screaming in its hood, the way animals scream at something they can smell and can't see.

Soren lay on his back beside the dead fire, one arm flung out, his broken sword in the sand by his open hand.

He was grey.

Not Husk-grey. Not emptied. Something worse. His skin had gone the colour of old ash, every part of it, his lips, his eyelids, the beds of his nails. And running all through the grey, across his face and down his throat and along both arms, were fine bright lines of *gold*. Thin glowing cracks, like the glaze on a broken pot. They were still glowing. Faintly. Fading even as Zayn watched, one by one, the way the last sparks go in a fire nobody is feeding.

Like the cracks the Light left on Zayn's own skin.

His eyes were open, looking up at the morning sky. They had gone pale. Not white. Pale, like a window someone has breathed on.

His mouth was a little open too, as though he'd been about to say something. As though someone had come up to the fire in the dark, and he'd looked up, and started to say a name.

The sand around him was smooth. Perfectly smooth, the way the wind leaves it, for ten paces in every direction. No struggle. No tracks. As if the desert had tidied up after itself.

And beside the fire, untouched, not a grain out of place, the letters he'd written in the sand four nights before and somehow never rubbed out. Zayn hadn't noticed they were still there. He noticed now.

ZAYN AND SOREN, MONSTERS.

Except the first word had been smoothed away. Just that one. The rest were perfect.

---

One of the travellers saw Zayn first.

An older man with a beard gone yellow at the edges. He backed away a step, then another. The others turned and saw, and went still.

They looked at Zayn. They looked at his arms, at the golden cracks still glowing faintly from wrist to shoulder.

They looked at Soren, cracked with the same gold.

"That's him," said the young man with the falcon. His voice was high and thin. "The prince. The exiled one. The *king's son*. Look at the jaw, the scar. They said in the free towns he was out here selling ash—"

"And *that*," said the old man, staring at Zayn, "is the glowing boy."

Zayn didn't move.

"From the caravan," the old man said. "They talked about him at the last well. The one the beast didn't eat. A man came out of the sand alive and he was telling everyone." He was backing toward his camel. "*Monster*, he said. And here he is."

"I didn't," said Zayn.

It came out of him like something broken. He looked at Soren, and back at them.

"I didn't do this."

The feather mark didn't burn. He felt it not burn. It was true, every word. And it didn't matter at all, because these men had never heard of a boy who couldn't lie, and if they had, they wouldn't have believed it.

There were other true things, too. They came up in him one after another, like water rising in a well that's about to drown you.

*I left him.*

*I said the worst thing I could think of, and I left him alone in the dark, and I watched his fire go out and I told myself it was nothing.*

"I left him," said Zayn. "I walked away. I left him alone."

The feather didn't burn for that either.

The travellers stared at him. And then one of them, I never learned which, said it: the word that would follow Zayn across the whole desert, carried on falcons' legs and in traders' mouths, faster than any lizard could run.

"*Prince-Killer.*"

They ran.

They were on their camels and away in moments, salt-blocks swinging, the falcon still screaming. East, toward the free towns, toward the wells. Toward everyone. I could hear them shouting it to each other as they went, until the dunes swallowed the sound.

*Prince-Killer. Prince-Killer. The glowing boy killed the prince.*

By the next turn of the Ash there wouldn't be a town in the desert that hadn't heard it. They'd paint it on doors, I heard later. Not his name. He didn't have one. Just that.

---

Zayn knelt in the sand beside Soren.

He didn't touch him at first. He knelt there with his hands open on his knees and the gold fading in Soren's face, crack by crack, until there were only a few lines left glowing at the corners of his eyes, like the last of something trying to get out.

Then, very slowly, he reached out to close Soren's eyes.

They didn't want to close. The lids were stiff and cold as wax and slid back open, a little, both times. The third time he held his fingers there and counted, the way you count through a pain, and when he let go they stayed. Mostly. A thin pale line of eye still showed beneath each lid, watching nothing.

He wanted to heal him. Of course he did. His hands were already burning. He laid them on Soren's chest and called the Light, and it came, gold and desperate, more gold than it had ever come for anyone. It poured into Soren and went looking.

It found nothing. Not a dark place, not an emptiness like a Husk's, where something had been scooped out. *Nothing.* As if there had never been anyone in there. The Light went down and down and touched no bottom, and came back to him cold.

He'd never seen it fail before.

And then, because it always did now, the vision came. Not of Soren. There was no past or future in Soren to see. Only his own: the full Ash, the dune, the fire climbing. But this time, for one breath, he wasn't alone in it. Somebody was standing on the sand below him, just at the edge of the light, very still, looking up. He couldn't see the face.

Then it was gone, and he was kneeling in the morning with his hands on a cold chest.

He took Soren's hand. It was cold. The fingers were grey and cracked with gold, and on the right hand, on the finger where the heavy ring had always been, there was nothing. Only a band of paler skin where it had sat for years. The ring he never took off.

Zayn looked at that bare finger for a long time.

He looked at his own arms, at the cracks. Then at the cracks on Soren's face. The same gold. The same light. And a question came into his eyes that I could see as clearly as if it had been written in the sand. He'd been asleep. He'd been far away. He'd felt nothing. But the Light had burst out of him once already when he didn't mean it to, and nearly killed me.

He thought of the little orange point going out.

*Did I do this?*

He would carry that question for a long time. I'm sorry to tell you that. I could have taken it from him. I'm sorry to tell you that, too.

---

The broken sword was lying in the sand by Soren's open hand.

Zayn picked it up.

It was lighter than he'd expected. Half a blade, a hand's width and a little more of jagged steel, dark leather on the grip and a bronze pommel that didn't quite match. It was cold. Everything in that hollow was cold, though the Fire was well up by then. He held it across his knees the way Soren used to hold it by the fire at night.

He didn't know what was inside it. Not yet.

He sat there with the sword in his lap and his friend beside him while the Fire climbed and the shadows of the dunes went short. Then the wind came, and began, gently, the way it does, to move the sand. A little over Soren's hand first. A little into the open fold of his collar. The desert, doing what the desert does with everything it's given.

Zayn watched it happen and didn't stop it. He didn't cry. Not then. That came later.

He just sat, and held on.

---

I was already on the dune above the hollow when he came over the rise that morning. I'd been there before the travellers came.

Don't ask me how long.

I sat at the top of the slope, a long way off, with my scorched fur and my one good eye, and I didn't go any closer. My tail lay in the sand behind me. It wasn't glowing. It wasn't dark. It just *lay* there, the way a tail does when no one's saying anything at all.

Zayn didn't look up. I'm not sure he knew I was there.

I could have gone down to him. I could have sat in his lap and let him hold me instead of a cold broken sword. I could have said a great many things. One thing, really. One thing, with a face in it.

I didn't say anything.

I'm very good at that. I've told you before. It's becoming a theme.

It's the one I'm least proud of. It's the one that still wakes me, when I sleep, which isn't often. In the dream I'm always on that dune, and the fire is always going out, and I am always, always a moment too far away.

That's all. That's all you get. Turn the page.

---

*End of Chapter Twelve*
