# Chapter Nineteen
## The Crown Dune

> *He'll walk where the dunes write the words of the sky,*
> *And the light will be born on the day he will die.*
> — from the Song of the Phoenix-Born; the verse mothers in the desert villages will not sing aloud

---

Mother Yusa found us on the palace stairs as we were leaving.

She'd come up out of the kitchens with a ladle like a club and a long red scratch down her cheek where something had reached for her in the dark. She saw the king's ring on the cord at Zayn's neck and understood everything at once. She didn't cry. She didn't ask. She pushed a small cloth bundle into his hands, took his face between her two enormous palms, and kissed his forehead hard, the way you'd stamp a seal into wax.

She pulled back with her lips blistered. His skin was that hot. She didn't say a word about it.

Then she went back down into the dark to look for Idan's nurse, and I never saw her again. I have never asked what found her first.

Inside the bundle were dates. Rolled in honey.

---

We rode out through the eastern gate, Daruun in front and Zayn behind him and me in the boy's lap, bleeding quietly into his shirt.

The Husks still stood where they'd stopped, every one facing east. As we passed, they began to say their last words again, very softly, all together, so that we went out of Veimore through a long low murmur of *bar the door* and *Mama, I'm here* and *where's the oil*. Then, one by one, they stopped speaking and only smiled.

Zayn unwrapped the bundle and held the dates in his palm.

He ate one. He held one out over Daruun's shoulder, and after a moment his father took it. Then he gave one to me, and I ate it, though I prefer scorpions and always will.

"This is the hardest day," Zayn said. "I don't think there's a harder one."

My tail glowed.

The Crown Dune rose ahead of us until it filled half the sky, and its flanks went on moving. Up. Pause. Down.

"Father," said Zayn.

It was the first time he'd called him that. I felt Daruun go rigid in the saddle.

"I forgive you."

Daruun didn't turn round. His shoulders shook. "You can't. I ran. If I hadn't run, none of this— your brother, the Hollow, all of it. You can't forgive that."

"I just did," said Zayn. "And I can't lie. So."

My tail lit up so bright it threw our shadow out ahead of us across the sand, long and gold, like a road.

Daruun reached back without looking, and Zayn took his hand, and they rode like that all the way to the foot of the dune. Zayn's hand was so hot that Daruun's palm blistered. He didn't let go.

---

The Husks were waiting.

Not only the ones from Veimore. All of them. They had come from the whole desert in the night, from the grey wastes and the emptied villages, from Harrow's End and the caravan roads, and they stood in a ring around the foot of the Crown Dune a hundred deep. Herders. Traders. Old women. Children. Thousands of white faces turned up toward the top of the dune, all smiling the same smile, all humming the cult's two notes so low that the sound came up through the sand into our legs. It was like standing on the skin of a drum.

They parted for us like water.

I saw Garrem from the trading post among them, and old Oszkar, his beard full of sand. I didn't see Bram.

We climbed. The sand slid under our feet, and under the sand something moved with the breathing, so that every step went down a little further than it should. Halfway up, Zayn turned and picked me up without a word and carried me the rest of the way. His arms singed my fur. I let him. I was too tired to be proud.

And then we were at the top, and I looked up, and it was the vision.

I had seen it in his eyes every time he healed. Here it was, every piece falling into place like the last stones of a tomb.

The Ash hung full and white above the Crown Dune, so close and so swollen it looked like something that had fed. The crest was pale sand, so cold after his skin that it ached. Below us, on every side, the desert went away silver and grey to the edge of the world.

Orrin waited at the far end of the crest, with Idan. Around him stood twenty of the Unnamed, white masks shining like small moons. A little apart stood Brother Seven, the crack in his mask so wide now that half his real face showed through, the skin around it raw where he'd been tearing at it.

Idan stood quite still in Orrin's grip, eyes open, humming.

And between us and them, the grey was gathering.

---

It rose out of the sand like smoke from a fire put out with water. It thickened. It took a shape.

A boy. Smaller than Zayn, barefoot and thin, in a shepherd's tunic, with a shepherd's crook. Everything about him was grey. Only his eyes were different: black, completely black, like two holes cut into the night, and if you looked too long you could see things moving far down in them, small and pale, like faces at the bottom of a well.

I knew that face. I had known it before there were cities in this desert, when it was brown and laughing and smelled of sheep.

"Hello, Malek," I said.

"Hello, little fox." A child's voice, and under it a thousand others, a beat behind. "You've come to watch another one burn."

"I've come to watch one *choose*. It's different. You'd know, if you'd done it."

The grey boy smiled. The smile went a little too far back on both sides.

Then he turned his black eyes on Zayn.

"Look at them," Malek said softly, pointing his crook down at the Husks. "That one threw stones at you when you were six. Those ones called you monster and watched you starve. And you'd burn for them?"

Zayn's light flickered grey at the edges.

"And here's what the fox won't tell you," said Malek, gentle as sand. "Ask him how many. Not bearers. *Children*. Ask him how old the Boy of Salt was. Ask him why, every time, it's a child. Ask him what's waiting on the other side of the light with its mouth open. It isn't me, Zayn. I'm only the one who said no."

Zayn looked at me.

My tail glowed. Gold. Steady. I couldn't stop it. I've never been able to stop it.

I saw it go into him.

"And after," said Malek, "they'll light a lantern. One more in a row of eleven. In a hundred years no one will remember what it was for. Bearers' names come off the walls like the names in Veimore. You'll be forgotten." A whisper. "You'll be forgotten, Zayn. Just like you always were."

My tail went dark. Pitch dark, cold as a dead coal.

But it didn't matter what my tail said. It was the oldest fear he had, the one he'd carried since he was a baby crying in the sand. *Nobody will remember me.* And now, beside it, a new one I had put there: *and something is hungry for me, and always has been.*

His light went grey all the way through.

"There's a way out," said Orrin.

He stepped forward with one hand on Idan's shoulder and the thin grey blade in the other. His soft voice carried along the crest the way it had filled the throne hall.

"Give me your name. That's all. Say it to the wind. Your true name, the one in your blood. Malek will take it, and the Light with it, and it's over. No burning. You'll simply forget. And then everyone will forget, every loss, every grief, every dead son." Something moved in his faded eyes, far down. "I promise you, child, it doesn't hurt. I burned my own name to stop remembering. Forgetting is the only mercy there is."

My tail flickered. Neither dark nor bright.

He believed it. That's the worst kind of lie, little one: the kind the liar has told himself so long it sounds like truth.

"Give me your name," said Orrin.

And Zayn, grey and shaking, opened his mouth.

---

Bells.

Small bronze bells, a great many of them, ringing faintly somewhere below on the slope. Even the Husks' hum faltered.

Someone was climbing the Crown Dune on hands and knees through the sliding sand. Around his waist hung a belt of tiny truth bells that chimed with every movement, and all the way down the slope the Husks had turned to watch him pass, and none of them had touched him.

Old Hesset.

For three hundred years he'd been bound to the edge of the desert, the price of the night he ran. To leave was to die.

He had walked across the whole Destiny Desert anyway.

He was dying as he climbed. You could see it. His beard was thin as smoke. His skin had gone papery and was splitting at the knuckles, and what came out of the splits wasn't blood but a fine pale dust. He climbed toward our voices as if they were a fire on a cold night.

He reached the crest and knelt, gasping. Malek turned to him.

"Hesset," said the grey boy, warmly. "My old friend."

Every bell on Hesset's belt rang at once.

"I was never your friend," said Hesset. "You only told me what I wanted to hear, so I'd run. I've had three hundred years in the dark to work that out." He turned his blind face. "Boy. Are you there?"

"I'm here."

"I asked you once, in my tent. *Would you die for them?* You couldn't answer." The old man swallowed. "Someone asked me the same, on this sand, three hundred years ago. I said no, and I ran, and the last thing I ever saw was the top of this dune. I hid from you that bearers die, because I was ashamed. And I hid something else. When I said no, up here, I *heard* it, child. I heard it be disappointed." His hands clenched in the sand. "I'm sorry. I'm so sorry."

Not a single bell rang.

"Don't answer for me," whispered Hesset. "Don't answer for them. Don't answer for whatever's listening. Answer for you."

Zayn stood very still on the top of the Crown Dune.

He looked at Malek. He looked at me, and at my tail, which had told him a thing about the Light he would never now stop knowing. He looked down at the Husks. He looked at Idan, humming, forgetting his own name a little more with every breath. He looked at his father.

Then he untied Hesset's truth bell from his belt and held it up in front of him, so everyone could see.

"Yes," he said.

The bell didn't ring.

And the grey went out of his light like a shadow when the Fire comes up. It turned gold. A deeper gold than I had ever seen, the gold of honey held up to the light. It was beautiful. I want you to know that. It was the most beautiful thing I have ever seen.

It was also the colour of a coal just before it falls apart.

Malek took a step backward.

---

"Brother Seven," said Orrin sharply. "Cut him."

Tamir raised his twin grey blades, the only things in the world that could cut the Light. The blades were shaking, and through the great crack in his mask one pale eye stared, wet and wide.

He was humming. Not the chant. The phoenix song. He didn't know he was doing it.

Zayn walked straight up to him, until the grey blades were a finger's width from his chest and the steel began to frost and smoke at once where the Light met it.

"You held me," he said. "When I was born. I saw it. You were five, and you held me, and you were scared you'd drop me."

The blades trembled. Then, slowly, Tamir lowered them.

Zayn walked past him to Orrin. The vizier backed away, dragging Idan, and laid the grey blade against the boy's throat. Zayn didn't stop. He put his hand over Orrin's hand on the grip, and the gold went into it.

Orrin cried out and let go. His hand was grey to the wrist where the Light had touched it, crazed with fine cracks like a glaze. The blade fell into the sand.

Idan stopped humming. He blinked. He said, in a small, astonished voice, "Idan," as if he'd found it in a pocket. Then he ran, straight to Daruun, and Daruun caught him, and the boy buried his face in the grey wrappings and shook.

Zayn knelt before him. He untied Soren's ring from beside the infinity knot and pressed it into Idan's hand. It left a red mark on the small palm. Idan closed his fingers on it anyway.

"Your father wanted you to have this. And so would Soren."

Idan was ten. He wasn't stupid.

"Are you going to die?"

"Yes."

"Like Soren?"

Zayn thought about it. "No," he said. "Soren didn't get to choose. I do."

---

Daruun let go of Idan and took his son's hands, and didn't flinch at the heat.

"Let me," he said.

It was not the voice of the Veiled Rider.

"Let me do it. It was mine before it was yours, and I ran. Feed it me. I'm old, I'm nothing, let it have me instead. I ran so you could live. Don't make that for nothing. Please, son. Not you. Not you."

Zayn shook his head. He couldn't speak for a moment.

"It doesn't want you," he said, very gently. "It wants the Light, and the Light's in me." He tightened his grip. "And it wasn't for nothing. You left me in the sand, and the desert kept me, and I grew up alone, so I know what it's like to be the one nobody wants. That's why I can do this. It all meant something. Even the bad parts. *Especially* the bad parts."

My tail was so bright I couldn't look at it.

I'll tell you what I thought, little one. I thought: *it meant something. And I no longer know whether that's a comfort or the worst thing in the world.*

Daruun bowed his head over their joined hands. I have never seen anything as brave as what he did next.

He let go.

One finger at a time, slowly, like a man lowering something precious into deep water. The skin of his palms stuck to his son's hands and tore, and he let go anyway. When the last finger slid free, he stepped back beside Idan and Hesset and didn't move again.

The hardest choice of his life. He made it.

---

Zayn turned to me last.

I was sitting in the sand, too tired to stand, my fur dark with my own blood, my silver eye aching the way it always aches near the end. He knelt so our faces were level. Heat came off him like an open oven.

"You lied to me once," he said.

"I know."

"Tell me the truth now. Will it hurt?"

"Yes," I said. My tail glowed. "More than anything has ever hurt."

"Will it matter?"

"Yes."

"Is Malek right? Is something waiting?"

"I don't know," I said.

And my tail, for the first time in all my life, did nothing at all. Neither gold nor dark. Some truths are too big even for a fox's tail.

He looked at it a long moment. Then he smiled, the small, crooked, rusty smile he never wasted, and put his forehead against mine. It singed the fur between my ears. I'll carry the mark until the world ends.

"Look after them," he said.

For the first time in all my long life, I couldn't think of a single word.

He walked to the very end of the crest, where the dune fell away on every side into the silver dark, and stood under the full Ash with his back to us all.

"I always wanted to be a hero," he said quietly. To the boy in the patched tent, maybe. "Like in the stories."

He took a breath.

"I am the hero."

It wasn't a boast. It was only true. My tail had never burned so bright.

Then he lifted his face to the Ash, and to whatever was beyond it, and he said the last words he would ever say.

"I'm coming, Soren."

---

And here I have to tell it the way I told it at the very beginning, because it has never stopped happening. It's happening now, somewhere behind my eyes, and I can still smell it.

He is burning.

He stands at the top of the Crown Dune with his arms open, as if he's about to catch something falling out of the sky. The fire doesn't start at his hands. It starts in his chest, under the mark, the small golden feather that has glowed in the dark his whole life. It splits like a seed splits, from the inside. I hear it crack. Light comes through every golden line that every healing left on him, and the lines split wider, and his skin curls back from them like paper from a flame. It runs down his arms and up his throat. It comes out of his eyes, and his eyes go with it.

The smell reaches me. Honey and hot metal, and under that, his hair. I will never stop smelling it.

And Orrin lunges.

Grey robes, grey hand, grey eyes wide and starving. He's waited his whole life for this light, and he throws himself into it with both hands, grabbing, as if he could tear it out of the boy's chest and keep it.

"*Mine,*" he says.

It isn't. It never could be. The Light can't be taken. It can only be given.

It turns grey in his hands. The gold he's grasping goes dull, then ashen, and the grey runs up his fingers and wrists and arms like frost climbing a window, cracking as it climbs. For a single moment his face changes. He sees something in the stolen light: a small boy, and a brother, and a name he burned long ago. His mouth opens to say it.

"Ez—"

His jaw goes first.

It doesn't fall. It crumbles, like an old grey statue left too long in the wind, and the rest of him follows from the face down, still kneeling, still reaching. The last of him to go is his hands, clenched in the light, trying to hold on. The wind lifts the dust off the Crown Dune and carries it into the dark, the way it carries away a name spoken carelessly.

There is nothing left of him. Not even the name. He never finished it.

And the boy doesn't scream.

That's the part I always remember. He is burning to the bone and he doesn't scream, and I have never understood how, and I have never wanted to.

He looks down. Below him, all across the desert, the dunes are moving, shifting and folding and rising, writing across the sand in letters miles high. The same shapes they wrote for him on the first nights after Harrow's End, when he couldn't read a word and I wouldn't tell him what they said.

He can read them now. A boy with a broken sword taught him, laughing the whole time.

*A*, the dunes write. *E. R. O. N.*

*Aeron.* In the old tongue, *the one who rises.*

His true name, the one the Weaver wrote for him before he was born, the one the wind could never find, because the desert kept it safe all this time. The way you keep something safe that you mean to use. He reads it, the last thing he will ever read, with eyes that are more light than eye now, and I see what's left of his mouth smile.

Then he's looking at something only he can see. But I see it too, for a moment, in the light pouring out of him.

The Singing Wells, under a thin Ash. The water humming. A young man on the edge of the well with half a sword across his knees, a scarred jaw and a crooked grin and dark eyes, laughing at him the way he always did. Holding out his hand.

*You took your time,* says Soren.

And behind Soren, in the dark beyond the well, standing in a row as if they've waited a very long time, I see the others. Eight small figures, the eight who gave themselves. Some barely taller than the well's edge. A boy of nine with salt-white hair. A smith's apprentice with burned hands. A girl holding a weaver's shuttle. All looking at Zayn. None of them smiling. Their faces are patient and gentle and very, very tired, the faces of children who have stood in a queue too long.

Zayn laughs, the way he laughed the first time, by a fire, when a prince tried to eat a scorpion. He takes the hand. The row of children opens to let him in, and he takes his place at the end of it.

There's a space beside him. An empty space at the very end of the row, the width of one small child.

I see it every time. I have never once been able to look away from it.

The light keeps growing until it isn't a boy anymore. It's a shape, two great wings of gold unfolding across the dark, wide enough to cover the dunes from one edge of the sky to the other.

Then it falls.

Gold rains down over the desert, and everywhere it lands, the grey draws back.

Somewhere below, someone is shouting a name.

It's his father, on his knees in the sand a little way down the crest, his arms around a crying prince, his raw hands, his face turned up into the falling gold. Shouting it over and over, the only name he was ever allowed to call his son.

"*Zayn! Zayn!*"

And the wind takes it, gently, and carries it out over the whole desert. Not to the dark this time.

There isn't any dark left.

That's what I believed, that night. I want you to know I believed it. For one whole night, I did.

---

*End of Chapter Nineteen*
