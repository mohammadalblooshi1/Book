# Chapter Fifteen
## The Buried Ship

> *A ship in the desert is a coffin waiting for the sea. What does it hold while it waits?*
> — a riddle the caravan children tell, which has no answer, and which they are told not to tell after dark

---

I'm going to tell you this part the way he told it to me.

He told it once, in the mountain caves, by a fire that kept going out, in a flat, quiet voice I never heard him use again. He looked at his hands the whole time. I spent most of it under a dead ship with sand in my mouth.

---

It took me a night and a day to find it.

I followed the camels, and under the camel-smell, the cold, empty smell of grey blades. And another, the closer I got: burning cloth, and hair, and something sweet and scorched beneath them, like honey left on a stove.

I carried Hesset's bell in my teeth the whole way.

At dusk on the second day, under an Ash thinned almost to nothing, a white rind, like the edge of an eye rolled back, I came over a long ridge and saw it.

A ship.

There's no sea in the Destiny Desert. There never has been. And yet there it lay between two great dunes: a vast wooden hull, longer than Harrow's End, sunk on its side like a drowned animal, its carved prow rearing out of the sand with its mouth open, its ribs pale through the broken planks.

And inside it, through every crack and gap, lanterns burned.

Grey lanterns, the colour of ash. Their light didn't spill onto the sand. It stayed close to the hull, like breath on cold glass.

I crept down the slope in the dark, and found a place where the sand had drifted up against the hull, and dug.

---

He woke up chained.

A great beam ran down the middle of the hold, and from an iron ring in it ran a grey chain to a cuff on each of his wrists. The chain was cold in a way iron isn't. When the light in his chest tried to rise, the chain drank it, with a faint sound like a child sucking at a cut finger.

The planks were covered in writing.

Names. Hundreds of them, scratched into the wood with nails and buckles and fingernails, as high as a man could reach. Every one had been scraped over afterwards until only a pale smear was left, like a word rubbed out of the sand. In places, scraped names lay over scraped names, years deep. He couldn't read a single one.

In the far corner, tossed in a heap like rubbish, were his bag and Soren's broken sword.

He stared at that sword a long time.

---

They brought him up on the first night, for the burning.

The upper deck lay tilted under the sky, one edge running down into the sand like a ramp. The Unnamed stood in a circle on it. Fifty of them, perhaps more, grey robes, smooth white masks, silent as Husks. In the middle stood an iron bowl, and in the bowl a fire burned.

The flames were grey, and gave off no heat. Only cold, the way cold comes off a cellar door.

To one side, in a line, stood the children.

There were eleven. The youngest perhaps five, the oldest perhaps twelve. Little grey robes. Shaved heads. They stood very straight and still, the way children stand when they've learned that moving gets you hurt.

They weren't crying. Not one. Not a sniff. Frightened children cry. These had been frightened so long that crying had been worn out of them, the way well-stones get worn into the shape of hands.

Behind them, on a row of pegs along the rail, hung eleven small white masks. Waiting.

One girl, six or so, kept glancing at her peg. Not in fear. The way a child glances at a door when she's been promised someone will come through it soon.

The Unnamed brought Zayn into the circle and forced him to his knees.

Then three young men stepped forward. They wore grey, but no masks. Each held a strip of white cloth, and on each, in charcoal, a word was written.

A name.

The circle began to chant: the wordless hum from the storm, fifty throats rising and falling like one breath. Then it flattened into words, fifty voices in one:

"*Names are chains. Memory is pain. Forget, and be free.*"

The first young man held his strip of cloth over the bowl. He said the name on it, once, out loud. His own name. He said it the way you'd say goodbye to someone at a gate.

He let it fall.

The cloth caught and curled, and as it burned he tried to say his name again. The first sound came. The second, slower. Then his lips stopped halfway through the shape of it and stayed open, puzzled, like a man who has walked into a room and forgotten why. The smell came off the bowl: scorched cloth, and that sweet burnt something underneath.

Then his face went slack. Not peaceful. *Empty*. The way a house looks the day after the family moves out.

A brother stepped up behind each of them and pressed a white mask to his face, and Zayn saw its edges *settle*, softening against the skin like wax near a flame. One of the young men raised a hand and felt for the edge, and couldn't find one. He lowered his hand.

Then the children were given little brooms to sweep the ash into a sack. The smallest hummed the chant while she swept, perfectly, every rise and fall.

Three men had walked into the circle. Three nobodies walked out.

Zayn told me it was the worst thing he ever saw. Worse than the Husks, because the Husks hadn't chosen it. And worse than the choosing, he said, was how *relieved* they looked.

---

Then a figure stepped into the firelight, and the circle bowed.

Grey robe, white mask, like all the others. But when it spoke, Zayn knew the voice before the first word was done.

"Hello again, child."

*The Voice*, they called him there. A voice was all he'd kept after he burned his name.

He crouched in front of Zayn and held out a strip of white cloth and a stick of charcoal.

"Write it," he said.

Zayn looked at the cloth.

"You can now, can't you?" said the Voice softly. "He taught you. Write it, and give it to the fire, and all of this stops. The grief. The guilt. That walk under the full Ash, every time you close your eyes. You saw their faces. Tell me they weren't at peace."

The charcoal hung in front of Zayn's face.

He thought of Soren drawing letters in the sand with a stick. *Z. Like a lightning bolt. Then A, like a tent. Then Y, like a man waving for help.* Laughing. Always laughing.

Zayn spat on the cloth.

The circle was very quiet.

The Voice looked down at the wet spot a moment. Then he folded the cloth carefully and tucked it into his robe, close to his heart, where he kept the ring.

"I must go back to Veimore," he said, rising. "The Night of Embers is close." He turned to the circle. "Drain him slowly. Make him remember. When he is empty enough to forget his own name, he will give it to us himself. They always do."

He rode off into the dark toward the red city. I'd like to tell you that was the last we saw of him. I can't.

---

They put him back on his chain in the hold, and the draining began.

I won't tell you all of it. But somebody should know what he survived.

They came in pairs, every few hours: one with a lantern, one with a grey blade.

A grey blade doesn't cut. It lies flat against you, cold as the bottom of a well, and pulls, and the Light comes out of you in thin gold threads and runs into the blade and goes grey.

And with each thread, a memory came.

Never a good one. The good ones they took. The bad ones they *played*. Zayn was six again, running with a stone in his back. Or kneeling with Tobin's hand in his while Bram screamed *Monster!*

Or he was on the dune under the full Ash.

Over and over: turning away, walking, watching the orange point of the fire go out. And each time, the memory changed a little. The fire went out sooner. Soren called after him, begging. By the second day he couldn't tell which version was true. In the version he saw by then, he'd heard Soren scream.

He hadn't. I know that much. One day I'll tell you how.

I couldn't tell him. I was under the hull, hearing every sound he made and making none, waiting for the one moment that would work. That's what I told myself. True things can still be cowardly.

---

Each time they finished with him, they marched the children past to watch. On the first day one boy flinched when Zayn cried out. On the second, nobody did. They looked at him with soft, patient faces, the way you'd watch rain.

On the second night, they let the Husks in.

Dozens of Husks were penned in the lower hold. The Unnamed didn't fight them. They *kept* them, like dogs. And Husks are drawn to light, the way moths are, with the same ending.

They shuffled out of the dark and gathered round the boy on the chain, close enough that he could smell them: dust and old breath and nothing at all, the smell of a house nobody lives in.

They were all muttering. That's what Husks do. Each one saying its own last words to itself, over and over, a soft dry babble like water in a ditch. *Mind the step. Mind the step.* *Is that you?* *Bring the goats in, it's going to blow.* *Tell her I said—tell her I said—tell her—*

Then, all at once, they stopped.

Every head turned to the same angle at once, the way the masks had in the storm. The silence was so complete he could hear the grey lanterns hiss.

Then they spoke.

Every mouth, together. One voice out of thirty throats, not a syllable apart. Not their voices. Not any voice they'd ever had.

"*Little light.*"

Zayn went rigid on his chain.

He knew that voice. He'd heard it in a dream, in the Valley of Echoes, leaning over him in the dark.

"*Little nameless light,*" said the Husks. Thirty jaws moving as one. "*They threw stones at you. They called you monster. They drove you into the desert to die. And you'd burn for them? Why? No one remembers the bearers. Ask the fox. Ask him how many he's buried. Ask him if anyone sings their names.*"

A pause. Thirty heads tilting, exactly together.

"*Ask him why it's always children.*"

Zayn shut his eyes.

"*Ask him who it is that's fed,*" the voice went on, gentle, almost sorry, "*every time one of you burns. Ask him what's on the other side of the Light. He's seen it. He's been inside it. He knows.*"

"*Let it go,*" said thirty voices. "*Let me hold it for you. It's so heavy, little light. I know. I carried it too.*"

Then the voice went softer. Almost shy. All thirty faces turned down, toward the floor of the hold, toward the sand where I lay.

"*Hello, Kiri,*" it said. "*I know you're down there. You always did listen at doors.*"

Under the hull, in the dark, I stopped breathing.

The burned place in my side said its three words louder. In his voice. It had always been his voice.

I won't tell you what that voice sounded like centuries ago, when it was a shepherd boy calling me in from the hills at dusk. That's mine. I'll only tell you that I didn't answer, and that it was the hardest thing I have ever not done.

---

The children came at dawn, with water.

One of them stopped in front of Zayn and stared.

He was seven. His head was shaved, his robe too big. And his eyes were brown, clear brown, not faded at all. Not yet.

"You're the monster," said Tobin.

He said it without fear. That was the horror of it. He said it the way you'd name a colour.

He'd last seen that face grey and empty in Harrow's End.

"Bram said you weren't," Tobin whispered. "After. He said he was wrong." Something moved in his face, deep down, like a fish rising in murky water. His lip shook. Just once. "Is Bram coming?"

Zayn opened his mouth. And closed it.

He wanted to say *yes. I promise. I'll take you home.* It's what you say to a frightened child in the dark.

But Zayn couldn't lie, and he didn't know.

"I'll try," he said. It was all he had. "I'll try. I swear I will."

Tobin nodded, as if *I'll try* from someone who couldn't lie was worth more than *I promise* from anyone else.

Then his lip went still again, and the fish sank, and his face smoothed over. He hummed two notes of the chant under his breath, without seeming to know he'd done it, and followed the others up the ladder.

---

That night, alone on the chain, Zayn reached for his name and couldn't find it.

The blades had been at him all day. He was so cold he couldn't feel his hands. And he thought, the way you think of something just to make sure it's still there, *My name is*—

And there was nothing.

A blank, smooth place, like the face of a mask. He could feel the edges of where his name should be, the way a tongue feels a lost tooth. He reached for the village, the patched tent, and they were greying at the edges like cloth left in the Fire.

Worse: it didn't hurt. It felt like setting something down. He understood, then, the young men's faces at the bowl.

*Write it,* the Voice had said. *They always do.*

His finger moved in the dust on the floor of the hold. Searching. He didn't know what for.

"That's not even a letter," said someone.

The hold was full of gold.

---

Soren was sitting on the beam above him, swinging his legs.

He looked the way he had on the dune, but dimmer, and the edges of him wouldn't hold still. They drifted like ash lifting off a fire just gone out, and where they drifted Zayn's breath smoked.

"I spend a whole turn of the Ash teaching you," he said, "and you give up on the first word. I'm insulted. I'm deeply insulted."

Zayn stared up at him.

"Z," said Soren. He leaned down and pointed at the dust. His finger left no mark. "Like a lightning bolt. Go on."

Zayn's finger moved. A line. A slant. A line.

"A. Like a tent." Soren was smiling now. "Y. Like a man waving for help, which is you, right now, honestly. And N. Two posts and a fence. Go on. Say it."

"*Zayn,*" whispered Zayn.

It came back all at once, like water into a dry well. A boy of seven at the edge of a village well, deciding he'd be called a word that sounded like wind over a dune.

"There you are," said Soren softly.

His hand came down on Zayn's shoulder. Warm. Warm as the Fire, though the frost was creeping out across the boards from where he sat, over the scraped-out names.

"You promised," he said. "Never give up." Then, quieter, with a grin that was almost the old grin: "Besides. There are eleven kids up there who are forgetting their names too. Somebody's going to have to teach them. I'd do it, but I'm a bit busy being dead."

Zayn grabbed at his sleeve. His fingers went through it into cold. "What's it *like*? Soren. Where you are. Is it—"

Soren's smile stayed exactly where it was. But his eyes went past Zayn again, to the dark at the end of the hold, the way they had on the dune. As if someone were standing there. As if someone were always standing there, wherever he went now.

"Crowded," he said.

Then the gold went out, and Zayn was alone in the dark, with four letters drawn in the dust beside his hand.

He didn't wipe them away.

In the morning, when the lantern came down the ladder, there were nine letters in the dust, not four. Under his own name, in his own hand, pressed in deep with one finger, the S the right way round:

SOREN.

He didn't remember writing it. He never did.

---

The guard who came at midnight on the third night was alone, and he was humming.

Zayn knew the tune before he knew the man: fire and rising wings, the song from the Singing Wells.

The masked figure came down the ladder with a lantern and two grey blades crossed on his back, sat a little way off, and went on humming as if he didn't know he was doing it. As if it were the last thing left in him, the way Husks have their last words.

Brother Seven. Always hunting him. Always, somehow, missing.

Zayn started to hum too.

It just came out of him: the next line, the one the masked man hadn't reached yet.

The humming stopped.

Brother Seven sat completely still. The white mask stared across the lantern-light.

"Where did you learn it?" Zayn whispered. "Who taught it to you?"

No answer. I never once, in all that time, heard him speak.

He stood and drew one of the grey blades. *Drain him slowly.* He knelt by the chain and laid the blade flat against Zayn's arm.

And Zayn's hand closed around his wrist.

He wasn't thinking about himself. That's the only reason it worked. He was thinking of eleven children who had stopped crying, and a boy asking whether his brother was coming.

The light came up out of his chest like the Fire coming over the dunes.

*Gold.*

It ran down his arm and into the grey blade and *through* it, faster than the blade could drink. Up the masked man's wrist. Up his arm. Into him.

And they both saw it.

---

A tent, at night. Lamplight. A storm howling outside, the walls of the tent bowing in and out like something breathing.

A small boy, five years old, sitting on a rug, very serious. In his arms, held carefully, the way he'd been shown, a newborn wrapped in grey cloth woven all over with strange symbols. The baby had one tiny fist round the boy's finger and wouldn't let go.

Somewhere above them, a man's voice was singing. Low and warm. Fire and rising wings.

The boy looked down at the baby and smiled. A gap-toothed smile, so proud he could burst.

Then the tent wall tore open, and there were white masks in the storm.

---

The vision broke.

So did the mask.

I heard it from under the hull: a clean crack, like ice splitting. A line ran down the white face from brow to chin, with gold shining through. Along the crack, Zayn saw, the mask had grown *into* him, fine pale threads running into the skin, and where they'd split they bled, very slightly, grey.

Through the gap, for one heartbeat, an eye. Wide and wet and pale.

Too pale for a desert child.

And Zayn saw what he always saw: the full Ash, the Crown Dune, the fire. Clearer. He could feel the heat on his shins now, his skin tightening. And there were people in it. A man on his knees in the sand, begging. A figure in a cracked white mask, standing very still.

It took a long time for his legs to stop burning.

Brother Seven tore free and scrambled backwards, one hand clamped over the crack, making a small broken wordless sound, like a child woken from a nightmare who can't find the door.

He stared at Zayn.

Then his blade flashed once. I can't tell you whether he meant to do it. I don't think he knew himself. But the grey chain parted between Zayn's wrists like a cut thread.

And Brother Seven fled. Up the ladder, through the hatch, out into the night. Humming again, I swear it, as he went. Broken, out of tune. But humming.

---

The cuffs were still on his wrists. It didn't matter. He was across the hold before the hatch stopped swinging, the broken sword in his hand and his bag over his shoulder.

He went up the ladder to the children.

They were in a narrow room near the prow, awake. Eleven small faces turned toward the boy in the doorway.

None of them moved. None of them made a sound. Two were wearing their masks already. He hadn't seen them put on.

"Up," he said. "All of you. Now. Hold hands. Don't let go."

They stared at him. They had been taught not to move.

He went to the nearest masked child and worked his fingers under the rim, gently, the way you'd work out a splinter, saying anything, *you've got a name, you've got a name*, and it came away with a soft sound like a lip unsticking. Under it was a girl's face: blank, then puzzled, then all at once crumpling.

She cried.

Zayn said, in the caves, that it was the most beautiful sound he ever heard.

Tobin moved first after that. He got up and took the smallest girl's hand. Then the next child. Then the next. The second mask came off easier.

That's when the shouting started.

Running feet. Grey lanterns swinging. Masks poured toward the prow from both ends, humming louder and louder. Zayn backed the children into the corner with half a sword and no way out, and the light in his chest flickered grey.

And the ship moved.

---

That was me.

Three nights under the hull with Malek's voice in my ears. When I felt the light go gold above me, I knew.

I let it out.

It came up through the burn like smoke through a crack, and all the voices came with it, his the loudest, glad, *there you are, there you are*. A shadow-beast with ember eyes and one silver eye burning cold, my back to the belly of a dead ship, my mouth full of teeth I didn't remember growing.

And I *lifted*.

The ship groaned like the whole desert sighing. Timbers that hadn't moved in a thousand years tore loose. Masks went tumbling down the deck. Grey lanterns smashed, and their light ran out over the boards like spilled milk and died. The hull split along its belly with a crack like thunder, and the night came pouring in.

I held it. Sand and wood and fifty masked men and all those scraped-out names, on my back.

Through the gap in the broken hull, Zayn looked down at me.

He'd seen this shape once before, and burned it.

He wasn't afraid now. I saw his face. I'll never forget his face.

"*Kiri,*" he said.

That was all. My name. Not *monster*. My name.

And all the voices inside me went quiet. Every one. Even his.

"*Hold it,*" said Zayn.

"*What do you think I'm doing?*" I said. My voice came out like a rockslide.

He laughed. In the middle of all that, he laughed. Then he was through the gap with eleven children holding hands behind him, sliding down the sand under the dark arch of me, one after another, Tobin last, the smallest girl on Zayn's back, crying, still crying, all the way down.

When the last bare foot touched the sand outside, I let go.

The ship fell back into its dune with a roar of splitting timber, and the sand closed over it like water over a stone.

The humming went on a little while, under the sand. Then it stopped.

---

I don't remember shrinking. I remember lying in the sand, small again, a fox again, with my side on fire and my legs not working.

I remember hands. His hands, picking me up. Holding me against his chest, right over the mark, where it was warm.

"I've got you," he said.

I had something in my mouth. I'd snatched it from the sand where I'd hidden it. I let it drop into his palm.

A small bronze bell, no bigger than a thumb.

"You dropped this," I said.

He looked at it. He looked at me. And behind us, over the ridge, came the sound I'd been dreading: claws on sand, the howl of Hounds, the drumming of lizards, and under it all, the chant. No words. A hundred voices, none of them breathing.

Zayn tied the bell to his belt. He tucked me into the front of his shirt. He took Tobin's hand.

Behind him, eleven children were holding on to one another in the dark. Some were crying. Some weren't. Two still hummed, under their breath, and didn't know they were doing it.

And he ran.

---

*End of Chapter Fifteen*
