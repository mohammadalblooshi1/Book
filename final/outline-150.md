# *Never Say Your Name to the Wind*: The 150-Chapter Outline

*They say it truly happened.*

This outline follows the architecture file exactly: ten parts of 15 chapters, with every twist landing in the part the architecture gives it. It also draws on the final direction, the author's master checklist (including Round 3), the creature-names rule, the earlier specs (additions, folklore and direction), and the book study, Parts 1, 2 and 4. The strongest scenes of the 20-chapter base edition are reused and marked *(base Ch N)*. (File names are left out here so the creature-names rule holds in this file too.)

## How to read this outline
- **Title:** a warning. All 150 titles are different. The 20 titles from `final-direction.md` sit at the matching story points; one of them is renamed under the creature-names rule (see "Reconciliations" at the end).
- **Epigraph:** an original saying from a Bedouin, a grandmother or a guide. No religious text is quoted.
- **Beats:** 4–7 per chapter. **Hook:** the wound or hook that closes the chapter.
- **Tags:** [TALE] a tale within the tale · [FIRE] a frame scene (italic, 300–700 words) · [TWIST-Tn seed/reveal] · [UNREAL] an unreliable-reality device · [ACTION] a chapter driven by action.
- **The narrator** is Ghareeb, a saluki, speaking to "little one". When a chapter is rebuilt from Theyab's own later account, the beats say so.
- **The hidden message** (kindness returns, and so do cruelty and greed) is never stated. Beats mark a kindness with ⟲ when it is done and ⟲⟲ when it comes back. These marks are for the writers only.

## The creature-names rule (applies to every line of the book)
No old folklore or classical creature name, and no word for the race itself, ever appears. Only the new names from `creature-names.md` are used: **the Smokeless** (the race); **the Sealed King** (the great one under Ubar); the four kinds: **Burners** (fire), **Whirlers** (wind, the great one being **the Black Wind**), **Drowners** (sea) and **the Hoofed** (land); the four ranks Hilal reads from a worn copy of al-Jahiz's *al-Hayawan*: **Dwellers** (who live in houses with people), **the Turned** (gone wicked), **Breakers** (who carry buildings) and **Burners** (the strongest); **Ember Hounds**; **the Lady of the Crossroads**; **the Deep Caller**; **the Noon Mare**; **the Lantern Man**; **the Cradle Mother**; **the Shimmer**; **the Halfman**; **half-people**; **the Bodiless Voice**; **the Twin**; **the Sitter**; **the Worn**; **the Marked**; **smoke-touched**; **the Grey**; **the Nameless**. In Ch 147, Ghareeb says once: "the old people had other names for them, names I won't put in your ears, little one."

## Before the story: the Quiet Country (author update)
When Sharood left baby Theyab in the sand, the **Sand-Mother** took him down into **the Quiet Country**, the hidden good part of the Underneath: sweet wells, ghaf roots like pillars, light with no sun. The good ones raised him there for about seven years: the Sand-Mother, the Lifter, the Quiet Voice, the Turning Wind and the Threshold-Watcher, **with Ghareeb at his side the whole time**. At about seven he lost the path by mistake, following a gazelle up through a noon door. The door closed, and he woke crying in Hilat al-Sarab's noon lane: his "first memory". His memories of below are fragments, dreams, smells of milk and sweet water, and sleep-talk in a tongue no one knows. Neither he nor the reader can be sure they were real until the Sand-Mother returns (Ch 84) and Sharood confirms it (Ch 92). The path back is the great longing of his life.

**The second secret:** Sharood was Worn by the Smiling Hunger for a season of his flight, when Theyab was conceived. Sorcerers whisper that Theyab is "a son of the ones from below". (The creature-names rule bans the older word; this phrase stands in for it.) Marked *and* below-fathered, his blood is twice precious.

**The Diggers**, treasure sorcerers led by **Haddaj al-Hafar** (clay under his nails, a book of buried places), bargain with Old Thorn and want Theyab's blood spilled as a sacrifice to the ones from below, for treasure and for revenge. They believe the boy raised below stole from them, and that the Grey came because he returned. They are rivals to the Nameless. Ghanim sells them the palm secret (Ch 2, 4). They attack in Ch 4 (Part I), Ch 30 (Part II), Ch 50–53 (Part IV, Bahla) and Ch 116 (Part VIII, Ubar's tombs), where Old Thorn takes Haddaj into the gold.

## The Underneath
Every creature is one of **the Smokeless**, the **ones from below**, and comes up from **the Underneath**: a world of heat with no light under the sand, smelling of burnt hair and old frankincense, with voices that use your name and endless rows of the emptied walking without going anywhere. The Smokeless rise through **doors**: wells, sinkholes, caves, graves, ruins, lone trees, the salt crust, the heat-shimmer of noon, and dusk. **Ubar's sinkhole is the great door.** The Sealed King lies in the brass vessel at the top of the Underneath, beneath Ubar's well, and as its seal weakens every door widens, which is why the Grey spreads. **Ghareeb's silver eye looks into the Underneath.** The book never uses the old words for these creatures or for the place below; only the names in this outline. The Underneath is shown only in glimpses, tagged ⟨U⟩, in Ch 7, 11, 21, 22, 26, 45, 49, 62, 77, 78, 99, 116, 124, 134, 142 and 149.

## Good and evil from below
The Smokeless are **good and evil**. **The evil ones want control**, and they get it three ways: by possession (the Worn), by the Sitter (crushing, stealing breath, planting false dreams), and by stealing (names, memories, children, breath, luck, water, treasure). Some evil ones pretend to be good. **The good ones defend people**: Dwellers who guard good houses (Umm Hanan's keeps her lamp lit), good Drowners who push drowning divers up, good Whirlers who turn a storm aside, well-keepers who keep water sweet, and a good Bodiless Voice that Theyab **cannot tell from the Twin**. **Iron, the hirz and Hilal's recitation drive back good and evil alike**, so Theyab must sometimes take off the hirz to let help in (Ch 22, 86, 96, 129). **No good one can defeat the Sealed King**; only the Light freely given can. On the Night of Lamps, good and evil from below fight their own war alongside the human battle.

**The Sand-Mother** (new character, after the Sayf epic's nursing Smokeless) is a good one from below who nursed the abandoned baby Theyab in the sand, then **raised him in the Quiet Country** until he was seven, and is bound to him by that milk. Her return is a reunion. Her enemy is Old Thorn, who wants Theyab's Marked blood. She gives no name. She appears as a gold-eyed gazelle, as a girl at the edge of firelight whose feet don't touch the sand, or only as warmth and a smell of milk. She is seeded in Ch 1, 2, 13, 17 and 22; she defends Theyab in Ch 21, 37, 49, 69, 84 and 86; she is revealed in Ch 92; she is unmade at the sinkhole in Ch 129; and Theyab smells milk once more in Ch 143. The kindness that returns: as a boy, Theyab saved a pale snake from Ghanim (Ch 2). It was her.

**The named good ones** never take a person over; they only protect, warn, carry and fight. That rule is how readers learn to tell them from the evil ones. Most of them help because someone was kind to them first.
- **The Sand-Mother** (land; set against Old Thorn). Above. She is unmade in Ch 129.
- **The Lamp-Keeper** is Umm Hanan's house-Dweller, there because she always named the Creator before pouring hot water. It keeps her lamp lit against any wind; Ash-Mouth can't eat names in a house it guards (Ch 5, 6, 19, 145). It survives.
- **The Lifter** is the good one of all water, wells and sea alike, set against the Deep Caller and the Smiling Hunger (which poisons wells to make the Worn). It keeps wells sweet, hides water from the Grey, leads the thirsty to it, and lifts drowning divers to the air: the reason a few ever came home. It lives in the well of Bir al-Ghina'. It is kind because Jasir's mother left fresh water at the sea's edge every night, and because Theyab poured the first cup back (Ch 33, 44–45, 80, 84, 106, 128, 139). Wounded under the salt, it survives, and carries Jasir up at the end.
- **The Turning Wind** is a good Whirler, set against the Black Wind. It turns storms from caravans and children and carries warnings, never names (Ch 37, 86, 89). It is torn apart turning the Black Wind away from the freed children (Ch 89).
- **The Quiet Voice** is the good Bodiless Voice, set against the Thousand-Voiced and the Twin. It whispers "Not that way" and "Wake up", and **Theyab can't tell it from the Twin** (Ch 22, 26, 30, 49, 62, 100). It is revealed in Ch 147 as the voice that said "Wake up" every time the Sitter came, and it says it one last time at the cradle (Ch 150).
- **The Threshold-Watcher** is a Dweller of doorways and cradles, set against the Heavy One and the Cradle Mother. It is Old Bakhit's unseen guest, poured a second glass of tea every night for three hundred years (Ch 4, 14, 146). After Bakhit dies it goes to Hessa's tent (Ch 148–150): old, and growing thin.
On the Night of Lamps the good ones of Ubar's houses and wells rise to fight the evil ones pouring from the sinkhole (Ch 123–124, 129). Some are unmade, and the reader should grieve them.

**The Sealed King's five lieutenants** (named Turned; original names only):
- **Ash-Mouth** eats names and is the source of the Grey's forgetting (Ch 5, 24, 79, 123). It is burned in the Light (Ch 143).
- **The Smiling Hunger** makes the Worn and smiles with stolen faces (Ch 7–8, 34, 39, 65, 78, 122). It rides Saqr. Saqr drives it out, and it burns on Tal Moreeb (Ch 140, 143).
- **The Thousand-Voiced** speaks in the voices of the dead and is the Twin's teacher (Ch 6, 26, 38, 63, 77, 141). **It survives.** Its child's voice is heard at the fire (Ch 150).
- **The Heavy One**, master of the Sitters (Ch 22, 49, 62, 100). **It survives** and comes for the newborn (Ch 149).
- **Old Thorn** guards sealed places and stolen treasure and demands Marked blood; the Nameless bargain with it (Ch 53, 82, 116). It is drowned by the Younger Sister (Ch 131).

## Standing devices
- **Nadim the reciter** (from *Sirat Sayf ibn Dhi Yazan*). Every fire scene opens with his fixed formula, *"The teller said…"*, followed by *"Hold your breath, little ones,"* before each horror. Every night closes with *"…and what he found there, I'll tell tomorrow, if the wind lets me."* When he falters (Ch 69 in Part V and Ch 98 in Part VII), **he skips the formula, and the children notice.**
- **Objects that hold the Smokeless** (from the Sayf epic). Whoever holds the object, the Smokeless in it must obey **in letter, never in spirit**. There are three: (1) a Dweller bound in Old Bakhit's brass truth bell, which is why it rings at lies; (2) the Younger Sister of Bahla, whose own sister is bound by a sorcerer's ring; (3) **the carved tablet from Ghidd al-Zujaj that commands the Sealed King.** The Nameless pass the tablet down, Wazir Muhaisen holds it, and **Saqr steals it** (Ch 130). **Ghareeb's silver eye is the Sealed King's *other* window**, the one no tablet can close.
- **A Smokeless princess ally:** the Younger Sister of Bahla, who dug its falaj in one night. She never gives her name (Ch 50–53, 131, 139).
- **Written against unwritten.** Hilal trusts only the Smokeless that are in his books. Umm Shamma, the divers and the grandmothers fear the Lady of the Crossroads and the Deep Caller, "who are in no book". **The unwritten ones are the real ones here.** Hilal dies to the Lady of the Crossroads, a thing his books never named (Ch 126).
- **"One blow. Never two."** It is Old Bakhit's rule for the Hoofed and a line that runs through the book (Ch 17, 20, 45, 125, 150). The hooved boy at the fire is one of the Hoofed, who can change everything except their feet.
- **The Bodiless Voice is the Twin's first voice** (Ch 26, 30). Hilal gives both of the old books' explanations: it is real (al-Jahiz), or it is loneliness (al-Mas'udi). The book never settles which (Ch 96).
- **The two major recurring unwritten horrors:**
  - **The Lady of the Crossroads:** Ch 29, 60, 65, 68, 69 [TALE], 112, 115, 126, 127, 130, 139 and 150.
  - **The Deep Caller:** Ch 3, 6, 28, 32–33 [TALE], 77, 80 [TALE], 84, 117, 128 and 139.
  - Also present: the Noon Mare (Ch 1, 3 [TALE], 11, 109), the Lantern Man (Ch 21, 104) and the Cradle Mother (Ch 148–149).

# PART I: THE MIRAGE VILLAGE (Chapters 1–15)

### 1. Never Walk at Noon
*"Come in from the sun, or the Noon Mare will take you." — what the mothers of Hilat al-Sarab call at noon*
- [FIRE] The italic prologue, at a winter camp in Liwa (*base Ch 1*). It opens with the formula, "The teller said… Hold your breath, little ones." The old man stammers, and spills the coffee he insists on pouring himself. "You'll say it's a tale. I tell you it happened. I was there." An ancient saluki lies at his feet: one eye amber, the other lid sealed by old scar, and the children swear something under it catches the light. He will tell it "the way the dog would tell it." There are eleven children.
- The flash-forward: a boy burning on Tal Moreeb under the full moon, smelling of honey, hot metal and hair. "But that is the end."
- **Before the beginning: the Quiet Country.** In broken glimpses, Ghareeb gives the hidden good part of the Underneath: sweet wells, ghaf roots like pillars, light with no sun, a gold-eyed gazelle-woman who smells of milk, a voice that whispers "wake up" at every dawn, and a small boy raised there by the good ones, with an old saluki who never left his side. Ghareeb refuses to say how the boy got there. [UNREAL]
- **The loss of the path.** At about seven, the boy followed a gazelle, or a sound, too far up one of the doors: up through the noon heat-shimmer. The door closed behind him. He woke alone, crying, in the white-hot noon lane of Hilat al-Sarab, with a dog watching him. That is Theyab's "first memory". Mothers were calling their children in by the Noon Mare's name. No one called him.
- Eight years later. The village has kept him as Laqeet, the foundling, and he is still the only child out at noon, scavenging the half-buried ruins. A gazelle with gold eyes watches from a ruin wall, where no gazelle should be. Ghareeb looks away politely, like a son who has seen his mother in the market. [Sand-Mother seed]
- He finds a Maria Theresa thaler. He keeps his left fist closed, as always, over the line across his palm. Ghareeb's silver eye aches, as it always does when Theyab is in danger.
- **Hook:** In the noon-empty lane, round cups in the sand, two by two (hoofprints), circle the hollow where Theyab sleeps and stop at his blanket. He dreams that night of a well of sweet water, and wakes saying words in a language no one in the village knows.

### 2. Never Sell What the Sand Gave You
*"A trader's scale has two pans, and his thumb is the third." — a saying of the Liwa market*
- Ghanim the trader weighs Theyab's scraps on crooked scales, pays half, and pockets the thaler "for safekeeping". When Theyab opens his fist for the coins, Ghanim sees the unbroken line across the palm. He says nothing, and smiles.
- In the ruins, Ghanim corners a pale snake with a stick. Theyab stands between them and warns the snake three times to leave, as the old people do, until it goes ⟲. (It is the Sand-Mother.)
- Umm Hanan's secret kindness ⟲: bread and honey-dates left on her wall. She lost her husband and two sons when the pearling boats came home without them, and she kneads their three names into her dough every morning.
- Nadim, Saqi the well-keeper's son, bullies Theyab at the well. Shibl, seven, is fascinated by the dog with the coin-coloured eye.
- Theyab cannot lie. Nadim makes a sport of it, and every true answer is a humiliation.
- Alone on the highest dune, Theyab whispers the name he gave himself: *Theyab*, "wolves".
- **Hook:** The wind says it back. On the horizon a flat grey line rises and the stars above it go out. Ghareeb scratches *twelve* into the sand and lies down on the marks.

### 3. Don't Let the Old Women Frighten the Children
*"The noon has teeth, and the teeth have hooves." — the grandmothers of Liwa*
- [TALE: The Noon Mare] Umm Hanan tells it to Shibl and two other children to keep them in at noon. Theyab listens through the palm-frond wall. It is her own story: the daughter called Hanan, whom she let play outside one noon forty years ago. She found small hoofprints and one sandal, warm as bread.
- The tale's rules are its horror. The Mare speaks in your mother's voice; she takes only the children no one is calling; she never takes them far, so you can hear them for days.
- Theyab understands it is about him. He is the child no one calls.
- Umm Hanan's sea-grief: her men were "called off the boat by a voice from the water". This is the first glance at the Deep Caller, a thing "in no book".
- **Hook:** At the next noon Shibl is missing. Theyab finds him in the empty lane, walking toward a woman in the heat-shimmer whose legs are wrong. Theyab carries him home. No one believes how.

### 4. Never Tell the Wind Who You Are
*"Say your name to the wind and the wind will find someone to sell it to." — a herders' saying*
- Before dawn, Theyab hammers on Sheikh Mazyad's door (*base Ch 2*). The answer is "Sea fog. Dust." Ghareeb smells fear, the lie's older brother.
- Old Bakhit, the blind healer at the western edge, calls him into his tent of herbs and bells. Two glasses of tea are poured: one for Theyab, one by the threshold "for the guest" (the Threshold-Watcher). The brass bell rings once when Theyab says, "I'm not afraid." Bakhit names the four things never to do: walk at noon, answer the Grey, follow a light, say your name to the wind. "Which have you done?"
- Ghanim has sold what he saw. That afternoon a stranger rides into the market: **Haddaj al-Hafar**, "the Digger", an old Bedouin sorcerer with clay under his nails and a book of buried places. He buys dates from Ghanim and asks about the foundling's hands.
- [ACTION] **The first kidnapping attempt.** At night the Diggers, Haddaj's brotherhood of treasure sorcerers, come into the village with sacks and a pot of clay. They gag Theyab in his tent. Ghareeb fights them in the dark lanes. Umm Hanan's Lamp-Keeper wakes her, and she comes out with her lamp held high and screams the village awake ⟲⟲.
- The villagers see strangers carrying off Laqeet and hesitate a long, ugly moment before they pick up sticks. The Diggers flee. Haddaj, going, spits a phrase the village repeats for weeks: the boy is "a son of the ones from below", and "his blood is owed to us".
- Nobody asks what that means. Everybody remembers it.
- **Hook:** Walking home, Theyab hears his name in the hiss of the dunes, in his own voice, one step behind him. Ghareeb's hackles rise at nothing.

### 5. Never Forget the Names You Knead Into Bread
*"What you say every morning is a wall. Stop saying it and see what comes in." — Umm Hanan*
- The Grey comes the way cold comes into a tent (*base Ch 2*). Umm Hanan's indigo door goes ash-coloured. Kneading, she says two names. For the third: "——".
- The blank device begins here: the blank is printed in the text, and Ghareeb tries and fails to say the name.
- The eastern lanes lose colour, then sound, then smell. A goat bleats without a sound.
- The village argues whether the strange dog is smoke-touched (as in *Frankenstein in Baghdad*). Ghanim says to sell it to the coast. It is never settled.
- Ash-Mouth, the first of the Sealed King's lieutenants, is feeding. Where it eats, people forget each other. The Dwellers of Hilat al-Sarab, the eerie house-residents nobody speaks of, knock things off shelves at night: they are frightened too. Umm Hanan's Dweller, the Lamp-Keeper, is different. She has always named the Creator before pouring hot water on the ground and never scalded the unseen, so her lamp burns steady while every other lamp gutters ⟲⟲, and her kneaded names hold one day longer than anyone else's.
- **Hook:** At dusk the first emptied villager stands at the well, smiling, repeating *"Bring the bucket, the bucket."* Then the woman beside him starts saying it too.

### 6. Never Answer When the Grey Calls
*"If the dark calls you by name, it learned it from someone you trusted." — a grandmother's warning*
- At night the Grey calls from the eastern lanes in the voices of the dead and the absent (the Thousand-Voiced, a lieutenant, is speaking), including the drowned voices of Umm Hanan's sons, as if the Deep Caller had lent them inland.
- Umm Hanan steps toward her younger son's voice. Theyab holds her back with both arms. She strikes him, then lays her hand flat on his cheek.
- A herdsman answers his dead wife and comes back emptied. Now the Grey speaks in his voice too: a monster made of the community.
- Ghareeb walks into the Grey on purpose to draw it off, and walks out again. The whole village sees. It makes everything worse.
- The first list of the emptied's last words: eleven lines in Ghareeb's flat voice.
- **Hook:** Saqi the well-keeper lowers a bucket after dark without naming the Creator. He hums to the water, and the water hums back.

### 7. Never Lower a Bucket After Dark
*"Name the Maker before you pour, or something under the ground will thank you for the drink." — the women at the well*
- A Dweller of the well, gone Turned with the Grey, comes up the rope and into Saqi.
- It smiles with Saqi's face: the Smiling Hunger, the lieutenant that puts people on. Saqi turns gentle and strange, kind to his sons for the first time in years. Nadim loves this version of his father. That is the horror.
- Shibl, at the wall: "Baba talks to the water. It talks back with Mama's voice." Their mother is dead.
- Ghareeb smells smokeless fire and wet stone on Saqi. His silver eye throbs, and for a breath it shows him down the well-shaft: not water at the bottom but a dark that goes on, heat with no light, and rows of figures walking without going anywhere. ⟨U⟩ The well is a door.
- **Hook:** At midnight Saqi stands over Shibl's mat, eyes rolled white, and says in a doubled voice: "This one is light enough to carry."

### 8. Never Look the Worn in the Eyes
*"The one who loves you most is the best door for the Smokeless." — a saying of the oasis women*
- [ACTION] Saqi is Worn and attacks his own family with impossible strength. He throws Nadim through a frond wall and drags Shibl toward the well.

- The village arrives with torches and sees Theyab glowing over a broken man. Saqi, still Worn, speaks in Theyab's own voice: "He did this to me."
- **Hook:** At dawn a thin old man with a stick and a goat-hair cloak walks in from the east, already reciting under his breath.

### 9. Always Let the Mutawwa' In
*"A guest who comes reading is better than a son who comes armed." — a saying of the majlis*
- Mutawwa' Hilal (about sixty, sun-blackened, dry-humoured) reads over Saqi in the sheikh's yard. The recitation is described only: breath, rhythm, the arch of the Worn man's back.
- The thing argues through Saqi. It names Mazyad's debts and Ghanim's thefts. Then it names "the boy with the line in his hand." Hilal goes still.
- It leaves with a sound like water on coals. Saqi weeps, remembering everything. Nadim can't look at him.
- Hilal sees Theyab's light, and is the one adult who doesn't say "monster". He ties his first **hirz** on Theyab's neck: a silver case on a cord that **burns hot when the Smokeless are near**. He also hangs a **blue bead** on Shibl.
- **Hook:** Hilal asks to see Theyab's left hand. Theyab hides it. That night the Grey leans toward the village exactly where Ghareeb is looking.

### 10. Never Name What You Haven't Met
*"Four winds, four waters, four ranks of the hidden, and none of them your friend." — Mutawwa' Hilal*
- Hilal stays the three days of guest-right. Under the ghaf tree he teaches from his worn copy of al-Jahiz's *al-Hayawan*: the **four kinds** (Burners of fire, Whirlers of wind, Drowners of the sea, the Hoofed of the land), each with its powers and weaknesses, and the **four ranks** (Dwellers, the Turned, Breakers, Burners). "Each rank comes in every kind."
- He explains the Worn ("he is still in there") and the Twin, the companion born with every person: "Most sleep. Pray yours does."
- The protections, shown in use: iron under the head, read-over water, salt across thresholds, naming the Creator before pouring or entering, a blue bead, the hirz. Hilal reads over the well, and colour returns to the well-court for a day ⟲.
- Theyab, half joking, asks about the voice from the water that took Umm Hanan's men. Hilal frowns: "That one is in no book. Then it isn't real." Ghareeb's hackles stay flat. Hilal believes it.
- **Hook:** Hilal leaves for a sick child in Liwa: "When they call you monster, remember who said it first." That night Shibl's mat is empty again.

### 11. Never Walk Into the Grey After a Child
*"The Grey keeps what it's given and counts what it keeps." — words worn into the lip of an old well*

### 12. Never Heal What the Village Fears
*"Beware the child who glows; the desert has plans for him, and the desert is always hungry." — the grandmothers of Liwa*
- Theyab heals Shibl ⟲. The Light shows Shibl's future: a grown man at a fire, telling a story. It shows Theyab his own death on a dune, burning, unable to scream.

- Nadim sees his brother come back from the glowing outcast's hands and screams the word: **"Monster."**
- It is the one word the whole village can agree on.
- **Hook:** The first stone is thrown by Saqi, the man Theyab saved two nights ago.

### 13. Never Throw the First Stone
*"The stone you throw comes home to your own roof; it only takes the long way." — a saying of the oasis*
- [ACTION] Stoning and exile past the last palms.
- Umm Hanan presses bread into his hands as he goes ⟲. It is still warm.
- His tent is torn down, ringed with salt, and marked with a soot handprint: *what was here is not of us* (*base Ch 4*). Ghanim takes his annas.
- Ghareeb steps over the salt ring and yawns: salt is mostly for show, for something like him.
- That night in the open, bleeding and with no iron, Theyab wakes warm, with a smell of milk in the cold air and a gazelle's cloven prints around him. Ghareeb knows whose they are. [Sand-Mother seed]
- Theyab digs the grey name-cloth from the wreck. It smells of smoke, and he has never noticed before.
- **Hook:** Two lights still burn in Hilat al-Sarab: Umm Hanan's lamp and Old Bakhit's tent. He walks toward the light. They always do.

### 14. Never Trust a Lamp That Was Waiting for You
*"The bell rings for the liar, and the liar is often you." — stamped round the rim of an old healer's bell*
- Bakhit's tent (*base Ch 4*): two glasses of tea already poured, paste and bitter herb for the wounds.

# Character arcs

1. **Theyab** goes from a foundling who lost the path home from the Quiet Country at seven, to the "monster" of Hilat al-Sarab, to a boy who learns to laugh and to read with Hazza'. He breaks when he learns his own hands held his friend (Ch 99–100). Offered every gift, including the way home, he chooses to give the Light *outward*, not into the fire's mouth: "I'm coming, Hazza'." (Ch 143)
2. **Ghareeb** is the loyal dog who half knows he is the enemy's window. He tells one lie (Ch 56), gives a false first telling (Ch 60), meets the truth (Ch 87) and tries to leave (Ch 88). He burns out his own eye (Ch 142), confesses (Ch 147) and guards the cradle. The window reopens on the last page.
3. **Hazza'** is a laughing exile who hid Suhail, taught a wolf his letters, and hid a letter in his sword to spare his friend one more weight. He died held by the Twin wearing that friend's body. Every kindness he did returns: Suhail, the hilt, the children's letters.
4. **Nadim** goes from bully, to the boy who screams "monster", to the man who betrays Theyab for his brother on the Lady of the Crossroads's road. He is forgiven with "not yet", raises the Light's next bearer, and becomes the old teller who falters, names himself, and warns too late (T8).
5. **Sharood and Saqr.** The Runner is twice ashamed: he ran from his fire, and he was Worn when his son was conceived. He watches as the Veiled Rider, begs, and lets go. The brother is Worn by the Smiling Hunger and spares Theyab again and again. He steals the tablet, drives the thing out of himself, and his first word in fifteen years is "Nahidh".
6. **Mutawwa' Hilal** is the decent man of books. He casts the Turned out of the Worn and can't touch the Sealed King. He dies to the Lady of the Crossroads, a thing in no book, while still reciting and carried by the children he saved.
7. **Jasir** is the half-of-below son of the Deep Caller. He was saved by Theyab's last water and guides him into Ubar. He refuses to become whole, trades his human voice to hold back the Drowners, and is carried up by the Lifter.
8. **Old Bakhit** is the Blind One, who refused and was bound to live three hundred years, pouring tea every night for the unseen guest. He walks to Tal Moreeb on Umm Hanan's arm, sees gold, and dies mid-sentence.
9. **Umm Hanan** is the widow who kneads her dead men's names into bread and leaves it on her wall. She names the Creator before pouring hot water, so the Lamp-Keeper guards her house. She refuses her drowned sons' voices on Tal Moreeb, and her lamp is the one that never goes out.
10. **Wazir Muhaisen (Mu'in, "helper")** chose forgetting when he was tested and made the whole desert pay for his grief. He wrote one of the holy lies, murdered Hazza' through the Twin's hands, and is destroyed by the Light he tried to steal.
11. **The good ones below:**

12. **The evil ones and the greedy:**
    - Ash-Mouth and the Smiling Hunger burn in the Light.

    - **The Thousand-Voiced and the Heavy One survive**, down below, waiting.

# Reconciliations made
1. **The creature-names rule against the given titles.** Final-direction title #3, "Never Look a … in the Eyes", named an old creature, so it becomes **"Never Look the Worn in the Eyes"** (Ch 8). The other 19 given titles are used unchanged: Ch 1, 6, 21, 26, 37, 45, 49, 54, 57, 60, 63, 69, 77, 92, 118, 127, 143 and 150, plus Ch 8 as renamed.
2. **When Hilal reveals the eye.** Final-direction puts it at the mountain chapters; the architecture says Part VI. The architecture wins: Hilal meets the fleeing children at the sabkha's edge (Ch 86–87), then goes on to Jebel Hafeet.
3. **When the Rider and the Seventh are unmasked.** The base edition does it at the mountain; the architecture says late Part V. The Rider's failed rescue (Ch 73–75) unmasks both, and Sharood's full story moves to Part VII (Ch 92).
4. **The frame dog's eye.** The base prologue gives the old dog a silver eye, but Ghareeb burns it out in Ch 142. At the fire the lid is scarred shut, and the "something under it" pays off as T10 on the last page.
5. **The bearers who did not burn.** The bible says "eleven who burned", but three of them refused (the Shepherd, the Blind One, the Runner). The eleven tales still stand. The graves in Ch 118 are six real, three cenotaphs and two empty, and the true count makes Theyab the tenth bearer, not the twelfth.
6. **T7 against the ending.** If every burning feeds the Sealed King, why does Theyab burn? Because he gives the Light outward, Ghareeb blinds the window that would draw it in, and the Grey still only goes *down* (the darker ending).
7. **Late author changes,** all applied:
