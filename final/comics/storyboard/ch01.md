# Chapter 1: Never Walk at Noon

## Slide 1 — The Old Man at the Fire
**Scene:** First night of winter at a camp in Liwa. A slow ghaf-wood fire throws a red circle on the sand; eleven children press close under a sky thick with stars, red Suhail low in the south. The old man pours coffee with shaking hands and spills it over his knuckles; at his feet lies an ancient saluki with one scarred-shut eye. Wide shot from behind the children, the fire in the centre.
**Characters:** The old man at the fire — male, very old, short white hair, white beard, brown bisht, pouring coffee from a brass dallah with shaking hands, face lit by a winter fire; at his feet an ancient saluki whose left eyelid is scarred shut (feathering on its ears gone white as salt, one amber eye). Eleven Bedouin children — boys with short hair and bare heads in small kanduras, girls in long dresses with shaylas covering their hair.
**Text:**
- Caption: "The teller said… that there was a boy in the sands of Liwa who had no name, and a dog who had too many."
- Old man: "I'll tell it the way the dog would tell it. The dog saw all of it."
- Old man: "And the dog forgives less than I do."
**Mood:** Hushed, firelit, ominous, intimate.

## Slide 2 — The End First: The Boy Who Burns
**Scene:** A flash-forward. The full moon hangs swollen over Tal Moreeb, the great dune of Liwa. On its crest a boy stands with arms open as golden light splits him from the chest outward, running along his arms and throat like gold thread, pouring from his eyes. Low angle from the slope below, the boy tiny and blazing against the moon.

**Text:**

- Caption (Ghareeb): "He doesn't scream. I used to think that was courage."

## Slide 3 — I Am Ghareeb
**Scene:** Back to the beginning. A portrait of the narrator: the saluki stands alone on a ridge of sand in soft dusk light, one eye amber, one glowing silver, his shadow stretched a little too long behind him. Close-up three-quarter view at dog's eye level.
**Characters:** Ghareeb — an old Arabian saluki hound (a real dog breed, not a fox): very slim and long-legged, deep narrow chest, long narrow head, long silky feathered ears, feathered curved tail, sand-coloured coat. Left eye glowing silver, right eye amber. His shadow is a little too long.
**Text:**
- Caption: "They call me Ghareeb. The stranger. It's the one name of mine people ever got right."
- Caption: "I can't speak to you. I couldn't speak to him."
- Caption: "A lie smells sour, like milk on the turn. The truth smells of nothing."
**Mood:** Watchful, ancient, wry, uncanny.

A small boy of seven cups his hands near a pale stone basin and the clear water rises into his palms by itself; a gazelle with gold eyes watches nearby. Wide establishing shot.
**Characters:** Theyab as a child — male, 7, small and thin, brown skin, thick black eyebrows, short black hair, light amber-gold eyes, plain small sand-coloured kandura, a faint glowing golden feather mark on his chest. Ghareeb — old Arabian saluki hound, very slim and long-legged, deep narrow chest, long narrow head, long silky feathered ears, feathered curved tail, sand-coloured coat, left eye glowing silver, right eye amber. The Sand-Guide — a gazelle with glowing gold eyes.
**Text:**
- Caption: "There was a well where the water was sweet. When he was thirsty, the water rose."
- Caption: "Down there, nobody had a name, and nobody needed one, and nothing ate them."
- Caption: "Think of a child with nothing for the wind to carry."

Low angle looking up into the blinding white.

**Text:**

- Voices from above (caption, faint lettering): "Come in! Come in from the sun, or the Noon Mare will take you!"
- Caption: "He went up. I was right behind him. Then — it closed."

## Slide 6 — The Child No One Called
**Scene:** Noon in Hilat al-Sarab: a narrow lane of blinding white mud-brick walls, gravel, shuttered doors, a strip of shade too thin to use, the air at the far end shaking. The seven-year-old lies on his back screaming, wrapped in a strange grey cloth, the saluki standing over him with hackles raised. Halfway down the lane one door is open a hand's width and a woman's covered face looks out. High angle from above the lane.

Ghareeb — old Arabian saluki hound, very slim and long-legged, deep narrow chest, long narrow head, long silky feathered ears, feathered curved tail, sand-coloured coat, left eye glowing silver, right eye amber, his shadow a little too long. A village woman — black abaya, shayla covering all her hair.

- Caption: "Not one of them was his. How could it be? He had none."

**Mood:** Abandoned, scorching, cold-hearted, piercing.
