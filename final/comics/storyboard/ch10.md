# Chapter 10: Never Name What You Haven't Met

## Slide 1 — Reading Into the Well
**Scene:** Early morning before the heat, the well-court. Women gather quietly in twos and threes with empty water-jars on their hips; the goat trough is cracked and white. Hilal sits on the lip of the well with his legs over the dark, leaning over the shaft, lips moving; the recitation shown only as cool blue ripples flowing down the shaft. Then he names the Maker over his clay bowl and pours it in. Ghareeb watches from the edge of the court, chin on paws.
**Characters:**
- Mutawwa' Hilal — Male, about 60, thin, very dark sun-blackened skin, short white hair, neat white beard, brown goat-hair cloak, wooden staff, leather satchel, a worn old book.
- Ghareeb — An old Arabian saluki hound (a real dog breed, not a fox): very slim and long-legged, deep narrow chest, long narrow head, long silky feathered ears, feathered curved tail, sand-coloured coat. Left eye glowing silver, right eye amber. His shadow is a little too long.
- Village women — fully covered in black abayas and black shaylas over all their hair, water-jars on their hips.
**Text:**
- Caption (Ghareeb): His voice went down the shaft and came back up changed, as if the well were reading with him.
- Caption (Ghareeb): And up the shaft came a breath of air. Cool, and wet, and very faintly sweet.
- Hilal: Draw. Name the Maker first. Every bucket. And the first splash goes back down the well.
**Mood:** Quiet, careful, hopeful.

## Slide 2 — The Day the Colour Came Back
**Scene:** Wide shot, sunlit. A walnut-faced grandmother draws the first bucket, names the Maker, pours the first splash back — and colour ripples outward from the well like a dropped stone: honey-coloured stones, bright old indigo trough, warm red-brown walls, green fodder. She sits on the lip with her jar in her lap and weeps; the other women weep behind their hands; one kisses Hilal's sleeve.
**Characters:**

- Village women — fully covered as Slide 1.
- Mutawwa' Hilal — as Slide 1.
**Text:**

- Caption (Ghareeb): It lasted a day. A day of colour, given for nothing.

## Slide 3 — Under the Ghaf Tree
**Scene:** Afternoon, the lone old ghaf tree on a sand rise at the southern edge. Hilal sits against the trunk and unwraps a fat battered leather book with pages sewn back in with goat-hair thread — the first book Theyab has ever seen. Theyab sits at the edge of the shade, Ghareeb beside him. Three children lie on their stomachs in the hot sand just outside the shade, pretending to watch a beetle — one is Shibl. At the far edge of the gardens, Nadim leans on a wall pretending to mend a rope.
**Characters:**
- Mutawwa' Hilal — as Slide 1, the book open on his knees.
- Theyab — Male, 15, Emirati Bedouin boy. Thin, brown sun-darkened skin, thick black eyebrows, short black hair, light amber-gold eyes. Ragged sand-coloured kandura. A faint glowing golden feather mark on his chest. Keeps his left fist closed. (Small dark silver hirz on a black cord at his neck.)
- Ghareeb — as Slide 1.
- Shibl — Male, 7, small, short black hair, huge dark eyes, a blue bead on a string at his neck.
- Nadim — Male, 15, broad-shouldered, short black hair, hard proud face, white kandura.
- Two other children — a small boy with short black hair and bare head, and a little girl in a long dress with her hair fully covered by a small headscarf.
**Text:**
- Hilal: *The Book of Animals.* Written in Basra by a very clever man with very bad teeth. He was wrong about the elephant. But he was right about more than you'd think.
**Mood:** Curious, warm, cautious.

## Slide 4 — Four Kinds
**Scene:** Close on Hilal holding up four fingers, with four illustrated vignettes drifting around him like pages come alive: a heat-shimmer in a noon hearth (Burners); a spinning dust-pillar walking across a gravel plain while camels shy (Whirlers); dark salt water at the bottom of an old well and the edge of a sabkha (Drowners); and a stranger at a night fire whose face is ordinary but whose feet, hidden under his robe hem, are a donkey's hooves (the Hoofed). Final small panel: Theyab gone still, remembering round hoof-cups in the noon sand circling a blanket.
**Characters:**
- Mutawwa' Hilal — as Slide 1.
- Theyab — as Slide 3.
- The Hoofed stranger (vignette) — a creature, not attractive: a stooped figure in a long dusty robe with a shadowed blank face, donkey hooves showing beneath the hem.
**Text:**
- Hilal: The Smokeless are made from fire without smoke. But fire goes into the world in four ways, and so do they.
- Hilal: Burners. Whirlers. Drowners. And the Hoofed — they can change into anything. Except the feet.
- Hilal: Look at his feet before you look at his face.
**Mood:** Eerie, instructive, unsettling.

## Slide 5 — Four Ranks, and None Your Friend
**Scene:** Under the ghaf. Hilal closes his hand slowly as if crushing something while he explains the Dwellers, the Turned, the Breakers and the highest Burners — a vignette of a gentle presence in the well breaking under a leaning grey weight; giant stones in the desert too big for men. A little girl cries quietly; Shibl pats her back, eyes on the book. Close on Ghareeb keeping his face very still, while a faint memory floats behind his eyes: a gazelle with glowing gold eyes in a ruined wall, giant ghaf roots like pillars.
**Characters:**

## Slide 6 — The Twin
**Scene:** Second day, under the ghaf. Close two-shot. Theyab asks whether Saqi was still inside; Hilal answers looking at the back of his own hand. Then Hilal turns to a crowded page near the back of the book, a torn page gummed back in, and speaks of the Twin. Theyab sits with left fist closed and right hand flat on the hirz. Visual echo: behind Theyab, his own shadow on the sand has turned its head toward him. Hilal looks at Theyab, then at Ghareeb.
**Characters:**
- Theyab — as Slide 3.
- Mutawwa' Hilal — as Slide 1.
- Ghareeb — as Slide 1.
- The Twin — voice only; shown as a speech-bubble shape with no speaker / a subtly wrong shadow.
**Text:**
- Theyab: Was Saqi still in there?
- Hilal: He's always still in there. That's the cruelty of it. You watch over its shoulder.
- Hilal: Every one of us is born with a twin. One of them. Most of them sleep. Pray yours does.
- Theyab: And if it doesn't?
- Hilal: Then one day you'll hear it. It'll talk to you in your own voice. And you'll think it's you.
**Mood:** Intimate, chilling, introspective.

## Slide 7 — Iron, Salt, Water, Words
**Scene:** Montage. Hilal sniffs Theyab's rusty khanjar, making the children giggle; the children now sit cross-legged like a little majlis. At the end of an eastern lane he pours a thin glittering line of salt across the threshold of the last house before the dead ones. Ghareeb stops before the salt line, nothing happens; he steps over and back and scratches his ear — and Hilal is watching him with small bright reading eyes. Last beat: Hilal touches the blue bead on Shibl's chest.
**Characters:**
- Mutawwa' Hilal — as Slide 1.
- Theyab — as Slide 3.
- Ghareeb — as Slide 1.
- Shibl — as Slide 3.
- Children — boys with short hair and bare heads; girls with hair fully covered.
**Text:**
- Hilal: Iron. Rust doesn't matter. Sleep with it under your head. Every night.
- Hilal: Salt across every doorway that faces the bad side. It won't stop the big ones. It'll make the big ones think.
- Hilal: If a bead breaks, it took a hard one. Tell your brother.
**Mood:** Practical, light, watchful.

## Slide 8 — "That One Is in No Book"
**Scene:** Two beats. Dusk: Hilal stands in the lane before Umm Hanan's open door, studying the steady lamp and the dark threshold; she kneads inside. Third morning under the ghaf, larger crowd, Nadim sitting at the edge of the shade with arms folded. Theyab asks about the voice that called Umm Hanan's men off the boat; Hilal's face closes, he lays his hand flat on the book, then ties it shut. Close-up of Ghareeb, nose in the air, hackles flat — and a faint ghostly image of drowned voices dripping seawater in the shadows behind him.
**Characters:**

- Theyab — as Slide 3.
- Nadim — as Slide 3.
- Ghareeb — as Slide 1.
**Text:**
- Hilal: Somebody in that house was kind to somebody, once. There's a wall round that house, and she builds it every morning with her own mouth.
- Theyab: What about the one that called Umm Hanan's men off the boat?
- Hilal: Divers' talk. That one is in no book. Then it isn't real.
- Caption (Ghareeb): My nose can smell a lie. It cannot smell a mistake.

## Slide 9 — "Mind Where You Look"
**Scene:** Late afternoon. A boy on a lathered camel calls the mutawwa' to a sick child two days away. Hilal fills a skin at the well, naming the Maker, pouring the first splash back. Then at the edge of the hollow; Theyab stands up for him without knowing why. Hilal gives his last advice and looks long at Ghareeb. Final wide: the camel a speck on the red dunes, the sun going down behind it, boy and dog watching.
**Characters:**
- Mutawwa' Hilal — as Slide 1.
- Theyab — as Slide 3.
- Ghareeb — as Slide 1.
- The rider — a boy of about 12, short black hair, bare head, dusty kandura, on a lathered camel.
**Text:**
- Hilal: Iron under your head. — Theyab: Every night.
- Hilal: They'll call you monster. When they do, remember who said it first.
- Theyab: It wasn't them. — Hilal: It never is, first. They only learn the word.
- Hilal: Look after him, old one. … And mind where you look.
**Mood:** Bittersweet, foreboding farewell.
