# Chapter 5: Never Forget the Names You Knead Into Bread

## Slide 1 — The Grey Door
**Scene:** Three mornings after the Diggers, before the doves. Umm Hanan steps out with her basin and lamp and turns to shut her door — and stops. The indigo her husband painted is gone; the door is the flat grey of old ash, grain and thumb-smudge intact, only the colour eaten. Through the gap at its foot her lamp still glows gold. Medium shot from the dark lane, the dog's point of view.
**Characters:** Umm Hanan — old woman, black abaya and black shayla covering all her hair, gold burqa face mask, kind tired eyes, wrinkled brown hands, flour on her fingers, holding a small oil lamp that burns steady. Ghareeb — an old Arabian saluki hound (a real dog breed, not a fox): very slim and long-legged, deep narrow chest, long narrow head, long silky feathered ears, feathered curved tail, sand-coloured coat, left eye glowing silver, right eye amber, his shadow a little too long. The Grey — a flat ash-coloured fog that drains all colour from whatever it touches.

- Caption: "Behind the door her lamp was burning. Gold. Steady. It hadn't changed at all."

## Slide 3 — "Did I Have Three?"

Over-the-shoulder shot past the two of them down the greying lane.
