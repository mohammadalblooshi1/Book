# *Never Say Your Name to the Wind*: 150-Chapter Architecture

**One long book.** 150 chapters of about 4,000 words each, around 600,000 words. Adult horror. The base is the 20-chapter Desert Jinn Edition (`../desert-jinn/chapters/`, with the folklore already added) plus everything in `final-direction.md` and `../desert-jinn/jinn-additions.md`.

The author's goal: **readers should cry, shake, and feel they are going mad.** The two engines are:
1. **Unreliable reality.** Readers can't trust what's real: the qareen, the jathoom, stolen memories and names, a narrator whose eye belongs to the enemy, and the elder whose telling falters.
2. **Mind-breaking twists.** Reveals that force readers to re-read earlier chapters and see them differently.

Reference books (see `../research/book-study.md`): *One Thousand and One Nights* (nested tales, the Fisherman, the City of Brass), *Frankenstein in Baghdad* (a monster made of the community, documentary evidence, rumour), *The Ocean at the End of the Lane* (an adult remembering, forgetting as horror), *Season of Migration to the North* (a story handed from witness to witness), *The Alchemist* (a circular journey, sayings), *The Road to Ubar* (real archaeology), *The Sand Fish* (pre-oil life), *A Small Death* (an ancient voice, life as stages), *Arabian Nights and Days* (jinn who test people, linked tales of one city), al-Koni (the animal soul, greed).

## The three layers of telling
1. **The fire (the frame):** old Nadim tells the tale to children over **many nights** at a winter camp in Liwa, decades later. Each part of the book begins or ends with a short italic fire scene (300–700 words). The children change: some stop coming, and a small boy who no one remembers arriving appears earlier and earlier at the edge of the light.
2. **Ghareeb's telling (the main narration):** the saluki's inner voice, addressed to "little one".
3. **The tales within the tale** (in the style of *One Thousand and One Nights*): stories told by characters inside the story, by Old Bakhit, Mutawwa' Hilal, Jasir, the blind diver, Umm Shamma, and the rock carvings themselves. They include **the Tales of the Eleven Bearers**, eleven children who burned before Theyab, each told as its own chapter, scattered through the book. Each is a small, complete horror story, and together they reveal the cycle.

## Rhythm rules
- **Almost every chapter ends on a hook or a wound.**
- About **one chapter in eight is a "tale within the tale"** (a bearer's tale, Ghadban, Bu Darya's origin, Umm al-Duwais, Bahla's jinn wall, Jasir's mother). Each must be a complete, frightening story.
- **The fire** returns at the start of each Part, and once in the middle of Parts V and VII (Nadim falters).
- **Unreliable-reality devices** are used throughout:
  - Theyab finds things he wrote but doesn't remember writing.
  - A scene repeats with one detail changed.
  - A character remembers something differently.
  - Names vanish from the text. Ghareeb tries to say a name and can't, and the narration shows a blank: "——".
  - The jathoom nights.
  - The qareen's voice.
  - Lists of the emptied's last words.
- **The emotional core:** love that is real before it is lost (Umm Hanan, Hazza', Hilal, Ghareeb and Theyab, Sharood and his sons).
- **Fixed lines:** last words "I'm coming, Hazza'."; Ghadban: "I remember my name."; Saqr's first word: "Nahidh"; Ghareeb's last line: "Wake up, little one."

## Author update: Theyab raised from below, lost back into the human world, and hunted
1. **Raised by the good ones.** When baby Theyab was left in the sand, the **Sand-Guide** took him **down into the Quiet Country**, the hidden part of the Underneath where the good ones live (wells of sweet water, ghaf roots like pillars, light with no sun). The good ones raised him there for years: the Sand-Guide, the Lifter, the Quiet Voice, the Turning Wind and the Threshold-Watcher. **Ghareeb was with him** the whole time.
2. **Lost back into the human world.** When he was about **seven**, he **lost the path by mistake**. He followed a gazelle, or a sound, too far up one of the doors, and the door closed. **Suddenly he was back in the human world**, lying alone in the sand at the edge of Hilat al-Sarab, crying, with a dog watching him. (This is his "first memory" from the earlier books. Now we know what came before it.) The village takes him in as **Laqeet**, the foundling.
3. **Memory of the Quiet Country fades and breaks** (unreliable reality). He remembers it only in pieces, in dreams, and in smells of milk and sweet water. Villagers say he talks in his sleep in a language no one knows. Readers, like Theyab, can't be sure it was real **until** the Sand-Guide appears. The path back is the great longing of his life, and the good ones can't simply fetch him: something sealed the door.
4. **"The demon's son."** The truth is that **Sharood was Worn** (possessed by **the Smiling Hunger**) for a season of his flight, including the time Theyab was conceived. Sharood fought it off later, but the rumour spread among sorcerers and the Nameless: *the boy's father was one of them.* So Theyab is **Marked** (the unbroken line across the palm) **and** "a demon's son". To sorcerers, that makes his blood doubly precious. Sharood's shame about this is a second secret behind the Part VII reveal.
5. **The treasure sorcerers: new hunters.** A brotherhood of **treasure sorcerers** (*the Diggers*), led by **Haddaj al-Hafar** ("Haddaj the Digger"), an old Bedouin sorcerer with clay under his nails and a book of buried places. They bargain with **Old Thorn**, who guards stolen treasure under the sand. Their price for the treasure is **the Marked child's blood, spilled as a sacrifice to the ones from below**. They want revenge, too: they believe the demon's son, raised below, stole from them, and that the Grey came because he returned.
   - **Ghanim**, the cheating trader, **sells Theyab's secret** (the palm line, which he saw when he cheated him) to the Diggers in Part I. This is how greed leads to his ruin later (a hidden-message return).
   - The Diggers hunt Theyab **separately from the Nameless**. They are rivals, and sometimes they fight over him. **The first kidnapping attempt** comes in Part I (the village at night), and further attempts follow at intervals (Parts II, IV, VIII). In Part VIII the Diggers try to sacrifice him in Ubar's tombs before the Nameless can.
   - Haddaj's fate: he opens a sealed treasure place with stolen blood (not Theyab's) and Old Thorn takes him **into the gold**.
6. **What this means for the outline:**
   - **Part I** opens with glimpses of the Quiet Country, then the loss of the path. The rest of Part I is village life, Ghanim's betrayal of the secret, and the first Digger attack.
   - **The Sand-Guide** is no longer just a protector who visits. She **raised him**, and her return is a reunion.
   - **Ghareeb** has known Theyab since the Quiet Country.
