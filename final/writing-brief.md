# Writing Brief for *Never Say Your Name to the Wind* (150 chapters)

## Read before writing (in this order)
1. `final/outline-150.md`: the front sections (how to read it, the creature-names rule, the Quiet Country, the Underneath, good and evil, standing devices), **your chapters**, and the chapters just before and after yours.
2. `final/creature-names.md`: **the naming rule. Obey it absolutely.**
3. `final/architecture-150.md`: the layers of telling, the twists, and the author's update about the Quiet Country and the Diggers.
4. `final/final-direction.md` (new horror, techniques) and `final/jinn-checklist.md` (everything the author asked for).
5. `desert-jinn/direction.md`, sections 1–8: setting, real places, 1920s life, **people's names (section 8)** and the hidden-message table.
6. For voice only: one or two chapters of `desert-jinn/chapters/`. **They use old creature names. Never copy those names.**

## Absolute rules
- **Never write:** jinn, jinni, djinn, demon, devil, hell, ifrit, marid, shaytan, ghoul, qareen, jathoom, malboos, zahwi, Bu Darya, Umm al-Duwais, Hmarat al-Qayla, Abu Fanous, Umm al-Sibyan, shiqq, nasnas, hatif, zawba'a. Use only the horror names in `creature-names.md` (the Smokeless, the ones from below, the Underneath, the Sealed King, the Worn, the Marked, the Twin, the Sitter, the Hoofed, the Lady of the Crossroads, the Deep Caller, the Noon Mare, the Lantern Man, the Cradle Mother, the Halfman, the Bodiless Voice, Ember Hounds, the Black Wind, Whirlers, Drowners, Burners, Breakers, the Turned, Dwellers; the evil lieutenants Ash-Mouth, the Smiling Hunger, the Thousand-Voiced, the Heavy One and Old Thorn; the good ones, the Sand-Mother, the Lamp-Keeper, the Lifter, the Turning Wind, the Quiet Voice and the Threshold-Watcher).
- **People's names:** use only the names in `desert-jinn/direction.md` section 8, plus new ones from the outline (Mutawwa' Hilal, Haddaj al-Hafar and so on). The narrator is **Ghareeb**, a saluki.
- **No religious text is quoted.** The Creator is never named; people "name the Creator". The mutawwa''s recitation is described, never quoted.
- **The hidden message** (kindness returns, and so does cruelty) is never stated.
- **Adult horror.** Fear comes from atmosphere and dread. Violence can be strong but not gratuitous, and there is no sexual content.

## Voice and form
- **Ghareeb narrates** in the first person: sarcastic, sad and very old, speaking to "little one". He is a real saluki who cannot speak to humans. He senses lies (his hackles rise and he growls) and is smoke-touched, with a silver eye that sees the Underneath.
- **Fire scenes** (tagged [FIRE]) are written in *italics*, in the third person: old Nadim (unnamed until Ch 150, called only "the old man") at a winter fire in Liwa, decades later, with children listening. Each fire scene opens with his formula, *"The teller said… Hold your breath, little ones,"* and ends with *"…and what he found there, I'll tell tomorrow, if the wind lets me."* He drops the formula only where the outline says he falters.
- **[TALE] chapters** are told by a character in the old storytelling manner, framed briefly by Ghareeb.
- **[UNREAL] devices** come from the outline's "standing devices": blanked names shown as "——", a scene repeated with one detail changed, things Theyab wrote and doesn't remember, the Sitter nights, the Twin's voice.
- **Energy:** hard action, short sentences in fights, and every chapter ends on a hook or a wound.
- **Emotion:** make readers love the characters before they lose them. The goal is that readers cry, shake, and doubt their own minds.

## Format (each chapter is its own file)
- Path: `final/chapters/NNN-kebab-title.md`, with a 3-digit number (for example `001-never-walk-at-noon.md`).
- Contents:
```
# Chapter One
## Never Walk at Noon

> *Epigraph: an original Bedouin or grandmother's saying.*
> — attribution

---

(prose; `---` between scenes)

*End of Chapter One*
```
- Write chapter numbers as words in the heading ("Chapter One Hundred and Twelve").
- **Length: 4,000–5,000 words per chapter.**
- Follow your chapters' outline beats exactly. Other writers are writing the neighbouring chapters at the same time, so don't resolve or invent anything major that isn't in the outline.
