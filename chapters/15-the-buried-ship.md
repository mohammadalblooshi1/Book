# Chapter Fifteen
## The Buried Ship

> *A ship in the desert is a coffin waiting for the sea.*
> — a riddle the caravan children tell, which has no answer

---

I'm going to tell you this part the way he told it to me.

He told it once, in the mountain caves, by a fire that kept going out, in a flat, quiet voice I never heard him use again. Most of that time I was lying in the dark under a dead ship with sand in my mouth, listening.

But I heard everything.

---

It took me a night and a day to find it.

I followed the camels, and under the camel-smell, something cold and dry and empty, like a room where no one has spoken in a hundred years. The smell of grey blades.

I carried Hesset's bell in my teeth the whole way. Don't ask me why. It seemed important not to put it down.

At dusk on the second day, under an Ash thinned almost to a sliver, I came over a long ridge and saw it.

A ship.

There's no sea in the Destiny Desert. There never has been, not in all my centuries. And yet there it lay in the trough between two great dunes: a vast wooden hull, longer than the whole of Harrow's End, sunk on its side into the sand like a drowned animal. Its prow reared out of the dune, carved like some long-necked beast with its mouth open. Its ribs showed through the broken planks, pale as bones.

And inside it, through every crack and gap in the hull, lanterns burned.

Grey lanterns. Their light was the colour of ash.

I never found out what sea it had sailed before the sand swallowed it. Some mysteries the desert keeps.

Then I crept down the slope in the dark, and found a place where the sand had drifted up against the hull, and dug.

---

He woke up chained.

A great beam ran down the middle of the hold, black with age, and from an iron ring in it ran a grey chain to a cuff around each of his wrists. The chain wasn't iron. It was cold in a way iron isn't, and when the light in his chest tried to rise, the chain drank it.

The hold smelled of old salt, which made no sense, and of lamp smoke, which did. The curve of the hull rose around him like the inside of a ribcage.

And in the far corner, tossed in a heap like rubbish, were his bag and Soren's broken sword.

He stared at that sword for a long time.

---

They brought him up on the first night, for the burning.

The upper deck lay tilted under the sky, one edge running down into the sand like a ramp. The Unnamed stood in a great circle on it. Fifty of them, perhaps more, grey robes and smooth white masks, silent as Husks. In the middle stood an iron bowl, and in the bowl a fire burned.

The flames were grey.

To one side, in a line, stood the children.

There were eleven of them. The youngest was perhaps five. The oldest might have been twelve. They wore little grey robes, and their heads had been shaved, and they stood very straight and still, the way children stand when they've learned that moving gets you hurt. None of them wore masks. Not yet. The masks came later, when there was nothing left behind their faces to hide.

The Unnamed brought him into the circle and forced him to his knees.

Then three young men stepped forward from the ranks. They wore grey robes, but no masks. Each held a strip of white cloth, and on each strip, in charcoal, a word was written.

A name.

"*Names are chains,*" said the circle, all together, in one flat voice. "*Memory is pain. Forget, and be free.*"

One by one, the young men held their names over the bowl and let them fall.

The cloth caught, and curled, and was gone. Each young man, as his name burned, gave a small gasp, as though something had been pulled out of his chest on a string. Then their faces went slack and empty, and the brothers fitted three white masks over three blank faces, and that was all.

Three men had walked into the circle. Three nobodies walked out.

Zayn told me that was the worst thing he ever saw. Worse than the Veilbeast. Worse than the Husks. Because the Husks hadn't chosen it.

---

Then a figure stepped into the firelight, and the circle bowed.

It wore a grey robe and a white mask like all the others. But when it spoke, Zayn knew the voice before the first word was finished.

"Hello again, child."

The Voice. That's what they called him there. Not Orrin. Not vizier. Just *the Voice*, because a voice was all he had left after he burned his name.

He crouched in front of Zayn and held out a strip of white cloth and a stick of charcoal.

"Write it," he said.

Zayn looked at the cloth.

"You can, now, can't you?" said the Voice softly. "He taught you. The prince. I imagine your name was one of the first things he taught you. Write it, and give it to the fire, and all of this stops. The grief. The guilt. That walk under the full Ash, over and over, every time you close your eyes. You'll never have to remember it again."

The charcoal hung in front of Zayn's face.

He thought about Soren drawing letters in the sand with a stick. *Z. Like a lightning bolt. Then A, like a tent. Then Y, like a man waving for help.* Laughing. Always laughing.

Zayn spat on the cloth.

The circle was very quiet.

The Voice looked down at the wet spot for a moment. Then he folded the cloth carefully and tucked it into his robe.

"I have to go back to Veimore," he said, rising. "The king grows restless without me, and the Night of Embers is close. But I'll leave you in good hands." He turned to the circle. "Drain him slowly. Make him remember. When he is empty enough to forget his own name, he will give it to us himself. They always do."

He climbed onto a camel at the edge of the sand and rode off into the dark toward the red city. I'd like to tell you it was the last time we ever saw him. I can't.

---

They put him back on his chain in the hold, and the draining began.

I'm not going to tell you all of it. But somebody should know what he survived.

They came in pairs, every few hours. One held the lantern. The other held a grey blade.

They didn't cut him. A grey blade doesn't cut skin. It lies flat against you, cold as the bottom of a well, and it pulls. And the Light comes out of you in thin gold threads, the way a thread pulls out of a hem, and runs into the blade, and turns grey, and is gone.

And with each thread, a memory came.

Never a good one. Every time the cold metal touched his arm, Zayn was six years old again, running from a crowd of children with a stone in his back. Or he was kneeling with Tobin's small hand in his, and Bram was screaming *Monster!* and the first rock was already in the air.

Or he was on the dune under the full Ash.

Over and over. *Promise me you'll never give up.* And Zayn turning away. Walking. Not looking back. Again. And again. And every time, the last thing he saw was Soren's face the next morning. Grey. Cracked with gold.

Under the hull, with the sand pressing in around me and my burned side screaming, I heard every sound he made.

I didn't make any. I couldn't do anything. Not while he was chained to that beam with fifty masks around him. I had one chance, and I had to choose the moment exactly right.

That's what I told myself. It was even true.

---

On the second night, they let the Husks in.

Dozens of them were penned in the deep lower hold. The Unnamed didn't fight Husks. They *kept* them, the way you'd keep dogs. And Husks are drawn to light.

They came shuffling out of the dark and gathered around the boy on the chain, so close he could feel the cold coming off them. They didn't touch him. They just stared, the way they'd stared at the lamp in Bram's window.

And then, all at once, they spoke.

Every mouth. Together. One voice, coming out of thirty grey throats at the same moment, low and soft and terrible.

"*Little light.*"

Zayn went rigid on his chain.

He knew that voice. He'd heard it in a dream, in the Valley of Echoes, leaning over him in the dark.

"*Little nameless light,*" said the Husks. "*They threw stones at you. They called you monster. They drove you into the desert to die. And you'd burn for them? Why? No one remembers the bearers. Ask the fox. Ask him how many he's buried. Ask him if anyone sings their names.*"

Zayn shut his eyes.

"*Let it go,*" said thirty voices. "*Let me hold it for you. It's so heavy, little light. I know. I carried it too.*"

Then the voice changed. It went softer. Almost shy.

"*Hello, Kiri,*" it said. "*I know you're down there. You always did listen at doors.*"

Under the hull, in the dark, I stopped breathing.

I'm not going to tell you what his voice sounded like, all those centuries ago, when it was just a shepherd boy calling me in from the hills at dusk. That's mine.

I'll only tell you that I didn't answer. And that it was the hardest thing I have ever not done.

---

The children came at dawn, with water.

It was a lesson, I think. The Unnamed wanted them to see what happens to people who hold on.

One of them stopped in front of Zayn and stared at him.

He was seven. His head was shaved. His grey robe was too big for him. And his eyes were brown, clear brown, not yet faded at all.

"You're the monster," said Tobin.

Zayn looked at him. He'd last seen that face grey and empty in Harrow's End, coming slowly back to life under his hands.

"Bram said you weren't," Tobin whispered. "After. He said he was wrong." His lip shook. "Is Bram coming?"

Zayn opened his mouth. And closed it again.

He wanted to say *yes. I promise. I'll take you home.* It's what you say to a frightened child in the dark.

But Zayn couldn't lie, and he didn't know.

"I'll try," he said instead. It was all he had. "I'll try. I swear I will."

Tobin nodded, as if *I'll try* from someone who couldn't lie was worth more than *I promise* from anyone else.

Maybe it was.

---

That night, alone on the chain, Zayn reached for his name and couldn't find it.

The blades had been at him all day. He was so cold he couldn't feel his hands. And he thought, the way you think of something just to make sure it's still there, *My name is*—

And there was nothing.

A blank, smooth place, like the face of a mask. He could feel the shape of where his name should be. But when he reached for it, his hand closed on air. He tried to remember who had named him, and the village, and the patched tent, and they were going grey at the edges, like cloth left in the Fire.

*Write it,* the Voice had said. *They always do.*

His finger moved in the dust on the floor of the hold. Searching. He didn't know what for.

"That's not even a letter," said someone.

The hold was full of gold.

---

Soren was sitting on the beam above him, swinging his legs.

He looked the way he had on the dune. Light coming through the edges of him. His hair falling loose. That bare finger, where the ring should have been.

"I spend a whole turn of the Ash teaching you," he said, "and you give up on the first word. I'm insulted. I'm deeply insulted."

Zayn stared up at him.

"Z," said Soren. He leaned down and pointed at the dust. "Like a lightning bolt. Go on."

Zayn's finger moved. A line. A slant. A line.

"A. Like a tent." Soren was smiling now. "Y. Like a man waving for help, which is you, right now, honestly. And N. Two posts and a fence. Go on. Say it."

"*Zayn,*" whispered Zayn.

It came back all at once, like water into a dry well. A boy of seven at the edge of a village well, deciding he would be called a word that sounded like wind over a dune.

"There you are," said Soren softly.

His hand came down onto Zayn's shoulder. Warm. Warm as the Fire.

"You promised," he said. "Remember? Never give up." And then, quieter, with a grin that was almost the old grin, "Besides. There are eleven kids up there who don't know their names either. Somebody's going to have to teach them. I'd do it, but I'm a bit busy being dead."

Then the gold went out of the hold, and Zayn was alone in the dark, with four letters drawn in the dust beside his hand.

He didn't wipe them away.

---

The guard that came at midnight was alone, and he was humming.

Zayn knew the tune before he knew the man. Fire and rising wings. It was the song from the Singing Wells, the one he'd heard in the water: a man's voice, low and warm, singing over a small boy's hand.

The masked figure came down the ladder with a lantern and two grey blades crossed on his back. He sat down a little way off and went on humming, very softly, as if he didn't know he was doing it.

Brother Seven. Always hunting him. Always, somehow, missing.

Zayn started to hum too.

It just came out of him: the next line, the one the masked man hadn't reached yet.

The humming stopped.

Brother Seven sat completely still. The white mask stared at him across the lantern-light.

"Where did you learn it?" Zayn whispered. "That song. Who taught it to you?"

No answer. Brother Seven didn't speak. I never once, in all that time, heard him speak.

He stood and drew one of the grey blades. Drain him slowly. He knelt beside the chain and laid the blade flat against Zayn's arm.

And Zayn's hand closed around his wrist.

He wasn't thinking about himself. That's the only reason it worked. He was thinking about eleven children in grey robes upstairs, standing straight and still. About a boy with clear brown eyes asking whether his brother was coming.

The light came up out of his chest like the Fire coming up over the dunes.

*Gold.*

It ran down his arm and into the grey blade and *through* it, faster than the blade could drink. It went up the masked man's wrist. Up his arm. Into him.

And they both saw it.

---

A tent, at night. Lamplight. A storm howling outside, the walls of the tent bowing in and out like something breathing.

A small boy, five years old, sitting on a rug, very serious. In his arms, held carefully, the way he'd been shown, was a newborn baby wrapped in a grey cloth covered in strange woven symbols. The baby had one tiny fist wrapped around the boy's finger and wouldn't let go.

And somewhere above them, a man's voice was singing. Low and warm. Fire and rising wings.

The boy looked down at the baby and smiled. A gap-toothed smile, so proud he could burst.

Then the tent wall tore open, and there were white masks in the storm.

---

The vision broke.

So did the mask.

I heard it from under the hull, a sharp, clean crack like ice splitting. A line ran straight down the middle of the smooth white face, from brow to chin, and golden light shone through it. Through the gap, for a single heartbeat, he saw an eye. Wide and wet and pale.

Too pale for a desert child.

Zayn saw something else, too, in the moment before the light faded. He saw what he always saw. The full Ash. The Crown Dune. The fire. But longer this time, clearer, and there were people in it now. A man on his knees in the sand, begging. A figure in a cracked white mask, standing very still.

Then it was gone.

Brother Seven tore free and scrambled backwards, one hand clamped over the crack in his mask, making a small, broken, wordless sound, like a child who's woken from a nightmare and can't find the door.

He stared at Zayn.

Then his blade flashed once in the lantern-light. I can't tell you whether he meant to do it. I don't think he knew himself. But the grey chain parted between Zayn's wrists like a cut thread.

And Brother Seven fled. Up the ladder, through the hatch, out into the night, and was gone.

---

The cuffs were still on his wrists. It didn't matter. He was up and across the hold before the hatch had stopped swinging, and he had the broken sword in his hand and his bag over his shoulder.

He went up the ladder to the children.

They were in a narrow room near the prow, awake. Children in places like that are always awake. Eleven small, frightened faces turned toward the boy in the doorway with golden cracks shining on his arms.

"Up," he said. "All of you. Now. Hold hands. Don't let go."

They stared at him. They had been taught not to move.

Tobin moved first. He got up and took the smallest girl's hand. Then the next child got up. And the next.

That's when the shouting started.

Running feet. Grey lanterns swinging. Masks poured toward the prow from both ends of the ship. Zayn backed the children into the corner and stood in front of them with half a sword and no way out, and the light in his chest flickered grey, because he was very, very afraid.

And the ship moved.

---

That was me.

I'd been waiting under the hull for three nights. Three nights in the sand with my burned side splitting and smoking and Malek's voice in my ears. I had one chance. And when I felt the light go gold above me, I knew.

I let it out.

The other half. The big half. The one I hide from every bearer. The one he'd burned in the storm.

It hurt. The burn hadn't healed, and the dark part of me came up through it like smoke through a crack, screaming. I became the thing I hate, a great shadow-beast with ember eyes and one silver eye burning cold, my back pressed up against the belly of a dead ship, sand pouring off me in rivers.

And I *lifted*.

The ship groaned, like the whole desert sighing. Timbers that hadn't moved in a thousand years tore loose from the dune. Masks went sliding and tumbling down the deck. Grey lanterns smashed. And the old hull split along its belly with a crack like thunder, and the night came pouring in.

I held it. Sand and wood and fifty masked men and a thousand years of silence, all on my back.

Through the gap in the broken hull, Zayn looked down at me.

He'd seen this shape once before, in the storm, and been so afraid of it that his light had burned it.

He wasn't afraid now. I saw his face. I'll never forget his face.

"*Kiri,*" he said.

That was all. Just my name. Not *monster*. My name.

"*Hold it,*" he said.

"*What do you think I'm doing?*" I said. My voice came out like a rockslide.

He laughed. I swear to you, in the middle of all that, he laughed. Then he was through the gap with eleven children holding hands behind him, sliding down the sand under the great dark arch of me, one after another, Tobin last, the smallest girl on Zayn's back.

When the last bare foot touched the sand outside, I let go.

The ship came down. It fell back into its dune with a roar of splitting timber, and the sand rushed in over it like water closing over a stone, and the grey lights inside it went out one by one.

---

I don't remember shrinking. I remember lying in the sand, small again, a fox again, with my side on fire and my legs not working.

I remember hands. His hands, picking me up. Holding me against his chest, right over the mark, where it was warm.

"I've got you," he said.

I had something in my mouth. I'd snatched it from the sand where I'd hidden it. I let it drop into his palm.

A small bronze bell, no bigger than a thumb.

"You dropped this," I said.

He looked at it. He looked at me. And behind us, over the ridge, came the sound I'd been dreading. Hissing. Claws on sand. The long, low howl of Hollow Hounds, and the drumming feet of sand lizards, and the flat voices of the Unnamed calling to one another in the dark.

Zayn tied the bell to his belt. He tucked me into the front of his shirt. He took Tobin's hand.

And he ran.

---

*End of Chapter Fifteen*
