# Chapter Nineteen
## The Crown Dune

> *He'll walk where the dunes write the words of the sky,*
> *And the light will be born on the day he will die.*
> — from the Song of the Phoenix-Born

---

Mother Yusa found us on the palace stairs as we were leaving.

She'd come up out of the kitchens with a ladle in one hand like a club, flour and grey dust in her hair, and she saw the king's ring on the cord at Zayn's neck and understood everything at once. She didn't cry. She didn't ask. She pushed a small cloth bundle into his hands, and then she took his face between her two enormous palms and kissed his forehead, hard, the way you'd stamp a seal into wax.

Then she went back down into the dark to look for Idan's nurse, and I never saw her again.

Inside the bundle were dates. Rolled in honey.

---

We rode out of Veimore through the eastern gate, the three of us on the pale camel, Daruun in front and Zayn behind him and me in the boy's lap, bleeding quietly into his shirt.

The streets were silent behind us. The Husks still stood where they had stopped, all of them facing east. We rode right past them. They didn't reach for us. They didn't even turn their heads. They were waiting, the way the whole desert was waiting.

Zayn unwrapped the bundle and held the dates in his palm for a long time.

He'd always saved them. You remember. Ever since he was a small boy in a patched tent, he'd saved dates and honey for the hardest days, as if you could store sweetness the way you store water.

He ate one. Then he held one out over Daruun's shoulder, and after a moment his father took it. Then he gave one to me, and I ate it, though I prefer scorpions and always will.

"This is the hardest day," Zayn said. "I think. I don't think there's a harder one."

My tail glowed.

They rode in silence a while. The Crown Dune rose ahead of us, higher with every step, until it filled half the sky.

"Father," said Zayn.

It was the first time he'd called him that. I felt Daruun go rigid in the saddle.

"I forgive you."

Daruun didn't turn round. His shoulders shook. "You can't. I ran. If I hadn't run, none of this—your brother, the Hollow, all of it. You can't forgive that."

"I just did," said Zayn. "And I can't lie. So."

My tail lit up so brightly that it threw our shadow out across the sand ahead of us, long and gold.

Daruun made a sound I won't describe. He reached back with one hand without looking, and Zayn took it, and they rode like that, holding on, all the way to the foot of the dune.

---

The Husks were waiting there.

Not just the ones from Veimore. All of them. They had come from the whole desert in the night, from the grey wastes and the emptied villages, from Harrow's End and the caravan roads and the oasis towns, and they stood in a ring around the foot of the Crown Dune a hundred deep. Herders. Traders. Old women. Children. Thousands of white, blank faces, all turned up toward the top of the dune.

They parted for us like water.

We climbed. The camel couldn't, so we left it at the bottom. The sand slid under our feet with every step, and Zayn went first, and Daruun came behind with his spear, and I came last, limping. Halfway up, Zayn turned and picked me up without a word and carried me the rest of the way, the way he had carried me out of the City of Glass. I let him. I was too tired to be proud.

And then we were at the top, and I looked up, and it was the vision.

I had seen it in his eyes every time he healed. Twelve times. Twenty. I had stopped counting. And here it was, every piece of it falling into place like the last stones of a wall.

The Ash hung full and white above the Crown Dune, so close it looked like you could reach up and smudge it with your thumb. The top of the dune was a long, flat crest of pale sand, cold under our feet. Below us, on every side, the desert went away silver and grey to the edge of the world.

Orrin was waiting at the far end of the crest, with Idan.

Around him stood the Unnamed in their grey robes, perhaps twenty of them, their white masks shining like small moons. And a little apart from the others, his twin blades in his hands, stood Brother Seven, with the crack in his mask grown so wide now that half his real face showed through it.

And between us and them, the grey was gathering.

---

It rose out of the sand like smoke from a fire that had been put out with water. It thickened. It took a shape.

A boy.

A small boy, smaller than Zayn, barefoot and thin, in a shepherd's tunic. He had a shepherd's crook in one hand. Everything about him was grey: his skin, his hair, his ragged clothes. Only his eyes were different. They were black, completely black, like two holes cut into the night.

I knew that face. I had known it before there were cities in this desert. I had sat beside that face at a thousand fires, when it was brown and laughing and smelled of sheep.

"Hello, Malek," I said.

"Hello, little fox." His voice was a child's voice, and under it, a thousand others. "You've come to watch another one burn."

"I've come to watch one *choose*. It's different. You'd know, if you'd done it."

The grey boy smiled at me. It was the worst thing I have ever seen.

Then he turned his black eyes on Zayn.

"Look at them," Malek said softly. He pointed his crook down the dune at the ring of Husks. "Look. Those are the people you're going to die for. Do you see that one? He threw stones at you when you were six. Those ones drove you out of your village. Those ones called you monster, and spat on you, and watched you starve. And you'd burn for them?"

Zayn's light flickered. It went grey at the edges.

"And after," said Malek, "they'll light a lantern. One more lantern, in a row of eleven. And in a hundred years, no one will remember what it was for. No one remembers the bearers. They didn't remember mine. You'll be forgotten." His voice dropped to a whisper, gentle as sand. "You'll be forgotten, Zayn. Just like you always were."

My tail went dark. Pitch dark, cold as a dead coal.

But I could see the boy's face. And I could see that it didn't matter what my tail said, because it was the oldest fear he had. It was the fear he'd been carrying since he was a baby crying in the sand. *Nobody will remember me.*

His light went grey all the way through.

"There's a way out," said Orrin.

He stepped forward with one hand on Idan's shoulder and the thin grey blade in the other. His soft voice carried along the crest the way it had always filled the throne hall.

"Give me your name," he said. "That's all. Say it to the wind. Your true name, the one in your blood. Malek will take it, and the Light with it, and then it's over. No burning. No pain. You'll simply forget. And then everyone will forget, everyone in the whole desert, every loss, every grief, every dead son." Something moved in his faded eyes. "I promise you, child, it doesn't hurt. I've done it. I burned my own name to stop remembering. Forgetting is the only mercy there is."

My tail flickered. Not dark. Not bright. Somewhere in between.

He believed it. That's the worst kind of lie, little one: the kind the liar has told himself so long it sounds like truth.

"Give me your name," said Orrin.

And Zayn, grey and shaking, opened his mouth.

---

Bells.

Small bronze bells, a great many of them, ringing very faintly somewhere below us on the slope.

Every head turned.

Someone was climbing the Crown Dune. Slowly. On hands and knees, through the sliding sand. He had a stick, but he wasn't using it any more. He was simply crawling, one hand and then the other, and around his waist hung a belt of tiny truth bells that chimed with every movement.

It was Old Hesset.

He should not have been there. You know that. He was bound to the edge of the desert, to Harrow's End, and had been for three hundred years, the price of the night he ran. To leave was to break the bond. To break the bond was to die.

He had walked across the whole Destiny Desert anyway.

He was dying as he climbed. I could see it. Every step had taken years off what little he had left. His beard was thin as smoke. His hands were like dry leaves. His milky blind eyes were fixed on nothing, and he climbed toward the sound of our voices as if they were a fire on a cold night.

He reached the crest and knelt there, gasping. Malek turned to look at him.

"Hesset," said the grey boy, in a warm voice. "My old friend."

Every bell on Hesset's belt rang at once.

"I was never your friend," said Hesset. "And you were never mine. You only told me what I wanted to hear, so I'd run. I've had three hundred years in the dark to work that out." He turned his blind face toward Zayn. "Boy. Are you there?"

"I'm here," said Zayn.

"I asked you a question once, in my tent. *Would you die for them?* You couldn't answer." The old man swallowed. "I never told you that someone asked me the same question, on this sand, three hundred years ago. I never told you what I answered. I said no. I said no, and I ran, and the last thing I ever saw was the top of this dune. I've been in the dark ever since." His voice broke. "I hid it from you. I hid that bearers die, because I was ashamed. I'm sorry. I'm so sorry, child."

Not a single bell rang.

"Don't answer for me," whispered Hesset. "Don't answer for them. Answer for you."

Zayn stood very still on the top of the Crown Dune.

He looked at Malek. He looked down at the thousands of Husks, and somewhere among them, maybe, was a boy who'd thrown a stone at him once. He looked at Idan in Orrin's grip. He looked at his father. He looked at me.

Then he untied Hesset's little truth bell from his belt and held it up in front of him, so everyone could see.

"Yes," he said.

The bell didn't ring.

And the grey went out of his light like a shadow when the Fire comes up. It turned gold. Not the gold I'd seen in Harrow's End, or in the City of Glass, or even on the stairs of Veimore. A deeper gold, steady and warm, the gold of honey held up to the light.

Malek took a step backward.

---

"Brother Seven," said Orrin sharply. "Cut him."

Tamir raised his twin grey blades.

They were the only things in the world that could cut the Light. He stood between Zayn and Orrin, and his blades were shaking, and through the great crack in his mask I could see one dark eye, wet and wide.

He was humming. He didn't know he was doing it.

Zayn walked toward him.

He didn't raise his sword. He didn't hurry. He walked straight up to his brother until the grey blades were a finger's width from his chest, and he looked into that one dark eye through the crack.

"You held me," he said. "When I was born. I saw it. You were five, and you held me, and you were scared you'd drop me."

The blades trembled.

Then, slowly, Tamir lowered them.

Zayn walked past him to Orrin. The vizier backed away, dragging Idan with him, and raised the thin grey blade to the boy's throat. Zayn didn't stop. He put his hand over Orrin's hand, over the grip of the knife, and the gold went into it.

Orrin cried out and let go. His hand was grey to the wrist where the Light had touched it, the grey of something that had stolen too much for too long. The blade fell into the sand.

Idan ran.

He ran straight past Zayn to Daruun, and Daruun caught him, and held him, and the boy buried his face in the grey wrappings and shook. After a moment Zayn came and knelt in front of him. He took the cord from his own neck, untied Soren's ring from beside the infinity knot, and pressed it into Idan's hand and closed the small fingers over it.

"Your father wanted you to have this. And so would Soren."

Idan looked at him. He was ten. He wasn't stupid.

"Are you going to die?" he said.

"Yes."

"Like Soren?"

Zayn thought about that. "No," he said. "Soren didn't get to choose. I do."

---

Daruun let go of Idan, and took his son's hands.

"Let me," he said.

His voice was not the voice of a warrior or a hunter, or of the Veiled Rider who had saved us three times out of the dark. It was the voice of a man begging.

"Let me do it. I was meant to. It was mine, before it was yours, and I ran. Let me pay for it. *Please.* I ran so you could live. Don't make that for nothing. Please, son. Not you. Not you."

Zayn shook his head. He couldn't speak for a moment.

"It wasn't for nothing," he said finally. "You ran, and you left me in the sand, and the desert kept me. And I grew up alone, so I know what it's like to be the one nobody wants. That's why I can do this. That's why it's me." He tightened his grip. "It all meant something. Every bit of it. Even the bad parts. *Especially* the bad parts."

My tail was so bright I couldn't look at it.

Daruun bowed his head over their joined hands. His whole body shook. I have seen men face Hounds and Veilbeasts and armies, and I have never seen anything as brave as what that man did next.

He let go.

He didn't do it all at once. He couldn't. He did it one finger at a time, slowly, like a man lowering something precious into deep water. And when the last finger slid away, he stepped back, and stood beside Idan and Hesset, and didn't move again.

The hardest choice of his life. He made it.

---

Zayn turned to me last.

I was sitting in the sand, too tired to stand, my fur dark with my own blood, my silver eye aching. He knelt down so our faces were level.

"You lied to me once," he said.

"I know."

"Tell me the truth now. Will it hurt?"

"Yes," I said. My tail glowed.

"Will it matter?"

"Yes."

The glow of it lit his whole face. He looked at it for a long moment, and then he smiled, the small, crooked, rusty smile that he never wasted, and he put his forehead against mine.

"Look after them," he said.

I couldn't answer. For the first time in all my long life, I couldn't think of a single word.

He stood up. He walked out to the very end of the crest, where the dune fell away on every side into the silver dark, and stood there under the full Ash, with his back to all of us.

"I always wanted to be a hero," he said quietly. Not to us. To himself, maybe, or to the boy in the patched tent. "Like in the stories."

He took a breath.

"I am the hero."

It wasn't a boast. It was simply true. My tail had never burned so bright.

Then he lifted his face to the Ash, and to whatever was beyond it, and he said the last words he would ever say.

"I'm coming, Soren."

---

And here I have to tell it the way I told it at the very beginning, because I have seen this moment so many times since that it has never stopped happening. It's happening now. It's always happening, somewhere behind my eyes.

He is burning.

He stands at the top of the Crown Dune with his arms open, as if he's about to catch something falling from the sky. The fire doesn't start at his hands. It starts in his chest, right where the mark is, the small golden feather that has glowed in the dark his whole life. It cracks open like a seed. Light pours through the cracks in his skin, through every golden line that every healing left on him. It runs down his arms and up his throat and out of his eyes.

And Orrin lunges.

I see him come. Grey robes, grey hand, grey eyes wide and hungry. He's spent his whole life waiting for this light, and he throws himself into it with both hands outstretched, grabbing, as if he could tear it out of the boy and keep it for himself.

"*Mine,*" he says.

It isn't. It never could be. The Light can't be taken. It can only be given.

It turns grey in his hands. I watch it happen. The gold he's grasping goes dull, then ashen, and the grey runs up his fingers and his wrists and his arms like frost climbing a window. He looks down at himself. For a single moment, his face changes. I think he sees something in the stolen light: a small boy, perhaps, and a brother, and a name he burned a very long time ago. His mouth opens.

Then he crumbles. Not like flesh. Like an old grey statue that has stood in the wind too long. He comes apart into grey dust, and the wind lifts the dust off the top of the Crown Dune and carries it away into the dark, the way it carries away a name that has been spoken carelessly.

There's nothing left of him at all.

And the boy doesn't scream.

That's the part I always remember. He doesn't scream.

He looks down. Below him, all across the desert, the dunes are moving. Under the full Ash, in the silver dark, they're shifting and folding and rising, writing something across the sand in letters miles high. The same shapes they wrote for him on the first nights after he left Harrow's End, when he couldn't read a word and I wouldn't tell him what they said.

He can read them now.

A boy with a broken sword taught him, by a fire, with a stick in the sand, laughing the whole time.

*A*, the dunes write. *E. R. O. N.*

*Aeron.* In the old tongue, it means *the one who rises.*

It's his name. His true name, the one the Weaver wrote for him before he was born, the one the wind could never find, because the desert kept it safe for him all this time. And he reads it, the last thing he will ever read, and I see him smile.

Then he isn't looking at the dunes anymore. He's looking at something only he can see. But I see it too, just for a moment, in the light pouring out of him.

The Singing Wells, under a thin Ash. The water humming. And a young man sitting on the edge of the well with half a sword across his knees, a scarred jaw and a crooked grin, laughing. Laughing at him, the way he always did. Holding out his hand.

*You took your time,* says Soren.

And Zayn laughs. The way he laughed the first time, by a fire, when a prince tried to eat a scorpion. And he reaches out and takes the hand.

The light keeps growing until it isn't a boy anymore. It's a shape, two great wings of gold folding out across the dark, wide enough to cover the dunes from one edge of the sky to the other.

Then it falls.

Gold rains down over the desert, and everywhere it lands, the grey goes away.

Somewhere below, someone is shouting a name.

It's his father. On his knees in the sand at the top of the Crown Dune, his arms around a crying prince, his face turned up into the falling gold. Shouting it over and over, the only name he was ever allowed to call his son.

"*Zayn! Zayn!*"

And the wind takes it, gently, and carries it out over the whole desert. Not to the dark this time.

There isn't any dark left.

---

*End of Chapter Nineteen*
