# Chapter Thirteen
## Nameless Again

> *The loneliest grave is the one no one visits.*
> — a saying of the desert burial-keepers

---

For four days, he didn't say a word.

You'd think that would be a relief to me. Now I had all the silence I could ever want, and I would have traded every scorpion between here and the edge of the world to hear him tell me to shut up.

He walked. That was all he did. He walked while the sand was still cold, and at noon when the Fire hammered down so hard the air shook, and at night under an Ash that was already starting to thin. He didn't choose a direction. He let the dunes choose for him. When a slope rose in front of him he climbed it. When a valley opened he went down into it. If the Weaver was writing the sands that week, it was writing them for a boy who had stopped reading.

Soren's sword was tied across his back, wrapped in his scarf. Half a blade and a plain leather hilt. It knocked against his spine with every step, and every time it did, he flinched, and every time he flinched, he kept walking.

He hadn't given Soren to the dunes. He hadn't been able to make his hands do it. He'd sat beside him until the Fire was high and the wind had begun the work for him, and then he'd walked away and left the sand to finish it, with the word the travellers had shouted chasing him over the dunes the way stones had once chased him out of Harrow's End.

*Prince-Killer.*

He had been called a lot of things in his life. Nameless. Sandborn. Monster. Now he had a new one, and the worst part, the part that sat in his chest like a swallowed stone, was that he couldn't be sure it was wrong.

I followed him a dune behind. I'd have walked closer, but I wasn't walking well.

---

You remember the storm. The pillar, and what I turned into to lift it, and how his light caught me full in my Hollow half. It burned the way truth burns a lie, all the way down, looking for the bottom.

There was a patch on my left side, the silver side, where the fur had come away. What was underneath wasn't fox. It was dark and faintly smoking, like a coal dropped in water that hadn't decided yet whether to go out. It hadn't healed.

That night, the night of the full Ash, had been one of the worse ones. I'll say that much and no more.

So I limped. And he walked. And neither of us talked about it.

---

He still did the things that kept him alive. Fifteen years of scraping by don't leave you just because your heart has.

On the second day he found water. He licked his finger and held it to the wind, then walked straight down to a hollow where camel-thorn grew, and dug until the sand went dark and damp, and drank.

He had taught Soren how to do that. I watched him remember it. I watched him sit back on his heels with wet sand on his fingers and his face go completely still, the way a pool goes still after something has sunk to the bottom of it.

Then he got up and kept walking.

At night he didn't sleep much. He sat at the top of whatever dune he'd stopped on, and with one finger he wrote in the sand.

The same word, every night. Five letters. He wrote them slowly and carefully, the way you write a thing you've only just learned, with your tongue between your teeth. Soren had made him practise that word fifty times by the fire, laughing at the way he wrote the S backwards. *It's my name, Zayn. Have some respect.* He didn't write it backwards anymore.

S. O. R. E. N.

And every morning, when he woke, the dunes had moved in the night, and the word was gone.

That's the trouble with being given to the desert. There's no stone. There's no place. The sand takes you and keeps moving, and the people who loved you have to carry the grave around inside them, because there's nowhere else to put it.

---

On the third night he tried to lie.

I watched him do it. He was sitting with his knees pulled up and the sword across his lap, and he said, so quietly I nearly didn't hear it, "It wasn't my fault."

His face twisted. His hand flew to his chest. The feather mark flared under his shirt, hot and sharp, and he doubled over and pressed his palm against it until the light went out.

He couldn't even lie to himself. Not even for comfort. Not even once.

Most people get to tell themselves small kind lies in the dark. *There was nothing I could have done.* Lies like that are how people get up in the morning. Zayn never got any.

And what was true was this: they had argued under the full Ash, and he had walked away, and he had left Soren alone in the dark.

"The sand remembers," he whispered afterwards. The old words. The ones he'd said every bad night in Harrow's End.

The mark didn't burn. It wasn't a lie.

It just wasn't something he believed anymore, either. There's a difference, and I watched him fall into it.

I limped up the slope and sat down a little way off. He didn't look at me.

"It wasn't your fault," I said.

My tail lit up, bright and steady as a lamp. I wanted him to see it more than I've wanted most things.

He didn't turn his head.

"Don't," he said.

It was the first word he'd spoken in three days, hoarse, as if his voice had rusted.

"It's true," I said. "Look at my tail. Look at it."

"It glowed at the City of Glass too," he said. "Right up until it didn't."

I had nothing to say to that. I had told him one lie in fifteen years, one small, merciful lie, and it had cost me every true thing I would ever say to him afterwards. Remember that, if you ever think a lie is cheap.

He was quiet for a while. Then he said, without looking at me, "Where were you?"

The wind moved over the dune.

"That night," he said. "After I walked away. You weren't with him. You weren't with me. Where were you?"

I opened my mouth.

I'm not going to tell you what I nearly said. I'm not going to tell you where I was, either. Not yet. There's a night in this story I haven't told you about, and I'm not ready, and it's my story, so I get to choose when.

I closed my mouth again.

He nodded slowly, as though I'd answered. Then he lay down with his back to me and the broken sword in his arms, and I sat beside him until the Embers went pale, and neither of us said anything at all.

---

There was a twist of waxed paper at the bottom of his bag. You'll know what was in it. Three dates rolled in honey, the last of the four, gone sticky and dark, carried all the way from Harrow's End.

He had carried them through the beast and the wells and the glass city and the storm, saving them for the hardest day.

On the fourth evening he took them out and held them in his palm for a long time. I think he understood that this was it. If he didn't eat them now, he never would.

Then he wrapped them up again and put them back in the bag. He couldn't do it. Eating them would have meant saying that this was as bad as it got, and some small, stubborn part of him still wasn't ready to promise that.

I'm not sure, even now, whether that was hope or the opposite of it.

---

The fourth night was the one I'd been afraid of.

He stopped at the bottom of a long slope instead of the top. In fifteen years he had always slept high, where he could see what was coming. Now he walked down into the cold trough between two dunes, where the wind poured sand down the slip-face in hissing curtains.

And then he lay down.

He lay on his back with the sword on his chest and his arms folded over it, and he looked up at the Embers, and he let the sand come.

It came softly. A trickle over his feet. A drift against his side. The dunes were moving, the way they moved every night, and he had lain down in the path of one.

"Zayn," I said.

He didn't answer.

"Zayn. Get up."

The sand was over his ankles now. His knees. It was spilling into the folds of his shirt.

The dead are given to the dunes. That's what desert people do. He hadn't stayed to see the sand close over Soren. So he was giving them the next best thing.

"*Get up.*" I was on my feet, if you can call it that. I got my teeth into his sleeve and pulled. I pulled with everything I had, which that night was less than a kitten has. My burned side split open and smoked and I yelped, and his arm didn't even move.

I could have changed. You're thinking it. I was thinking it. I could have let the big dark half out and dug him free in three strokes.

He'd seen that half once, and been so afraid of it that his light had nearly burned it out of the world. I didn't know what it would do to him to wake up in the mouth of that thing.

So I stood there in the sliding sand, shaking, a small burned fox shouting at a boy who wouldn't listen.

And the light came.

---

It wasn't his light. That's what I knew first.

His light starts at the mark and pours outward. This came from the slope beside him, from nowhere at all. It was soft and gold and it didn't burn. It just *glowed*, the way a lamp glows in a window when someone is waiting up for you.

I didn't see anyone in it. I want to be honest about that. I did not see him. I only saw the light on Zayn's face, and Zayn's eyes open wide, and Zayn's lips move.

He told me later what he saw. I'll tell it the way he did.

Soren was sitting on the slope beside him. Cross-legged, elbows on his knees, the way he used to sit by the fire when he was about to say something annoying. The scar along his jaw. His dark hair coming loose. He looked exactly as he had in life, except that light came through the edges of him, like a cloth hung in front of the Fire.

His hand was bare. No ring. Zayn noticed that. He couldn't stop noticing it.

"You look terrible," said Soren. "That's weird."

Zayn made a sound. It wasn't a word.

"You're lying in a hole," said Soren. "Letting a dune sit on you. Do you know how stupid that looks from up here? Very. It looks very stupid."

"I left you," Zayn whispered.

Soren stopped smiling.

"Yeah," he said. "You did."

There was no anger in it. That was the worst part, Zayn told me. Soren only said it the way you say *the sand is hot*. A plain fact, laid down between them.

"I left you," Zayn said again, "and you died."

"I know. I was there." Soren leaned forward. He put out his hand and laid it on Zayn's shoulder, and it was *warm*. Zayn swore to me it was warm, warm as a living hand, warm as the Fire on a cold morning. "Get up anyway."

"I can't."

"Yes, you can. You're just choosing not to. It's different." Soren's grip tightened. His eyes were very bright. "Never give up, Zayn. You promised."

Zayn shut his eyes.

He had. Under the full Ash, on the last night, Soren had held his arm and said *Promise me you'll never give up*, and he had said *I promise*. And he could not lie. Not even with his last breath. Not even lying in the sand with a dune on his chest.

He had promised. So it was true. So he had to make it true.

When he opened his eyes again, the slope was empty and dark, and there was only a fox standing knee-deep in the sand beside him, with a tail blazing so bright it hurt to look at.

I hadn't seen anything. I'll swear to that.

But my tail had been burning gold the whole time he was talking, and it doesn't do that for dreams.

---

He got up.

He dug himself out with his hands, the way he'd once dug himself out of collapsed rooms in the Old Ruins, and climbed the slope on his hands and knees, dragging the sword.

Then he looked at me. Actually looked at me, for the first time in four days.

"Did you see him?" he said.

"No," I said.

My tail stayed bright. He watched it.

"But you believe me."

"Yes," I said. It stayed bright.

He looked at it a long moment. Then he looked away, out over the moving dunes, and he didn't say anything more. But he didn't turn his back on me either. That night, for the first time since the full Ash, he slept with me curled against his side.

It's not nothing. In the desert, you learn to count the things that are not nothing.

---

This next part is where I went wrong.

In the morning he walked on, and I couldn't keep up.

The dune had cost me more than I'd thought. By the time the Fire was two hands above the horizon I'd folded up in the shade of a rock with my legs not listening to me. He was three dunes ahead. Then four. I told myself I'd catch up when the heat broke.

Which is why I was a long way off, lying on a ridge, when the lanterns came.

You don't usually see lanterns in daylight. That's what made me lift my head. A little line of them, swaying, coming over the eastern dunes in a string: camels, six or seven, with lanterns hung from their saddles still burning in the morning, the way they burn on the walls of Veimore day and night. Rich camels. Red tassels. Water skins so fat they sloshed.

They came straight to him. Not near him. *To* him, as if they'd known exactly where a tired boy would be standing.

Fox ears are very good. Fox legs, that morning, were not. I dragged myself to the crest of the ridge and lay flat and listened.

The lead camel knelt. A man climbed down from it.

He was old, his hair fully silver and combed long over a robe the deep blue of the sky just after the Fire sets. His face was soft and kind and lined, the face of someone's grandfather. His voice was low and gentle, the kind that made you lean in to hear it.

And his eyes were grey.

Faded grey, flat and pale, like cloth left out in the Fire for a hundred years. Like a Husk's eyes, before the white sets in.

The fur along my spine stood straight up, and I couldn't have told you why. That's not true. I could have. I'm not going to. Something in me went cold and silent the moment I saw that face, the way it always goes cold and silent when anyone speaks of the night of the full Ash. Let's leave it there.

Behind the old man, another figure climbed down from a camel. He was the biggest man I had ever seen. Shaved head. Arms like the trunks of date palms. He didn't look as though he had ever said a word.

"Take the boy's bag," the old man said to him softly. "Can't you see he can barely stand?"

And the giant stepped forward and lifted the bag gently from Zayn's shoulder and carried it back to the baggage camel and tied it there, among the water skins.

Remember that. I didn't, not then. I was watching faces. I should have been watching the bag.

---

"You are the boy who glows," the old man said.

Zayn didn't answer. His hand had gone to the hilt of the broken sword on his back.

"My name is Orrin," said the old man. "I am the vizier of Veimore. I serve King Absalon." He paused. "You will have heard my name before. From him."

Zayn's knuckles went white on the hilt.

He had. Soren had told him the whole story by the fire: the stable boy he'd hidden, the broken sword, and the soft old voice of the vizier calling it treason, calling it dark magic, until his father believed it.

"Yes," said Orrin. "I was the one who named him traitor." He bowed his silver head. "I was wrong. I have spent a turn of the Ash crossing this desert to tell him so. And I came too late."

He reached into his robe.

He held something out on his open palm, and I saw Zayn's whole body go still.

A ring. Heavy gold, worn smooth, with the crest of Veimore cut into its face.

Soren's ring. The one he never took off. The one that had not been on his hand when the travellers found him.

"He gave me this," Orrin said gently.

On the ridge, my tail flickered.

I *think* it dimmed. I'm nearly sure. But the Fire was bright across the sand by then, and a tail is a hard thing to read in daylight, and I was in pain, and afraid. All of that is true. None of it is an excuse.

"When?" said Zayn. His voice cracked on it.

"On the night of the full Ash," said Orrin. "I found him at last. We spoke. He forgave me, I think, or began to. And he gave me his ring, and he said, *If anything happens to me, find the boy who glows. Look after him.*" The old man's voice shook, very slightly, in exactly the right place. "I didn't understand what he meant. Not until morning."

There was no bell. Hesset's bell was tied to a camel twenty paces away, in a patched bag, among the water skins. I told you to watch the bag.

Zayn reached out. Slowly, the way you reach for something that might vanish. Orrin let him take the ring, and Zayn held it in his palm and closed his fingers over it, and his face crumpled.

"Did you kill him?" Orrin asked quietly. Not cruelly. Almost kindly. "They're saying you did."

Zayn's eyes were streaming. He could have said *no*. It would have been the truth. Zayn hadn't killed anyone.

But the boy could not lie, and in his heart, the truth was something else.

"I left him," he said.

Orrin nodded slowly. "I know," he said. "So did I, once, a long time ago. Someone I loved." His grey eyes were wet. "Everyone leaves someone, child. It's what we do afterwards that matters."

He held out his hand. After a moment, Zayn gave the ring back. Orrin tucked it into his robe again, carefully, close to his heart.

"It should go to his father," he said. "And to his brother. I will see that it does. But you need food, and shade, and sleep. There's an oasis town two days east of here. I have a house there. Come with me." He paused. "And when you're stronger, I'll tell you about your father."

Zayn's head came up.

"I knew him," said Orrin. "Long ago. He carried what you carry. I can tell you who he was. If you want to know."

---

By the time I got there, Zayn was already climbing onto a camel.

I came down the slope in a sort of controlled fall, my side smoking. Orrin's soft grey eyes found me, and he smiled.

"Ah," he said. "The fox. Of course."

There was no surprise in it at all.

I stood at the camel's feet with my legs shaking and my fur on end and looked up at the boy. He was holding the reins in one hand. His other hand was pressed flat to his chest, over the mark.

I could have said a great many things in that moment. I've had a long time to make a list of them.

I said, "I don't like him."

Four words. My tail glowed. They were perfectly true.

Zayn looked down at me. He looked tired beyond anything a boy of fifteen should be. "You don't like anyone," he said.

That was true too.

"And you told me *yes* once," he said, more quietly. "Remember?"

Then he turned the camel's head east and followed the old man's lanterns over the dune.

I stood there in the sand for a long moment. Then I followed. What else was I going to do? I'd been following him for fifteen years.

I didn't know then what it would cost, those four small, cowardly, true words, and all the words I didn't say after them. I know now. I'll tell you. Not yet.

But the Ash was thinning above us, night by night, and I had started counting again.

*End of Chapter Thirteen*
