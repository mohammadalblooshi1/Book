# Chapter Twelve
## The Night of the Full Ash

> *Under the full Ash, every promise is weighed.*
> — from the oath-sayings of the free towns, spoken over water at weddings and at graves

---

I didn't speak again before the full Ash.

I'll tell you about them anyway. That's the strange thing about being the one who tells a story: you can be silent all the way through it and still have to describe every minute.

I walked. That's mostly what I did. I limped along behind the two of them with my fur scorched brown down one side and my silver eye swollen shut. I didn't catch scorpions. I didn't eat much at all. At night I slept a little way off from the fire instead of against Zayn's side, and every night he looked over at me before he lay down, and every night I turned my face away.

He tried to say sorry. Again and again. Sometimes out loud, sometimes just by bringing me water in his cupped hands and setting it down beside me without a word. Once he left me the best piece of a roasted sand-hen, and sat with his back to me so I could eat it without being watched. I ate it after he'd fallen asleep.

I wasn't angry with him. You need to understand that, because he didn't. I wasn't angry with him at all.

I was ashamed. He had seen what I was. And there is no word for how that feels, except that it's the opposite of being remembered.

---

Soren was different too, after the storm.

He still joked. He still complained about his feet, and his bruised legs, and the general unfairness of sand. He still drew letters every evening, and Zayn learned fast now. He could read whole short lines. One night Soren wrote ZAYN AND SOREN, MONSTERS in big letters in the sand and made him read it out, and Zayn did, and they both laughed.

But Soren had started touching the hilt of his sword. Not the grip, the way a fighter does. The pommel. He'd rest his thumb on the bronze cap without seeming to know he was doing it, the way Zayn touched his feather mark. And when he thought no one was looking, he'd stand on top of a dune and stare west, toward Veimore, for a long time.

"We're changing direction," he announced on the fourth morning. "West. I need to go home."

Zayn stared at him. "You were exiled."

"I know. I was there."

"They'll kill you."

"They'll *try*." Soren's grin came and went too fast. "I need to see Idan. I need to make sure he's all right."

My tail glowed. That was true. Every word.

But Zayn, who had been watching my tail since Harrow's End, had also learned to watch the spaces between words. He looked at Soren a long moment. He knew there was something more. And because Zayn never in his life asked anyone for something they didn't want to give, he didn't ask.

I knew what it was. I'd watched it go into the hilt. I didn't say anything either.

Three of us walking west together, and every one of us carrying something we didn't say.

---

Every night, the Ash got fuller.

Zayn stopped looking at it. Then he started looking at it all the time. He'd lie on his back by the fire with his hand on his chest, staring up at that great white face as it swelled a little more each evening. I knew what he was seeing. It was the dune, and the fire, and the sound of somebody shouting a name at the bottom of the world.

He wasn't afraid it would happen *tonight*. He wasn't stupid; he knew the Crown Dune was nowhere near. But every full Ash would bring it one turn closer, and this was the first one since he'd learned what the word *ash* meant at the end of eight lines in the glass.

On the twenty-ninth night, it rose full.

It came up out of the eastern dunes huge and white and close, so close it seemed to fill half the sky, exactly as it did in his vision. The whole desert went silver under it. Every grain of sand had a shadow. It was the brightest night Zayn had ever seen, and it was beautiful, and he sat by the fire and couldn't stop shaking.

One turn of the Ash. Thin to full, and the dark between. That's how long they'd had.

I knew it. I'd counted. Neither of them knew.

---

It started, as the worst things do, with someone trying to help.

"Come to Veimore with me," Soren said.

He'd been quiet for a long time, sitting across the fire turning his ring. Now he leaned forward, and his face in the firelight was serious in a way Zayn had hardly ever seen it.

"I mean it. Listen. Under the city there are the catacombs, and in the catacombs there's the Archive. It's every book anyone in Veimore ever wrote. Every story. Every name. Thousands of years of them, Zayn, rooms and rooms, all the way down. If there's a third way written anywhere in this desert, it's there. You can read now. Not fast. But we could look together."

"Soren—"

"I can get you in. I know a way through the kitchens. The cook would hide an army if I asked her nicely and brought back her honey jars."

"I can't go to Veimore."

"Why not?"

"Because it's not *there*." Zayn's voice cracked. "It's not in a book. It's not in a library. You read them. In the glass. You read them out loud to me. Eight of them burned, and three of them ran, and the three who ran—" He pointed east, at nothing, at the empty silver desert. "Look what happened. One became the *Hollow*. That's the third way, Soren. That's the only other way there is."

"You don't *know* that."

"I've seen it! I see it every time! Every time I use the Light, I'm standing on that dune, and I'm—" He stopped. He pressed his hand flat over his chest. "It's not a story anymore. It's going to happen. It's *written*."

"Then we unwrite it."

"You can't unwrite the *sand*!"

The wells, the glass, the storm: none of it had been as loud as that. The words rang out across the silver dunes and the desert swallowed them, the way it swallows everything.

They sat there breathing hard on either side of the fire.

Soren looked away first. He looked down at his hands. When he spoke again his voice had gone quiet.

"Promise me something," he said.

Zayn didn't answer.

"Promise me you'll never give up." Soren looked up. His eyes were very bright. "Whatever happens. Whatever you see in your visions. Whatever the walls say. You keep going. You don't just *lie down* and let it take you. Promise me."

Zayn looked at him across the fire, under the full Ash.

He could not lie. You know that. He'd never been able to. So when he said it, he said it knowing that the feather on his chest would have burned like a coal if he hadn't meant it with everything he had.

"I promise," said Zayn.

It didn't burn.

My tail lit up the sand like a second fire.

---

If they'd stopped there, this would be a different story.

They didn't stop there.

"Good," said Soren. He let out a long breath, almost a laugh. "Good. Then come to Veimore."

"That's not—"

"You just promised."

"I promised not to give up. Going to the dune isn't giving up." Zayn's voice was shaking now. "Don't you see? It's the *opposite*. The ones who gave up are the ones who ran. If I keep going, Soren, *that's* where I'm going. That's what not giving up *is*, for me."

Soren stood up so fast he nearly kicked the fire.

"No," he said. "No. I'm not doing this again. I'm not standing by while someone I—" He bit it off. "I'm not letting them take you away. I won't. I'll drag you to Veimore by your ankles if I have to. I'll lock you in the catacombs. I'll find the third way myself and read it to you through the door. I am *not* losing anybody else."

"You can't save me."

"Watch me."

And then Zayn said it.

He didn't plan it. I know that, because I know him. He was frightened, so frightened, and he was tired, and the Ash was full and white above him like an eye that wouldn't close. Soren was standing over him promising things nobody could promise. And it hurt so much to be loved like that, when he knew exactly how it was going to end, that something in him reached for the sharpest true thing it could find and threw it.

"You can't save me," he said. "You couldn't even save Nim."

---

The fire cracked.

That was the only sound in the whole desert.

Soren didn't move. He didn't shout. His face didn't even change, at first. He just stood there with the full Ash behind him, looking down at Zayn, and something went out of his eyes. It was like a lantern going out on a high red wall, one of the ones that must never all go out.

My tail glowed. I wish it hadn't. But it was true, every word, and there's nothing in the world that cuts quite like the truth said at the wrong moment for the wrong reason.

Zayn saw his face. He saw what he'd done.

He got up. He didn't say sorry. He couldn't make his mouth do it. He turned round, and he walked away from the fire, out into the silver dark.

"Zayn," said Soren behind him.

Just his name. Once. Not loud.

Zayn didn't turn round.

I've thought about that moment more than any other in this story. More than the dune, even, and more than the fire. A boy walking away across moonlit sand, and another boy standing alone by a small fire, saying his name once to the wind.

He meant to come back. You should know that. He meant to walk until he'd stopped shaking, and then come back and say sorry, and make it right. He'd come back in the morning, he thought. There'd be time in the morning.

He walked away and left Soren alone.

It was the worst mistake he ever made. He'd tell you so himself.

---

And where was I?

I'll tell you that much. I'll tell you the first part.

I'd smelled Hounds that evening, before the argument even started. They were a long way off, but they were coming, drawn by the Light still glowing in the cracks on Zayn's arms. I'd been lying a little way from the fire with my scorched side to the cold sand, listening to the two of them talk. When Zayn walked off into the dark, I got up and went the other way.

I went hunting.

I let the Hollow half out. It hurt. Everything about it hurt that night. The burned part of me screamed every time it moved. But there were Hounds out in the dunes and the boy I loved was alone out there in the dark, and that was the only use I've ever found for the thing that I am.

I hunted them across the silver sand under the full Ash, for most of the night.

That's all I'm going to tell you about where I was. Don't ask me any more. Not yet.

---

Zayn didn't go far. He climbed a high dune a long way off and sat at the top, with his knees pulled up to his chest and the Ash pouring white light down on him. He could still see the glow of the fire, very small, down in the hollow where he'd left it.

He said sorry to it. Out loud, over and over, practising.

*I'm sorry. I didn't mean it. You were right to try. You were right to try to save him. You were right to try to save me.*

He was going to say all that in the morning. He went through it again and again, getting the words right, the way he'd copied his name in the sand until the letters were perfect.

At some point, near dawn, with the Ash sinking down into the western dunes and the sky going grey, he fell asleep sitting up.

He didn't dream. He'd tell you that if you asked him. He had no visions that night and no warnings, and he didn't feel anything at all. Not a whisper in the sand. Not a single bell.

The Weaver was very quiet that night. I've never forgiven it for that either.

---

He woke when the Fire touched his face. He scrambled up and ran down the dune, sliding and stumbling. He had the words ready in his mouth. *I'm sorry. You were right to try.*

He came over the last rise and stopped.

There were camels in the hollow. Five of them, kneeling in the sand, with packs of salt-blocks strapped to their sides. And there were men, five desert travellers in dusty white robes, standing in a ring around the cold remains of the fire.

They were looking down at something on the sand.

Zayn walked toward them. He didn't feel his legs moving. He didn't feel anything at all.

Soren lay on his back beside the dead fire, with one arm flung out and his broken sword in the sand by his hand.

He was grey.

Not Husk-grey. Not emptied. Something worse. His skin had gone the colour of old ash, every bit of it, and running all through the grey, across his face and his throat and down both his arms, were fine bright lines of *gold*. Thin glowing cracks, like the glaze on a broken pot.

Like the cracks the Light left on Zayn's own skin.

His eyes were open, looking up at the morning sky. They didn't see it.

---

One of the travellers saw Zayn first.

He was an older man with a beard gone yellow at the edges, and he backed away a step, then another. Then the others turned and saw him too, and they all went still.

They looked at Zayn. They looked at his arms, at the golden cracks still glowing faintly from wrist to shoulder, left by the storm.

They looked at Soren, cracked with the same gold.

"That's him," said one of them, a young man with a falcon hooded on his wrist. His voice was high and thin. "That's the prince. The exiled one. The *king's son*. Look at the jaw, the scar—they said in the free towns, they said he was out here selling ash—"

"And *that*," said the old man, staring at Zayn, "is the glowing boy."

Zayn didn't move.

"From the caravan," the old man said. "They talked about him at the last well. The one the beast didn't eat. The one who glows. A man came out of the sand alive, and he was telling everyone." He was backing toward his camel. "*Monster*, he said. And here he is."

"I didn't," said Zayn.

His voice came out of him like something broken. He looked at Soren, and back at them.

"I didn't do this."

The feather mark on his chest didn't burn. He felt it not burn. It was true. Every word. And it didn't matter at all, because these men had never heard of a boy who couldn't lie, and if they had, they wouldn't have believed it.

There were other true things, too. They came up in him one after another, like water rising in a well.

*I left him.*

*I said the worst thing I could think of, and then I left him alone in the dark.*

"I left him," said Zayn. "I walked away. I left him alone."

The feather didn't burn for that either.

The travellers stared at him. And then one of them, I never did learn which, said it: the word that would follow Zayn across the whole desert, from oasis to oasis, carried on falcons' legs and in traders' mouths, faster than any lizard could run.

"*Prince-Killer.*"

They ran.

They were on their camels and away in moments, with salt-blocks swinging and the young man's falcon screaming on his wrist. They went east, toward the free towns, toward the wells. Toward everyone. I could hear them shouting it to each other as they went, over and over, until the dunes swallowed the sound.

*Prince-Killer. Prince-Killer. The glowing boy killed the prince.*

By the next turn of the Ash, there wouldn't be a town in the desert that hadn't heard it.

---

Zayn knelt down in the sand beside Soren.

He didn't touch him at first. He just knelt there, with his hands open on his knees and the golden cracks glowing in his skin.

Then, very slowly, he reached out and closed Soren's eyes.

He wanted to heal him. Of course he did. His hands were already burning. He put them on Soren's chest and called the Light, and it came, gold and desperate. And it found nothing to hold on to. Nothing at all. There was nobody in there to bring back.

He'd never seen it fail before.

He took Soren's hand. It was cold. His fingers were grey and cracked with gold, and on the right hand, on the finger where the heavy ring had always been, there was nothing. Only a band of paler skin where the ring had always sat. The ring he never took off.

Zayn looked at that bare finger for a long time.

He looked down at his own arms, at the golden cracks. Then he looked at the cracks on Soren's face. The same gold. The same light. And a question came into his eyes that I could see as clearly as if it had been written in the sand. He'd been asleep. He'd been far away. He'd felt nothing. But the Light had burst out of him once already when he didn't mean it to, and nearly killed me.

*Did I do this?*

He would carry that question for a long time. I'm sorry to tell you that.

---

The broken sword was lying in the sand by Soren's open hand.

Zayn picked it up.

It was lighter than he'd expected. Half a blade, a hand's width and a little more of jagged steel, with dark leather on the grip and a bronze pommel that didn't quite match. It was still warm from the morning Fire. He held it across his knees the way Soren used to hold it by the fire at night.

He didn't know what was inside it. Not yet.

He sat there in the hollow with the sword in his lap and his friend beside him, as the Fire climbed up the sky and the shadows of the dunes went short and the wind came and began, gently, to move the sand.

He didn't cry. Not then. That came later.

He just sat, and held on.

---

I was already on the dune above the hollow when he came over the rise that morning. I'd been there before the travellers came. Don't ask me how long.

I sat at the top of the slope, a long way off, with my scorched fur and my one good eye, and I didn't go any closer. My tail lay in the sand behind me. It wasn't glowing. It wasn't dark. It just *lay* there, the way a tail does when no one's saying anything at all.

Zayn didn't look up. I'm not sure he knew I was there.

I could have gone down to him. I could have sat in his lap and let him hold on to me instead of a broken sword. I could have said a great many things.

I didn't say anything.

I'm very good at that. I've told you before. It's becoming a theme.

It's the one I'm least proud of.

---

*End of Chapter Twelve*
