# Chapter Ten
## The City of Glass

> *Here the first one said no, and the sand wept glass.*
> — scratched into a glass wall, in a hand no one living can read

---

Night fourteen. We saw the City of Glass at sunset, which is the only proper way to see it, and the worst.

It rose out of the dunes like something the desert had coughed up and couldn't swallow back. Towers of it, and walls and broken arches, and whole streets of glass running away into the sand. Not clear glass, like a window. Glass the colour of everything: green as a bottle, black as oil, amber as honey, and here and there a strange cloudy red. When the Fire went down behind it, the light went *into* the glass and stayed there, and for a few moments the whole city burned from the inside, every tower lit up like a lantern.

Soren stopped walking.

"Weaver's teeth," he said softly.

Zayn didn't say anything at all. His hand was on his chest. The mark under his shirt had been warm all day, and now that the city was in front of him it was hot, throbbing in time with his heartbeat.

I sat down in the sand and did not look at it.

---

It had taken us four days to walk there from the Singing Wells. Zayn led us by the Embers at night, and Soren read us the stones by day: old waymarkers, half-buried, with letters carved into them that had been worn almost smooth. They were good days. I want you to know that. Soren complained about his feet, and Zayn found water twice in places Soren swore there couldn't be any, and every evening they sat by a small fire while Soren drew letters in the sand and Zayn copied them.

He'd learned a good many by then. He could write his own name, and Soren's, and mine. He could write *water* and *fire*, and *camel*, which Soren insisted was the most useful word in any language. And on the twelfth night, sitting under the Embers, he'd asked Soren to show him the sky-words.

"Fire," Soren had said, drawing. "Embers. And Ash. That one's easy. Three letters. *Ah. Sss. Huh.*"

Zayn had copied it ten times. He didn't know yet why it mattered. Neither did Soren.

I did. I could have stopped the lesson. I didn't.

---

"Here the first one said no," I said, when they'd both stared at the city long enough. "That's what the old stories say. The first bearer came here, a long time ago. There was a city then, an ordinary one, stone and clay. He stood on that hill over there," I nodded toward a glassy rise at the heart of it, "and the Weaver asked him to give the Light away, and he said *no*. And the Light went the wrong way. It went *inward*. It burned so hot the sand around him melted. The whole city." I watched a cloud of glass-dust blow across the dunes. "The sand wept glass."

My tail was glowing. Every word of it was true.

"How do you know that?" Zayn asked.

"Stories," I said.

My tail stayed bright, because I'd *been* the story. But Zayn didn't know that, and I didn't tell him.

I'd stood on that hill. I'd watched a small, poor, furious boy with a shepherd's crook close his hands around the Light instead of opening them. I'd felt the heat of it singe my whiskers. I'd called his name over and over, and he'd looked straight at me and hadn't known who I was.

I hadn't been back in a very long time. I'd have been happy never to come back at all.

"Kiri?" said Zayn.

"I'm fine," I said. "Glass makes my paws cold. Let's get inside before the Hounds smell us."

My tail flickered. That one was only half true, and Zayn saw it, and for once he was kind enough not to say so.

---

The murals were in the great hall at the centre of the city.

It might have been a temple once. It was hard to tell. The roof was gone, and the walls were glass all the way up, smooth and dark, and the evening came down through the open top in long violet bars. And in the walls, *inside* the walls, deep in the glass like insects in amber, there were pictures.

Nobody had painted them. Nobody had carved them. They were simply *there*, floating in the glass, the way your reflection floats in water. The glass had remembered them.

There were eleven.

They ran in a long curve around the hall, each one taller than a man. Under each one, set into the glass in pale gold, ran a line of letters.

Soren had gone off to find the old cistern. He said a city that size must have had one, and we were down to half a skin of water. "Don't touch anything," he'd said as he left. "Especially you, fox."

So it was only Zayn and me in the great hall when he started to read.

---

He went slowly. You have to understand how slowly. Two weeks before, he hadn't known a single letter. Now he stood in front of each mural with his lips moving, sounding out the gold shapes underneath one by one, the way a child sounds them out. *Thuh. Eh.* A long pause. His finger traced the letters on the glass without quite touching them.

He couldn't read most of it. The titles were too long, the old words too strange. But he could read *some*. He could pick out letters he knew, and short words, and every so often a whole word came clear for him like a coin turning up in the sand.

"*Smith*," he said, in front of the third mural. It showed a broad man with a hammer. "The… Smith."

In the fifth he found *king*. In the seventh, *salt*.

He stopped in front of the first one for a long time.

It showed a small boy holding a crook. The boy was turning away, out of the picture, his face hidden. The glass all around him was black and cracked, as if something had struck it from the inside. I didn't look at it. I looked at the floor. I am very good at looking at the floor when I need to.

"Is that—" Zayn said.

"Keep reading," I said.

He did.

The ninth mural showed an old man walking away from a dune with his hands pressed over his eyes. Zayn looked at it for a long while, and something moved across his face that he didn't put into words. I didn't help him. I'll tell you about that one later. I promise.

The eleventh was clouded, as if the glass had breathed on itself. You could just make out a figure running. Nothing else.

And then he went back to the beginning and looked at them all again, together. That was when he saw it.

---

It wasn't the words that told him first. It was the pictures.

Not all of them. In the first, the boy with the crook was turning away. In the ninth, the old man was walking away. In the eleventh, whoever it was was running. But in the other eight, the picture was the same.

A dune. A very high dune, so high that its top went up out of the picture into the dark. A full white Ash hanging above it, enormous, filling half the sky. And on the top of that dune, a figure with its arms open, and fire rising up out of it like wings.

Eight times. Eight different people. A woman with long hair. The broad man with his hammer. A boy who looked like he was made of white crystals. A man with a falcon lifting from his wrist. Each one of them on that same dune, under that same Ash, with their arms open, and burning.

Zayn had seen that picture before.

He'd seen it every time he used the Light. He'd seen it in Harrow's End with Bram's little brother in his arms. He'd seen it at the Singing Wells, with a stranger's back pressed against his own. Every time, a little longer and a little clearer. He'd told himself it was a dream, or a warning, or the Light showing off. He'd told himself it was a picture of something that *might* happen.

He went back to the first of the eight, the woman with long hair. His finger found the line of gold letters underneath, and moved along it, letter by letter, past all the words he didn't know. Right to the very end, where there was one short word on its own.

Three letters.

*Ah. Sss. Huh.*

"*Ash*," Zayn whispered.

He went to the next mural, the Smith. The last word was *ash*.

The next. *Ash*.

The next. *Ash*. *Ash*. *Ash*. Eight times, eight murals, the same word at the end of each line. He'd written it ten times in the sand two nights ago by the light of a small fire, and Soren had told him it was an easy one.

He stood in the middle of the great hall of the City of Glass with the night coming down on him through the broken roof, and he understood.

It wasn't a picture of something that *might* happen.

The Phoenix-Born didn't save everyone and go home. They didn't grow old. They weren't given feasts. They climbed the Crown Dune under the full Ash and they opened their arms and they burned, every one of them who'd been brave enough, and the grey went away, and they were *ash*.

*They are remembered*, Hesset had said, in his tent full of herbs and bells, when Zayn asked what happened after.

No bell had rung. It was true, after all.

---

He didn't cry. I'd half expected him to. He'd cried at the Singing Wells, and that was only for a song.

He just sat down on the glass floor, slowly, the way a very old man sits, and pulled his knees up to his chest. His hand was pressed flat over the feather mark. He was very quiet for a long time.

Then he said, without looking at me, "You knew."

"Yes."

My tail glowed. I couldn't have lied about that if I'd wanted to. I'm not even sure I wanted to.

"Hesset knew."

"Yes."

He nodded slowly. He looked up at the eight figures in the glass, burning, burning, burning. In the dark the gold in them shone faintly, like coals under ash.

And then he asked me.

He asked it in a very small voice. It wasn't the voice of a boy who'd fought Husks with a rusty knife, or run into a pack of Hounds to save a stranger. It was the voice of a seven-year-old sitting on the edge of a well, saying his own name over and over so he wouldn't forget it.

"Kiri," he said. "Is there a way I don't have to die?"

---

I want to tell you about that moment properly. I've told you I can't stand liars. That's true. I've spent more years than I can count sitting beside people who lie, to me and to each other and to themselves. I have watched my own tail go dark for them over and over, and every time, I've thought a little less of them.

I have walked beside eleven bearers. I'd never lied to one. Not once.

And I looked at that boy on the glass floor, with his knees pulled up and his hand on his chest, and the gold-lettered word *ash* written eight times around him in the dark. I thought: *He's fifteen. He's only just learned to laugh. He has a friend for the first time in his life, and that friend is somewhere in this city right now looking for water, and he'll come back soon, and they'll eat together and argue about the Embers.*

I thought: *Let him have that. Just for a little while. Let him have a little longer.*

So I said, "Yes."

---

My tail went dark.

Not dim. *Dark*. It went out like a lamp in a gust of wind, all at once, so that the only light left in the hall was the faint gold glow inside the glass.

And somewhere in Zayn's pack, muffled in its cloth, Hesset's little bronze bell gave one small, clear note.

*Ting.*

Zayn was looking at my tail. He'd looked at it before he'd even finished asking. Of course he had. He'd been looking at my tail every time anyone spoke, ever since Harrow's End.

He didn't say anything.

That was the worst part. I'd been ready for anything else. I'd been ready for him to shout at me, or call me a liar, or turn his back. I'd have deserved all of it. But he just looked at my dark tail for a long moment, and then he looked away. He looked up at the eight burning figures in the glass, and he nodded slightly, as if I'd confirmed something.

Which, of course, I had.

"Okay," he said quietly.

That was all.

I've told a great many hard truths in my life. I've told mothers what happened to their sons. I've told kings their cities were going to fall. None of it ever hurt as much as that one small lie, and that one small word he gave me back.

I'll be honest with you now, because I owe somebody the truth about it. I don't know whether I lied to protect him, or to protect myself from watching his face when I said *no*.

Both, probably. People are rarely only one thing. Not even foxes.

---

Soren came back with a full water skin and glass-dust in his hair, talking before he was even through the doorway.

"There's a cistern under the east side, it's full, it's disgusting, there's something living in it but I think it's only a frog. Zayn, you have to see the—"

He stopped.

He looked at Zayn on the floor. He looked at me, with my tail still dark as an old coal. He looked up, slowly, at the eight figures burning in the glass.

"What happened?"

Zayn told him.

He couldn't have done anything else. Lying wasn't open to him. And I think, even if it had been, he wouldn't have lied to Soren. He told him all of it: the dune, the Ash, the word *ash* eight times over, and the vision he'd been having since Harrow's End. He told it in a flat, quiet voice, staring at the floor.

He didn't tell him what I'd said. He left that out. I've never forgotten that he left that out.

When he'd finished, Soren set down the water skin very carefully. Then he walked to the first of the eight murals and began to read it. Properly, out loud, every word. He read it like a prince who'd had six tutors.

"*The Weaver's Daughter*," he read, "*who climbed the Crown Dune under the full Ash and opened her hands, and the grey went away. And of her there remained only*—" His voice caught. "*Ash.*"

He went to the next one. And the next. His voice got quieter and harder with each one. He read all eight. Then he read the first, the ninth and the eleventh. *Refused.* *Refused.* *Refused.*

Then he stood in the middle of the hall with his fists clenched at his sides.

"No," he said.

"Soren—"

"*No*." He swung round. His eyes were too bright in the dark. "Look. Look at them. Three of them said no. Three of them *lived*."

"The first one turned into the Hollow," Zayn said quietly. "Kiri told me. This whole city is what happened when he said no."

"Then he said no *wrong*." Soren's voice rang off the glass. "There's eleven of them, Zayn. Eleven. Eight burned and three ran. That's two ways. Who says there are only two? Who says there isn't a third, or a tenth? Who *decided* that? Some sand? A wall?"

"The Weaver."

"Then the Weaver can explain itself to me in person." He jabbed his broken sword at the open roof, at the dark sky and the swelling Ash. "I mean it. I've already had one person taken away from me because someone decided there was only one way things could go. I'm not standing here and letting it happen again. I'm *not*."

My tail glowed. He meant every word of it. He couldn't have meant it more.

Zayn looked at him for a long time.

And there, on the floor of the City of Glass, something happened that I'll remember until the Embers go out. Zayn smiled. It was small and tired and terribly sad. But it was a real smile.

"Okay," he said. "A third way."

He didn't believe it. I could see that. But he'd seen how much Soren needed him to say it, and he'd said the only true thing he could find. He *would* look for one. That was true. My tail glowed faintly gold for him, the first light it had shown since my lie.

---

They slept in the great hall that night, because it was the only place in the city out of the wind.

Soren lay down with his back to the wall and his broken sword across his chest, facing the doorway. He didn't say much else. Once, in the dark, I heard him say, "Idan would like this place," to nobody. Then he was quiet.

Zayn didn't sleep for a long time.

He sat cross-legged on the glass floor with his pack open in front of him. He took out the broken golden feather Hesset had given him, the one that had been found with him in the sand. He turned it over in his fingers. In the dim light of the murals, it seemed to me that the break across its middle had closed. Only a little. Only by the width of a hair. As if facing one hard truth had mended something in it.

He didn't notice. He was too busy looking at the twelfth place on the wall.

There was one. I'd known there would be. At the end of the long curve, after the clouded figure of the Runner, there was one more stretch of smooth, dark glass. It was empty. It had nothing in it at all.

Not yet.

Zayn looked at it for a long time. Then he reached out with one finger and, in the thin film of glass-dust that lay over everything, he wrote three letters on the floor.

*A. S. H.*

He looked at them. Then he wiped them away with the flat of his hand, lay down beside Soren, and closed his eyes.

I sat in the doorway and kept watch, with my tail glowing faintly in the dark. Above the broken roof, the Ash hung half full now. It was halfway to the dune.

I counted the night. Night fourteen. I wished I'd never learned to count.

---

*End of Chapter Ten*
